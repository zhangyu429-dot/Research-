# 金海通 (Jinhaitong, SH 603061) — Investment Analysis: The Key Questions

*Tianjin Jinhaitong Semiconductor Equipment Co., Ltd. — IC test handlers (测试分选机)*
*Analysis date: 2026-08-11. All figures RMB unless noted.*

**Framing:** the operating business is doing extremely well — three consecutive quarters of triple-digit
profit growth, margin expansion, and a genuine high-end product transition. The market has already
paid for a great deal of that. At ~¥26.8bn the stock is ~65x the 2026 consensus, ~29x 2028 consensus,
and ~38x *trailing 2025 sales*. Nearly every question below is really a sub-question of:
**is this a durable global share-gainer in high-end handlers, or a well-run cyclical whose 2026 numbers
are the top of the range?**

---

## 2. The key questions

### Q1. What exactly is driving the 2026 surge — AI/HPC, autos, or a back-end restocking cycle?

Management attributes H1 2026 to "AI, high-performance computing, new-energy vehicles" plus overseas
order growth. Those are three very different-duration drivers. Back-end (OSAT) capex is among the most
cyclical line items in semis: 2023–24 was a trough, 2025–26 a sharp recovery. A ¥698m company printing
+120% revenue growth off a depressed base is not yet evidence of secular share gain.

**What would answer it:** revenue split by end-application and by customer type (OSAT vs IDM vs design
house) in the H1 2026 report; whether contract liabilities (合同负债) and backlog are still building in
Q3, or whether Q2's strength was delivery catch-up. Cross-check against 长电/通富/华天 capex guidance
and Cohu's handler-segment commentary.

### Q2. Is the EXCEED-9000 / tri-temperature win durable, or a one-cycle product?

This is the actual bull case. The EXCEED-9800 tri-temperature handler (32-site, 64-site in development)
went to volume in 2024 and drove mix: EXCEED-9000 series went from **25.8% of 2024 revenue** to
**51.4% of H1 2025 equipment sales** to a stated **39.0% for FY2025** (the FY figure appears to use a
different denominator than the interim one — worth reconciling in the primary filing).

Tri-temp handling (hot/cold/ambient, typically −55°C to +150°C) is the technically hard,
automotive/industrial-grade end of the market and is where the incumbent import share sits. If
Jinhaitong holds it, gross margin >50% is defensible. If it's a window before 长川科技 and 鸿劲科技
ship equivalent platforms, margins compress.

**What would answer it:** repeat-order rate from the same customers on 9800; whether the 64-site model
ships on schedule; ASP and gross margin *by series*, not blended.

### Q3. Can the overseas story actually scale — and does the market already assume it has?

FY2025 overseas revenue was **¥85.9m, +125.6%, but only ~12.4% of main business**. Domestic was ¥610m
(87.7%). The Malaysia (Penang-area) production/operations center opened in H1 2025, and H1 2026 growth
is explicitly credited partly to overseas orders.

The consensus 2028 revenue of ¥2.57bn (~US$360m) would put Jinhaitong at or near top-3 globally in test
handlers. The global top five — Cohu (Xcerra/MCT), 长川科技, Advantest, 鸿劲科技 (Taiwan), Techwing —
hold roughly 57% of the market. **Getting to consensus 2028 requires winning share from those five in
their home accounts (ASE, Amkor, global IDMs), not just serving Chinese OSATs.**

**What would answer it:** overseas revenue share in H1 2026 (due late August) — is it 12% or 25%?;
overseas gross margin vs domestic; whether any tier-1 global OSAT/IDM has moved past evaluation into
repeat volume orders; Malaysia center utilization; export-control and tariff exposure on a
China-headquartered equipment vendor selling into Taiwan/SE Asia/US-affiliated fabs.

### Q4. How much of the "domestic substitution" runway is already spent?

Sources conflict in a way that matters. Overall Chinese test-equipment localization is cited at **<30%**
(and <5% for high-end memory/SoC testers) — but *handlers specifically* are cited at **~60% localized in
2025**, with one industry source calling handlers "essentially fully substituted."

If handlers are already ~60% localized, the remaining domestic growth is share-shift among Chinese
vendors (mainly against 长川科技, which is a far larger, full-stack platform: testers + handlers + probers
+ AOI), not a soft import-substitution tailwind. That reframes the whole thesis from "policy-driven
substitution" to "competitive share war + export."

**What would answer it:** Jinhaitong's own unit share in Chinese handler tenders over the last four
quarters; whether it is winning against 长川 or only against imports.

### Q5. Customer concentration is rising, not falling — who are they, and what happens when one pauses?

Top-5 customers: **51.0% (2024) → 65.4% (H1 2025) → 58.3% (FY2025)**. Concentration *increasing* during
an upcycle is the wrong direction — it means the growth is coming from a handful of accounts. A single
large OSAT deferring a line pushes a quarter badly for a company this size.

**What would answer it:** named top-5 (disclosed in the annual report), whether the largest is a Chinese
OSAT or an overseas account, and single-customer share.

### Q6. Are the margins operating leverage, or mix that mean-reverts?

Gross margin 47.5% → 52.2% → 53.0% (Q1 2026) with net margin at 29.1%. Some of this is real (tri-temp mix,
scale absorption of fixed cost). But handler makers structurally earn less than ATE makers (华峰测控 has
historically run ~75% GM), and 52%+ on a ~¥700m revenue base is unusually high for the segment.

**What would answer it:** the split between price, mix, and unit-cost; whether Q2/Q3 2026 hold >52% as
volume mix shifts; competitor pricing behavior in 2026 tenders.

### Q7. Is the earnings quality keeping up with the reported growth?

This is the clearest amber flag in the numbers.

- Accounts receivable **+52.4% in FY2025** and **+56.8% YoY at Q1 2026**; AR is reportedly **~328% of profit**.
- Inventory **¥484m at 2026-03-31**, +17.3% vs year-end ¥413m — larger than a full year of 2025 revenue on a cost basis.
- Total asset turnover deteriorating across the last three annual reports: **0.6 → 0.29 → 0.26**.
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow was genuinely strong (**¥102m**, +1602% YoY), but the three-year average
  OCF/current liabilities is only ~15.4%.

Rapid AR and inventory build during an equipment upcycle is normal (you build ahead of shipment, and
customers pay on acceptance). It is also exactly what a channel-stuffed or acceptance-delayed revenue
line looks like. The two are only distinguishable over time.

**What would answer it:** AR ageing buckets and bad-debt provisioning policy; inventory composition
(raw materials vs 发出商品 goods shipped pending acceptance — the latter is the tell); whether FY2026
OCF converts at anything close to net profit.

### Q8. The ¥850m convertible bond — is capacity the binding constraint, and what is the return on it?

The company plans up to **¥850m** of convertibles (plan disclosed 2026-05-06, shareholder meeting
2026-05-21) for a semiconductor advanced-equipment intelligent manufacturing centre (a subsidiary
acquired 21,000 m² in Shanghai), R&D, and working capital.

¥850m against a book equity of roughly ¥1.7bn (implied by 15.9x P/B) is a big raise. At current total
asset turnover of 0.26x, an ¥850m asset base supports only ~¥220m of incremental revenue unless turns
improve materially.

**What would answer it:** current capacity utilization (产能利用率) and lead times — if utilization is
<85%, the raise is strategic rather than demand-forced; the project's stated incremental capacity, IRR
and payback in the feasibility report; the conversion price and dilution at various share prices.

### Q9. Who is selling, and how much overhang is left?

- **2026-03-03: 18.17m shares (30.29% of then-outstanding capital) came off IPO lock-up** — the 36-month
  founder/pre-IPO tranche (崔学峰 8.51m, 龙波 5.34m, 南通华泓 3.96m, 天津博芯 0.36m).
- Second-largest holder **宁波旭诺** sold 598,100 shares for ~¥156m (Oct 2025–Jan 2026), then announced a
  further plan to sell up to 1.80m shares (3.00%) between 2026-04-30 and 2026-07-29 (~¥517m at then-prices),
  and executed reductions around 2026-07-22.
- 2026-07-24: five block trades at ¥268.70, an **8.79% discount** to market.
- The company itself has issued repeated **abnormal-volatility notices** (2026-07-01 after +20% in three
  days, 2026-07-20 after −20% in three days, 2026-08-07 after another +20% in three days) and has
  explicitly warned that its valuation is high relative to the industry.

None of this is a fundamental judgement, but a newly-freed 30% float plus a demonstrably price-insensitive
seller plus 8.8%-discounted blocks is a meaningful supply/technical factor at 115x TTM earnings.

### Q10. What does the current price actually require, and is that achievable?

Reverse the multiple rather than debating it:

- ¥26.8bn market cap on ¥177m of 2025 profit = **151x**; on ¥414m of 2026 consensus = **~65x**;
  on ¥921m of 2028 consensus = **~29x**.
- Consensus requires revenue to go **from ¥698m (2025) to ¥2.57bn (2028)** — a 3.7x in three years,
  with net margin expanding from 25% to 36%.
- To justify even 30x on 2028, essentially all of that must land: the 64-site tri-temp platform ships,
  overseas goes from 12% to a third or more of revenue, gross margin holds >50% through a competitive
  response, and the back-end capex cycle does not roll over before 2029.

**The single most useful thing to do is not to forecast, but to write down the 2028 revenue by
customer/geography that the price implies and ask whether those specific sockets exist.** ¥2.57bn of
handler revenue is a global top-3 position. That is possible — it is not the base case that a 29x
"cheap on out-year" framing implies.

### Q11. Where are we in the cycle, and what is normalized earnings?

Handlers are late-cycle back-end equipment: order books can go from record to empty in two quarters.
2023 (IPO year) into 2024 was a trough for this company. If ¥414m is the 2026 peak-cycle number and
mid-cycle is ¥250–300m, the stock is on 90–110x mid-cycle. **A view on this stock is mostly a view on
whether the AI-driven back-end build is a multi-year plateau or a 2025–27 pulse.**

### Q12. Governance and disclosure — minor, but worth noting

FY2025 dividend was **¥0.38/share, ¥22.8m total — a 12.9% payout**, alongside the 10-for-4.5 bonus share
issue. The bonus issue creates no value; combined with a low payout and an ¥850m raise, capital
allocation is unambiguously growth-first. Reasonable for the situation, but it means shareholders are
underwriting reinvestment at a 15.9x book multiple.

## 3. What would make this a buy / what would break it

**Bull case confirmation (watch for, in order of informativeness)**
1. H1 2026 report (due late Aug 2026): overseas revenue share materially above 12%, with gross margin
   intact.
2. Contract liabilities / backlog still building through Q3 2026 rather than being drawn down.
3. A named tier-1 non-Chinese OSAT or IDM in repeat volume orders.
4. 64-site tri-temp platform shipping on schedule.
5. FY2026 operating cash flow converting near net profit despite the AR build.

**Thesis breaks**
1. Top-5 concentration rising above ~65% with a single customer >25%.
2. Gross margin dropping below ~48% (competitive response from 长川 / 鸿劲 / Cohu).
3. AR days and 发出商品 inventory continuing to outgrow revenue into H2 2026.
4. Any quarter of sequential order decline — at 65x forward, multiple compression does the damage,
   not the earnings miss.
5. Continued insider selling at discounts into strength.

---

## 4. Bottom line

The business quality question and the stock question have different answers right now.

**The business** looks genuinely good: a real technology transition into tri-temperature and
multi-site handlers, margins above the industry, a credible early overseas footprint, and a demand
environment that is working strongly in its favour.

**The stock** at ~115x trailing / ~65x 2026 consensus prices in a global top-3 outcome by 2028, against
a company that today does ¥698m of revenue with 88% of it in China and 58% of it from five customers,
in a domestic segment that is already ~60% localized. It also carries a freshly unlocked 30% float, an
active discount seller, and a pending ¥850m dilutive raise.

**The questions that most change the answer, in priority order: (1) overseas share and whether it can
reach a third of revenue (Q3); (2) earnings quality — AR, 发出商品 inventory, cash conversion (Q7);
(3) whether tri-temp is a durable moat or a window (Q2/Q6); (4) where in the cycle 2026 sits (Q11).**
The H1 2026 report in late August answers meaningful parts of (1), (2) and (3) at once, and is the
natural next checkpoint.
