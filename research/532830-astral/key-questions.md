# Astral Ltd (ASTRA IN · NSE: ASTRAL · BSE: 532830) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 14 September 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Astral is **a very good pipes business that spent five years and ₹1,400 crore of capex buying
its way into adjacent categories, produced no earnings growth doing it, and has now announced
it is splitting those adjacencies back out.** The core is CPVC and PVC plumbing — 71% of
revenue, effectively all of the profit, a ~19–20% segment EBITDA margin, and a distribution
network (3,610+ distributors, ~251,000 dealers) that is the real moat. Bolted on are adhesives
(India and UK), paints, bathware and water tanks — 29% of revenue at roughly 8% EBITDA margin,
with paints only reaching breakeven in the June 2026 quarter after two years of losses. On
**25 June 2026 the board approved a composite scheme** to demerge the chemicals business into
a separately listed **Astral Chemie Limited** on a **1:1 entitlement**, and to amalgamate
Al-Aziz Plastics into Astral. **The stock fell 9–11% on the news.** Any view on this security
is therefore two views: what a pure-play pipes company is worth, and what a sub-scale
paints-and-adhesives company is worth — because within roughly twelve months the market will
price them separately instead of applying one blended multiple to both.

> **Two years of zero progress, reported as a 52% jump, because the base was broken.** The
> stock is at 75x. Management's answer is to split the company in two.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. After the demerger, what multiple does each half deserve — and does the sum still reach ₹40,000cr?

This outranks everything else because it is **mechanical, dated, and already in motion.** Today
one blended 75x multiple is applied to a company in which the two halves have completely
different economics. Within roughly twelve months the market must name two multiples instead of
one. Run the arithmetic on FY26 actuals:

| | Revenue (FY26) | Share | EBITDA *(derived)* | Share | Implied PAT *(derived, pro-rata)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plumbing** | ~₹4,679cr | 71.2% | **~₹912cr** @ ~19.5% | **85.9%** | **~₹459cr** |
| **Paints & Adhesives** | ₹1,890cr | 28.8% | **~₹150cr** @ ~7.9% | 14.1% | **~₹76cr** |
| Consolidated | ₹6,569cr | | ₹1,062cr @ 16.2% | | ₹535cr |

*EBITDA is split using Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 disclosed segment margins and calibrated to reproduce
the consolidated total exactly; PAT is allocated pro-rata to EBITDA. Both are my derivations,
not disclosure. The company has never published segment PAT, which is itself the point.*

Now solve for what each half must carry:

- **If Astral Chemie is valued at 50x its ~₹76cr (≈₹3,800cr), the pipes business alone must be
  worth ~₹36,300cr — which is 79x its ~₹459cr.** That is *above* today's blended 75x, for the
  slower-growing, more commoditised half.
- **If the pipes business gets 45x — roughly where Supreme Industries trades, and generous for a
  pipe maker — that is ~₹20,700cr, and Astral Chemie would have to be worth ~₹19,400cr: about
  256x earnings, or 10x sales, on a business running an 8% EBITDA margin.**

Neither is comfortable. The honest version of the bull case is that you are not meant to
capitalise FY26 chemicals earnings at all: management targets **₹2,300–2,400cr of revenue and a
14–15% EBITDA margin by FY28** for Astral Chemie, scaling to **₹4,500–5,000cr in four to five
years**. At ₹2,350cr and 14.5% that is ~₹341cr of EBITDA in FY28; at 30x EV/EBITDA, ~₹10,200cr.
Which still leaves the pipes business needing ~₹29,900cr — **65x its FY26 profit.**

**And there is a disclosure hole sitting in the middle of this.** The demerged "Chemicals
Business Undertaking" is stated at **₹1,266.3cr, 21% of *standalone* turnover**. The
consolidated Paints & Adhesives segment is **₹1,890cr**. The ~₹620cr gap is the UK adhesives
business (Seal IT / Silicone Engineering — Q1 FY27 revenue ₹121cr, annualising near ₹480cr) plus
other subsidiaries. **Nobody outside the company currently knows whether the UK business, its
4.9% EBITDA margin and its turnaround, travels to Astral Chemie or stays with Astral.** That
single fact changes Astral Chemie's revenue base by roughly 40% and its margin materially.

**Ask:**
- **The scheme document's Appointed Date, and the audited financials of the demerged
  undertaking** — revenue, EBITDA, PAT, net assets and capital employed for FY25 and FY26. This
  is the one disclosure that settles the entire sum-of-the-parts question.
- **Does the UK adhesives business go with Astral Chemie or stay?** Same question for bathware
  and water tanks, currently reported inside Plumbing.
- **What debt, cash and corporate overhead transfer to Astral Chemie?** A newly listed company
  with paints at breakeven and a growth plan needs funding; if it leaves with no cash it will
  raise equity, and that dilution is part of what you are buying today.
- **The residual Astral's own capital allocation policy post-split** — if plumbing was the piggy
  bank for paints capex and A&P, what happens to that cash now: dividends, buyback, or the next
  adjacency?

### Q2. Is the plumbing margin expansion a PVC price cycle or a structural improvement?

Plumbing segment EBITDA margin went **16.4% → 18.9%** in Q1 FY27, and **20.4% → 22.8%** in
Q4 FY26. This is the number carrying the whole earnings recovery. It is also the number most
likely to be misread, because in Indian plastic pipes **the margin is largely a derivative of
which way PVC resin prices moved last quarter, not of how well the company traded.**

The evidence that this is cycle, not franchise:

1. **Q1 FY27 plumbing volumes were flat at 56,146 MT while segment revenue rose 10.1%.** All of
   the growth was realisation. Realisation is resin price.
2. **PVC resin prices rose ~64% YoY into March 2026**, then fell through Q1 FY27. Management
   stated there was **no inventory loss in Q1 FY27 despite the PVC price decline** — which is
   another way of saying the quarter's margin was helped by the timing of resin flowing through
   cost of goods sold.
3. The Q4 FY26 blowout (+24.2% volume, 22.8% margin) was explicitly attributed to **channel
   restocking** after a destocking year — the mirror image of the Q1 FY26 collapse.
4. **Anti-dumping duty on PVC suspension resin is pending**, recommended at **$22–284/tonne**,
   alongside a **US$766/tonne minimum import price** and quantitative restrictions after the
   nil-BCD window of 2 April – 30 June 2026. Management said on the August call that the process
   was "underway with government, timeline 1–2 months." **If it lands, the industry books
   inventory gains and the sell-side calls it margin expansion.** It is neither durable nor
   Astral-specific.

The evidence that some of it is structural, and it is real:

- **The captive CPVC resin plant.** Astral is the first pipe company anywhere to backward
  integrate into CPVC resin: 40,000 MT via Nexelon Chem (80% owned, ₹120cr of a ₹150cr project),
  enough to cover **50–60% of internal CPVC requirement**, claimed to add **~200bps to piping
  margins** and free **₹120–130cr of working capital**. Astral terminated its Lubrizol sourcing
  and licence arrangement years ago, so this closes a genuine strategic dependency.
- Mix: bathware, water tanks and valves are growing faster than pipes and carry different
  economics.

**Ask:**
- **Decompose the FY26 and Q1 FY27 plumbing margin into volume leverage, realisation/resin
  timing, and mix** — in the company's own numbers, not a narrative. Resin timing reverses.
- **What was the inventory gain or loss in each of the last six quarters, in ₹cr?** Indian pipe
  companies quantify this on calls; it is the single cleanest way to strip the cycle out.
- **What plumbing EBITDA margin does management underwrite at flat PVC prices?** The FY27
  guidance of 16–18% consolidated is below the 18.9% just printed — **management's own guidance
  implies the Q1 margin does not hold.** Make them say why.
- **The CPVC plant's slipped timeline** — originally Q2 FY27, now trials from December 2026 and
  commercial production in Q4 FY27. What is the commissioning risk, and when does the +200bps
  actually appear in reported numbers: FY28, or later?

### Q3. Is the FY27 volume guidance of 10–15% credible when the first quarter delivered zero?

Volume is the only honest measure of whether the franchise is winning, because value growth is
contaminated by resin prices. The record is volatile and the guidance has been moved repeatedly:

| Period | Plumbing volume | Note |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 full year | **+16%** | Guidance raised mid-year from "low double digits" to ">14%" |
| Q3 FY26 | +17% | Described by the sell-side as market-share gain |
| **Q4 FY26** | **+24.2% to 84,041 MT** | Aided by **channel restocking**; January alone >20% |
| **Q1 FY27** | **flat, 56,146 MT** | Against an industry estimated **down ~10%** |
| July 2026 | **+40%** | Off a collapsed July 2025 base |

Two readings fit, and they are worth very different multiples. **The benign one:** Astral held
volume flat while the industry fell 10%, which is share gain; July's +40% shows the destocking
is over; the full year still lands in the 10–15% band. **The unkind one:** FY26's 16% was
restocking after a lost FY25, Q1 FY27 gave it back, and the underlying run rate of this business
is high-single-digit volume growth in a market growing 12–14%.

The base rate is not encouraging. **FY26 volume grew 16% and net profit grew 3.0%.** Whatever
volume does, it has recently not been converting into earnings.

**Sub-questions:**
- **Monthly volume for August and September 2026**, against July's +40%. One month off a broken
  base is not a trend; management itself flagged sustainability as the key monitorable.
- **Primary versus secondary sales.** Volume shipped to distributors is not volume consumed. Ask
  for channel inventory in weeks, now versus a year ago — this is the variable that produced both
  the Q4 FY26 spike and the Q1 FY27 flat print.
- **Volume by product** — CPVC (where share is 25–50% and pricing holds) versus PVC (where share
  is ~5% and the fight is with everybody). Growth mixing toward PVC is growth at a worse margin.
- **Capacity utilisation.** FY26 plumbing volume of roughly **270,000 MT** *(derived)* against
  **420,000 TPA** of pipes/tanks/bathware capacity is about **64%**. Confirm the real figure and
  ask what utilisation the FY27 capex of ₹300–350cr is being added into.

### Q4. Did the diversification create value — and is the demerger an admission that it did not?

Astral has been a serial acquirer of adjacencies: Resinova and Seal IT (adhesives, 2014), Rex
Polyextrusion (2018), Gem Paints and Silicone Engineering (2022), the decorative paints launch
(2024), **Al-Aziz Plastics** (FY26, ₹37cr revenue, now being amalgamated), **Nexelon Chem** (80%,
Nov 2025), **DSS LLP** (60% for ₹39.11cr, June 2026), plus stake-ups to 100% in Seal IT and
Astral Coatings. Against **₹1,400cr of capex over four years**, the scoreboard:

| | FY22 | FY26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ₹4,394cr | ₹6,569cr | **+49.5%** |
| Net profit | ₹490cr | ₹535cr | **+9.1%** |
| Net margin | 11.2% | 8.1% | **−310bps** |
| EBITDA margin | ~17% | 16.2% | −80bps |
| RoE *(derived)* | ~22% | **~14.5%** | **−750bps** |

**Half the revenue was bought or built; almost none of the profit was.** The EBITDA margin
decline is modest — the operating businesses are fine. What broke the net line is **depreciation
on the capex and losses in the new ventures**, and that is a capital allocation outcome, not an
operating one.

The specific case that deserves the sharpest question is **paints**. Astral entered decorative
paints **in 2024 — the same year Aditya Birla's Birla Opus launched with more than ₹10,000cr of
committed capacity**, 30,000+ retail outlets and aggressive trade spend, into a market where
Asian Paints was already losing share and cutting margin to defend it. Astral entered that fight
with **36,000 TPA of capacity** and reached **EBITDA breakeven only in Q1 FY27, at a 0.1%
margin.** Bathware is also still at breakeven. Adhesives India — the one genuinely good piece of
the chemicals arm, ~68% of the demerged revenue with 15–16% margins historically — **fell to
12.2% from 14.0%** in Q1 FY27 on high-cost inventory.

In fairness, and it matters: **the balance sheet was never risked to do any of this.** Net cash
of **₹943.3cr** at March 2026 (up from ~₹608cr a year earlier), gearing 0.15x, interest cover
23x, **FY26 operating cash flow of ~₹630cr against ₹535cr of profit (118% conversion)**, and
working capital **cut from 17.4 days to 11.0 days**. This is not a company that financed growth
with receivables. It financed it with its own cash — which is precisely why the returns question,
rather than the solvency question, is the one that matters.

**Ask:**
- **The post-tax return on the ₹1,400cr of capex**, project by project: paints, bathware, water
  tanks, the UK, Nexelon. Which have cleared cost of capital, and which have not yet?
- **Cumulative losses in the paints business since launch**, and the revenue and utilisation at
  which it reaches a double-digit EBITDA margin. Management targets "high single to low double
  digit" by FY28 — what volume does that assume?
- **Why demerge now, rather than three years ago or three years hence?** The most valuable
  answer is the honest one: whether this is a focus decision or a funding decision.
- **FY27 capex of ₹300–350cr was guided, but Q1 alone spent ₹137cr** (₹87cr plumbing, ₹32cr
  adhesives India, ₹2cr UK, ₹16cr CPVC). Annualised that is ~₹550cr. Which is the real number?

### Q5. What has to be true for ₹40,100cr to be cheap?

Reverse the valuation rather than forecasting into it. Take the market cap, apply a terminal
multiple, and solve for the profit required:

| Exit P/E | Net profit required | × FY26 (₹535cr) | vs sell-side FY28E (₹836–950cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30x | ₹1,337cr | **2.50x** | 41–60% above consensus |
| 35x | ₹1,146cr | **2.14x** | 21–37% above consensus |
| 40x | ₹1,003cr | 1.88x | 6–20% above consensus |
| **45x** | **₹891cr** | **1.67x** | **inside the consensus range** |
| 50x | ₹802cr | 1.50x | below consensus |

**So today's price is approximately the full sell-side FY28 estimate, capitalised at ~45x,
discounted at zero.** 45x is what Supreme Industries — the larger, more diversified, better-known
compounder — carries. Astral would have to hold that multiple two years out, *after* handing a
quarter of its revenue to a separately listed company, for today's buyer to earn nothing but the
passage of time.

Three checks make this less comfortable rather than more:

> **1. The sell-side's own targets say the same thing.** Across 25 recent reports the **average
> target price is ₹1,543** against ₹1,493.50 — **+3.3%**, plus a 0.26% dividend yield. The bulls
> are not arguing the stock is cheap; they are arguing it is roughly fairly priced. Motilal
> Oswal's ₹1,697 rests on **~48x FY28E**; Axis's ₹1,750 on **51x FY28E**. **Every published
> target on this name is an exit-multiple assumption, not an earnings argument.**

> **2. The targets have been marked down while the price went nowhere.** Motilal Oswal's target
> ran **₹1,950 → ₹1,710 (June 2026, 52x FY28E) → ₹1,697 (August 2026, 48x FY28E)** — cut ~13%,
> and the multiple assumption cut with it, even as the company reported a +52% quarter. The
> estimates are following the price, not leading it.

> **3. The de-rating is not finished, and it has been paid for by the multiple, not by earnings.**
> At the **all-time high of ₹2,454 (2 July 2024)**, the stock was on **~121x** FY24 EPS of
> ₹20.33. It is now ~75x FY26 EPS of ₹19.91. **The 39% fall from the peak was entirely multiple
> compression on flat earnings.** Nothing in the record establishes that 75x is the floor rather
> than a waypoint — and the demerger is precisely the event that forces the market to re-examine
> the number.

**The question is not whether this is a good business — the plumbing franchise plainly is. It is
whether a company that has delivered 3% profit growth over three years, is about to give away a
quarter of its revenue, and whose own analysts see 3% upside, should be capitalised at 75x.**

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — There is no customer concentration; there is channel concentration, and it is the reason
the numbers swing.** Astral sells through **3,610+ distributors and roughly 251,000 dealers**, so
no single buyer matters — a genuinely better position than most industrials. But that same
channel is a leveraged inventory position on PVC prices held by thousands of small traders. When
resin falls they stop buying and wait; when it rises they buy ahead. That mechanism, not
end-demand, produced the **Q1 FY26 collapse, the Q4 FY26 +24.2% spike, the Q1 FY27 flat print and
July's +40%** — four consecutive quarters whose headline growth told you almost nothing about
consumption. **Ask for channel inventory in weeks across the last eight quarters**, and for the
split of volume between primary (to distributor) and secondary (to dealer/site). Without it,
every quarterly volume number in this file is uninterpretable.

**Q7 — Where in the cycle is FY27, and what is normalised earnings?** Two datable cycles now
exist: the FY21 profitability peak (12.9% net margin, 20.3% EBITDA margin) and the FY24 earnings
peak (₹546cr, ₹20.33 EPS), with FY23 and FY25 as troughs. **FY26's ₹535cr has still not matched
FY24.** The industry backdrop is genuinely good — India's plastic pipes market is projected to
grow ~14% a year, from ₹541bn in FY24 to ₹805bn by FY27 — but Astral's participation in that
growth has so far shown up in revenue and not in profit. If ₹535cr is a mid-cycle number and FY27
is a resin-assisted up-year, the stock is on 75x mid-cycle. If FY26 was the trough of a
margin cycle that the CPVC plant and anti-dumping duties now lift, it is on ~45x FY28. **Make
management state which of those two they believe, in a number.**

**Q8 — The balance sheet is a strength, and the funding question has moved to the spin-off.**
Net cash ₹943.3cr, gearing 0.15x at FY25, interest cover 23x, OCF/PAT 118%, working capital 11.0
days. Nothing here is stressed. The open funding question is **Astral Chemie**: a company being
launched with roughly ₹1,266–1,890cr of revenue at an ~8% EBITDA margin, a paints business at
breakeven, a target of ₹4,500–5,000cr of revenue in four to five years, and no disclosed opening
balance sheet. **Growth of that order is not self-funded from ₹150cr of EBITDA.** Ask what cash
and debt travel with it, what its first-year capex plan is, and whether an equity raise is
contemplated within 24 months of listing. Note also the rating agency's stated trigger: a large
debt-funded capex or acquisition taking TOL/TNW above 0.75x would be a negative rating action —
which constrains the parent, not the spin-off.

**Q9 — Governance: a founder-run family company with clean mechanics and no independent check on
strategy.** Promoters hold **54.22%** with **zero pledge** — better than most Indian mid-caps,
and the absence of pledging removes a whole category of risk. But **Sandeep Engineer is Chairman
*and* Managing Director**, his wife **Jagruti** is a Whole-Time Director, his son **Kairav** is a
Whole-Time Director running pipes, bathware, water tanks and brand, and the **CFO was elevated to
the board**. Succession is structured through family trusts. There is no mechanism by which
minority holders influence the decision to enter paints, to buy five companies in two years, or
to demerge. That is not an allegation of anything — it is a statement that **the entire capital
allocation record in Q4 is one family's judgement, and you are underwriting the next decade of it
at 75x.** Ask what the independent directors' process was on the demerger, and who valued the
demerged undertaking.

**Q10 — Share supply: quantifiable, and the informed holders have been sellers.** Three live
items. (a) In **December 2023 two promoter entities — Kairav Chemicals and Saumya Polymers LLP —
sold 1.74% of the company for ₹885cr** in the open market, near the top of the de-rating. That is
not a governance failure, but it is information about what informed holders thought of the price.
(b) An **ESOP 2025 scheme** exists; its grant volume, exercise price and performance conditions
could not be obtained and should be. (c) **The demerger itself creates a mechanical seller.**
Astral sits in index products; **Astral Chemie will list into those portfolios without being in
any index**, forcing passive and mandate-constrained holders to sell it — the standard
post-demerger technical overhang, and the reason spin-offs routinely trade poorly for their first
weeks. If you like Astral Chemie, that flow is an opportunity; if you own Astral for the blend,
it is a cost.

**Q11 — Competition is intensifying in all three businesses at once, and the one durable edge has
a clock on it.** In **pipes**: Supreme Industries is the diversified leader, Finolex leads
revenue in organised PVC, Ashirvad (Aliaxis) is the other premium brand, and **Prince Pipes has
taken installed capacity to 435,222 MT — larger than Astral's 420,000 TPA pipes/tanks/bathware
base — while growing 20–25%.** In **adhesives**: Pidilite, at many times the scale and a
materially higher margin. In **paints**: Asian Paints and **Birla Opus**, against which Astral's
36,000 TPA is a rounding error. And the CPVC integration edge — genuinely first-in-industry — is
time-limited: **DCW, Lubrizol and Epigral have launched or proposed Indian CPVC resin capacity
totalling over ₹2,400cr of investment.** Once domestic CPVC resin is abundant, "we make our own"
becomes "so does everyone, and it is cheaper to buy." **Ask how long management expects the
captive cost advantage to persist, in years and in ₹/kg.**

**Q12 — Capital return is a rounding error and the demerger does not change that.** Dividend is
roughly **₹4/share for FY26** (₹2.50 final plus interim) against ₹19.91 of EPS — a **~20% payout**
and a **0.26% yield**. That is a defensible growth-first policy, and with net cash and 118% cash
conversion the company can afford both. But it does mean **the entire return case is capital
appreciation from a 75x starting multiple**, with no yield support on the way. If the residual
Astral is a cash-generative pipes company with a completed capex cycle and no paints business to
fund, the payout policy is the clearest signal management can send about what it thinks the
business now is. **Ask whether the post-demerger dividend or buyback policy has been decided.**

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **Q2 FY27 results (late October / early November 2026) — the nearest and highest-value event.**
   It answers Q2 and Q3 at once. Look for: **plumbing volume growth** (did July's +40% survive
   August and September?); **plumbing EBITDA margin against Q1's 18.9%**; any **inventory gain or
   loss** disclosure; whether **paints** held above breakeven; whether **adhesives India**
   recovered from 12.2% toward its historical 15–16%; and the **capex run rate** against the
   ₹300–350cr full-year guide after ₹137cr in Q1.
2. **The PVC anti-dumping duty decision (September–October 2026).** Management said in August the
   process was "1–2 months" away. If duties land at the recommended **$22–284/tonne**, expect
   resin prices to firm and Q3 FY27 to carry **inventory gains that will be reported as margin
   expansion**. Score it as cycle, not franchise.
3. **The scheme document filed with SEBI and the exchanges — the single most informative
   disclosure pending.** It should contain the **audited financials of the demerged undertaking**,
   the **Appointed Date**, the **opening balance sheet of Astral Chemie** (cash, debt, capital
   employed), and — decisively — **whether the UK adhesives business travels with it.** Q1 cannot
   be answered without this.
4. **The NCLT Ahmedabad convening order, then the shareholder and creditor meetings, then SEBI and
   exchange observation letters, then the record date.** A scheme approved by the board on
   **25 June 2026** and guided to complete **by end-FY27** has a visible milestone roughly every
   six to eight weeks. **Silence past December 2026 is the signal**, not the completion.
5. **The CPVC resin plant: mechanical completion December 2026, trials, commercial production in
   Q4 FY27.** It has already slipped once from "Q2 FY27." A second slip pushes the claimed
   **+200bps** of piping margin out of FY28 entirely — and that 200bps is a large fraction of the
   margin expansion embedded in consensus.
6. **Whether FY27 lands inside the 10–15% volume guidance.** Q1 delivered **zero**, so Q2–Q4 must
   now average roughly **13–20%** to hit the band. By the Q3 FY27 print this is arithmetic, not
   opinion.
7. **Paints margin in Q2 and Q3 FY27.** Breakeven at 0.1% is not profitability. Management targets
   "high single to low double digit" by FY28; the path has to start appearing now.
8. **Astral Chemie's listing and its first month of trading.** This is Q1's sum-of-the-parts test
   settled by the market rather than by argument — and the passive-selling overhang means the
   first print will understate it. Watch where it stabilises, not where it opens.
9. **Q3 FY27 (late January 2027)** — the first clean read on whether FY27 profit is tracking the
   ~₹670cr that the consensus FY26–28 growth path implies, or the ~₹535–580cr that flat volumes
   and a normalising resin price would produce.
10. **Any seventh acquisition.** Six in roughly two years. One announced *after* a demerger
    justified by "sharper business focus" would tell you the focus rationale was presentational.
11. **ESOP 2025 grant terms and any performance conditions**, in the FY27 annual report. If they
    carry profit targets, that is management's own committed forecast and worth more than any
    sell-side estimate.

## 4. Provisional read

**The business is good, and the parts of it that are good are not in doubt.** A premium plumbing
brand with 25–30% of the CPVC market, 3,610 distributors and 251,000 dealers, net cash of
₹943cr, 118% cash conversion, working capital cut to 11 days, and the first captive CPVC resin
plant owned by any pipe maker anywhere — in a market growing ~14% a year toward ₹805bn by FY27.
None of that is fragile, and none of it was bought with debt. Management held volume flat in a
quarter when the industry fell 10%. The demerger is defensible on its own terms: paints and
adhesives were sub-scale inside a pipes company, and a separate listing gives them a currency and
a focus they did not have.

**The stock is a different question, and the answer is harder.** You are paying **75x** for a
company whose EPS was ₹20.33 in FY24 and ₹19.91 in FY26; whose celebrated "+52%" quarter produced
₹120.2cr against ₹118.2cr two years earlier; whose net margin has fallen from 12.9% to 8.1% while
revenue doubled; whose ₹1,400cr of capex bought 49.5% more revenue and 9.1% more profit; and
whose own analysts, across 25 reports, see **3.3% upside**. The 39% fall from the July 2024 peak
was **entirely multiple compression on flat earnings** — 121x to 75x — and nothing establishes
that 75x is a floor.

**The demerger is the specific reason the multiple question comes due now rather than eventually.**
Today one 75x multiple covers both halves. Afterwards, the market names two. On FY26 numbers the
arithmetic is unforgiving: if Astral Chemie is capitalised at 50x its ~₹76cr of implied profit,
**the pipes business must be worth 79x its own** — more than the blend it currently enjoys. The
only way the sum holds is if Astral Chemie is valued on its FY28 ambition (₹2,300–2,400cr of
revenue at 14–15% margins) rather than its FY26 reality (₹1,266–1,890cr at ~8%). **That is a
legitimate argument. It is also, precisely, the argument that the scheme document will either
support or destroy** — and nobody outside the company has seen it.

**What would flip the conclusion is narrow and specific, and it is three things together:**
(i) **the scheme document showing Astral Chemie materially larger and better than the ₹1,266cr /
8% version** — the UK business travelling with it, paints scaling, a funded opening balance sheet;
(ii) **two consecutive quarters of double-digit plumbing volume growth with segment margin holding
at or above 19% while PVC prices are flat** — which would establish the margin as franchise rather
than resin timing; and (iii) **the CPVC plant commissioning on the December 2026 / Q4 FY27
schedule with the +200bps visible.** All three, and ~45x FY28 is defensible. Any one alone is not,
because each is separately embedded in consensus already.

**What would confirm the other reading** is equally specific: Q2 FY27 volume growth back below 5%,
plumbing margin reverting toward 16–17% as resin normalises, and a scheme document that confirms
Astral Chemie is the small, 8%-margin version.

**Do not size a position before the scheme document and the Q2 FY27 result.** Both are due within
roughly eight weeks, and between them they settle Q1, Q2 and Q3 — three of the five questions that
decide this investment. At 75x, the cost of waiting is small and the cost of being wrong on the
margin question is a de-rating rather than a miss.
