# 柏诚股份 (Baicheng, 601133.SH) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 30 August 2026 (revised against the 1H26 interim report) · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Baicheng is **a cleanroom engineering contractor earning a 3%–5% net margin, sitting inside a
genuine semiconductor fab-building cycle, priced by the market like a high-margin technology
manufacturer**. It makes no product. It delivers design–procurement–manufacture–construction (EPFC)
cleanroom systems to wafer fabs, display plants and pharmaceutical plants; revenue is recognised on
project progress, profit is the spread between the bid price and execution cost, and cash is set by
the owner's acceptance and settlement schedule. Founded in 1994, it has completed over 800
mid-to-high-end cleanroom projects and listed on the SSE main board on 10 April 2023.

**The 1H26 interim report puts the central tension on the table for the first time: the order
conversion really happened, and the gross margin fell at the same time.**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. The first report card on ¥4.3bn of orders is in: revenue +29%, gross margin −1.8pp

The previous version of this memo named 3Q26 as the first test of order conversion. **Q2 answered
early, and the answer has two faces.**

**On orders, the interim confirms an outcome better than expected:**

| Period | New contracts (ex-VAT) | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 | ¥3,762m | ¥314m |
| 1 Jan – 28 Apr 2026 | ¥4,325m | c.¥1,110m |
| 1H26 total | **¥5,833m (an all-time high)** | c.¥970m |
| of which 29 Apr – 30 Jun (derived) | ¥1,508m | c.¥730m |
| 1 Jul – 25 Aug 2026 | ¥1,034m | c.¥565m |

**Backlog rose from ¥2,448m at end-2025 to ¥5,629m at 30 June 2026, +222.15% year on year** — 2.12x
first-half revenue, against 0.59x of full-year revenue at end-2025. That is a real, order-of-
magnitude improvement and should not be argued away.

**Two things sit alongside it:**

1. **The signing pace is decelerating** — from c.¥1,110m a month (Jan–Apr) to c.¥730m (May–Jun) to
   c.¥565m (1 Jul–25 Aug). Still far above 2025's ¥314m, but "January–April was a catch-up" now has
   data behind it. **¥8–9bn for the full year is the reasonable extrapolation, not ¥12bn.**
2. **The margin on the conversion is falling.** Q2 revenue of ¥1,792m (+29.2%) came at a gross margin
   of about 10.14% — **below Q1's 11.94%, below FY2025's 11.93%, and only 0.14pp above the all-time
   low of 10.00% set in 2024.**

So the question shifts from "can the orders convert" to **"at what price do they convert"**. The
interim reports a blended gross margin of 10.72%, up 0.58pp year on year — **the year-on-year
comparison is true, but the sequence is what matters: 11.94% in Q1, 10.14% in Q2, with the margin
dropping 1.8 points in the quarter the volume arrived.**

**Ask:**
- **First-half gross margin by business** — cleanroom integration and process systems versus
  secondary fit-out, and domestic versus overseas. The interim gives the revenue split but not the
  margins. **That one disclosure would largely settle the whole judgement.**
- **Was the Q2 margin drop mix (large primary-system projects executing at once) or bid pricing?**
  The first is temporary; the second is terminal.
- **The bid gross margin on the ¥5,833m signed in 1H26**, against the 2023–24 vintage. The hardest
  number to obtain and the most valuable.

### Q2. Reverse the valuation: H2 must earn 3.3x H1 just to reach consensus

Start with the simplest arithmetic. First-half net profit was **¥86.0m**; the sell-side mean for
FY2026 is **¥371m**. **That requires ¥285m in the second half — 3.31x the first.**

Historical second-half to first-half ratios:

| | 1H | FY | H2/H1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | ¥99.5m | ¥212m | **1.13x** |
| 2025 | ¥83.5m | ¥205m | **1.46x** |
| 2026 (to reach the ¥371m consensus) | ¥86.0m | ¥371m | **3.31x** |

On 2025's seasonality, FY2026 lands near **¥212m (+3%)**; on 2024's, near **¥183m**. Even assuming
the backlog releases hard and revenue returns to the 2024 peak of ¥5.2bn, at the first half's 3.24%
net margin that is about **¥240m**. **The ¥371m consensus implies not just a revenue ramp but a net
margin back above 5% at the same time — and Q2 moved the other way.**

Now the terminal test. Give a cleanroom EPC contractor a defensible 20x (engineering names get
15–25x in good times):

| Market cap | Net profit required at 20x | Multiple of 2025 (¥205m) | Multiple of the 2022 peak (¥251m) | Revenue needed at 3.24% (1H26) | Revenue needed at 9.12% (best ever) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **¥13.0bn** (21 Jul) | ¥650m | **3.2x** | 2.6x | ¥20.1bn | ¥7.1bn |
| **¥17.0bn** | ¥850m | **4.1x** | 3.4x | ¥26.2bn | ¥9.3bn |
| **¥22.0bn** (29 Jun) | ¥1,100m | **5.4x** | 4.4x | ¥34.0bn | ¥12.1bn |

The book-value cross-check is blunter. BVPS ¥5.804, weighted ROE 2.83% for the half (c.5.7%
annualised). Using P/B = (ROE − g)/(r − g) with a 10% cost of equity and 5% growth: **4.25x P/B
implies ROE of about 26%, 7.26x implies about 41%** — against an actual ROE below 6%. There is no
precedent for a 26%–41% ROE in a 3%–5% net margin business that must fund its own projects.

**Ask:**
- What magnitude does management expect for **FY2026 revenue and net profit**? How much of the
  ¥5,629m backlog is expected to convert to revenue within 2026?
- What is the **normalised net margin**? Where did 2022's 9.12% (¥2,751m revenue, 14.67% gross
  margin) come from, and is it repeatable? **The only existence proof of a high margin at this
  company.**

### Q3. Secondary fit-out is 50.5% of backlog and 84.6% of the newest orders, but only 20.6% of first-half revenue — is the mix dividend real this time?

This is the most bullish set of numbers in the interim and deserves full weight:

| | 2023 | 2025 | **1H26 revenue** | **Backlog 30 Jun 2026** | **Orders 1 Jul – 25 Aug** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Secondary fit-out share | 13% | 28% | **20.59%** | **50.51%** | **84.58%** |

Secondary fit-out carried a 14.43% gross margin in 2025, 3.38pp above the 11.05% on primary systems.
**If the backlog converts as disclosed, the blended gross margin should trend to roughly
0.5 × 14.43% + 0.5 × 11.05% ≈ 12.7%**, well above today's 10.72%. That is a real, testable
improvement path — and a bigger one than the previous version of this memo assumed.

**Three hard constraints go with it:**

1. **The first half printed below what the mix alone implies.** Weighting 2025's sub-margins by 1H26
   revenue mix: 0.206 × 14.43% + 0.794 × 11.05% = **11.75%**, against an actual **10.72%** — **a full
   point lower. Project-level pricing and execution have deteriorated by about one point, offsetting
   the mix gain.**
2. **The ceiling is unchanged.** Even at 100% secondary fit-out, 2025's sub-margins imply **14.43%**.
   This business is structurally capped below 15%.
3. **Secondary fit-out execution is gated by the owner's tool deliveries.** The company says so
   plainly: its execution cycle "may be extended by the phased arrival of the owner's process
   tools". **Half the backlog now sits in a business whose delivery pace Baicheng does not control**
   — the biggest uncertainty for 2H26 revenue recognition and the mechanism by which contract assets
   keep swelling.

**Ask:**
- **What was the secondary fit-out gross margin in 1H26?** If it has fallen from 14.43%, the mix
  dividend is being competed away before it is banked.
- How much of the secondary fit-out backlog converts in 2H26, 1H27 and 2H27 respectively?
- Overseas backlog is only ¥351m (6.23%), yet 1H26 overseas signings were ¥367m and overseas revenue
  *fell*. **Is the overseas push a growth curve or still an experiment below scale?**

### Q4. Contract assets are 46.7% of total assets and cash fell ¥631m in six months — whose money funds this growth?

This is the set of numbers a headline will not capture and which will decide the outcome.

| 30 June 2026 | Amount | Change |
|---|---|---|
| **Receivables + contract assets, net** | **¥3,318m** | **56.33% of total assets** |
| of which contract assets | ¥2,751m | **+41.37%** (revenue +9.40%) |
| of which projects in progress | ¥2,043m (gross) | **+51.5%** |
| of which **completed but unsettled** | **¥1,077m (gross)** | +22.7% |
| Receivables | ¥568m | −6.61% |
| Cash | ¥1,529m | **−29.23% (−¥631m)** |
| Operating cash flow | **−¥606m** | (−¥643m a year earlier) |
| Interest-bearing debt | **zero** | — |
| Asset impairment losses, 1H | ¥58.65m | **68% of net profit** |

**Contract assets grew 4.4x faster than revenue** — the single most important diagnostic in this
business, the accounting equivalent of work done but not paid for. **¥1,077m of it is completed but
unsettled**, provisioned at 10.2%; work in progress is provisioned at 5.0%. Credit impairment losses
fell 35.92% year on year, and the company's own explanation is that **"fewer long-aged receivables
were recovered in the period"** — **the lower charge is not better collection; it is less
collection.**

On cash: ¥631m consumed in six months, ¥1,529m left, no debt. At that pace, own cash funds **a
little over a year** of this growth. That is the economic case for the ¥1.2bn placement — **and in
the interim report, the placement has vanished.**

**Section 5 of the interim ("Significant matters") contains no progress disclosure on the private
placement at all.** Meanwhile, on 20 July (board) and 31 July (EGM) the company approved deploying
the **remaining ¥135.24m of over-raised IPO proceeds** into "a cleanroom plant project undertaken by
the company" — **exactly the category the ¥1.2bn placement was to fund.** Separately, the IPO's "R&D
centre" project was pushed in February 2026 from April 2026 to **April 2028**, and ¥780m of the
¥915m over-raise had already gone to "working capital and debt repayment".

**Ask:**
- **Is the ¥1.2bn placement still live?** The board approved it on 26 May and an EGM considered it on
  11 June, yet the interim does not mention it. Not yet filed, withdrawn, or a disclosure omission?
  **This is the single thing most in need of clarification.**
- **How much working capital does each ¥100m of orders absorb?** ¥5,833m signed and ¥606m of
  operating outflow in the half implies about 10%. Converting the ¥5,629m backlog on the same ratio
  needs **another ¥500–600m of net funding.**
- The project list and owner acceptance dates behind the **¥1,077m of completed-but-unsettled
  contract assets**, and how much is aged over one year.
- Guarantees to subsidiaries include **¥152.57m to entities geared above 70%** (against a ¥1.4bn
  authorisation). What is the financial condition of those subsidiaries?

### Q5. The largest debtor is CEEDI (11th Institute) and the fourth is Shiyuan — half the pricing power is not yours

This is the newest and most easily missed structural fact in the interim. **Top five debtors by
receivables plus contract assets:**

| # | Counterparty | Receivables + contract assets | % | Provision | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CEEDI Technology Engineering (信息产业电子第十一设计研究院, "11th Institute")** | **¥440m** | **11.63%** | ¥32.35m | 7.4% |
| 2 | Customer 2 (unnamed) | ¥327m | 8.66% | ¥16.37m | 5.0% |
| 3 | Customer 3 (unnamed) | ¥296m | 7.84% | ¥15.54m | 5.2% |
| 4 | **Shiyuan Technology Engineering (世源科技工程)** | ¥176m | 4.65% | ¥22.58m | 12.8% |
| 5 | CRRC Zhuzhou Times Electric | ¥163m | 4.31% | ¥8.25m | 5.1% |
| | **Total** | **¥1,402m** | **37.09%** | ¥95.09m | 6.8% |

**CEEDI and Shiyuan are not owners; they are general contractors** — the interim itself lists
"Yikede, the 11th Institute and Shiyuan, general contractors in the industry" among its strategic
partners. **So the company's first- and fourth-largest counterparties (16.28% of receivables plus
contract assets combined) are peers using it as a subcontractor.**

That explains why the gross margin has been locked at 10%–12%: **on those projects Baicheng is not
negotiating with the owner but with the main contractor, one layer further down the value chain.**
Both also carry higher provisioning rates (7.4% and 12.8%) than CRRC (5.1%) — **general contractors
settle more slowly than owners do.**

**Ask:**
- **What share of first-half revenue came from direct EPC work for owners versus subcontracting?**
  That split determines who owns the ceiling on gross margin.
- **Who are the unnamed "Customer 2" and "Customer 3"** (16.5% combined) — owners or contractors?
- Are the CEEDI and Shiyuan relationships project-by-project or framework-based? **How much longer
  are their settlement cycles than direct owner contracts?**
- The interim flags "high customer concentration" as a risk but discloses no top-five revenue
  concentration. **Ask for that number.**

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Cycle position: so far this looks exactly like 2023–24.** Last time: 2023 revenue +44.6% and
profit −14.7%; 2024 revenue +31.8%, profit −0.8%, gross margin down to 10.00%. This time: Q2 2026
revenue +29.2%, gross margin c.10.14%, net margin 3.06%. **Peak profit still stands at ¥251m in 2022,
a year with revenue of only ¥2,751m.** If 2026–27 repeats it, revenue could reach ¥7–8bn with profit
stalled near ¥300m, **leaving today's market cap at 43–73x peak-cycle earnings.** The difference:
50.5% of this backlog is higher-margin secondary fit-out — **the one substantive way this cycle
differs from the last, and the only ground the bull case genuinely stands on.**

**Q7 — Share supply: the unlock is done, the controlling shareholder has not moved, and the placement
is unaccounted for.** Four things: (a) the 71.02% IPO lock-up expired on 10 April 2026 and
unrestricted shares are now **99.65%** of the register; (b) **Baiying Baicheng, Guo Jianting and Wuxi
Rongji made no sales, pledges or freezes during the half** — a fact that cuts the other way from the
previous version of this memo and should be recorded as such; (c) the employee platform **Wuxi
Rongji** filed on 6 July to sell up to 1.00% of capital (window 28 July–27 October), unexecuted as at
30 June; (d) **the ¥1.2bn placement (capped at 158,405,527 shares, 30% of pre-issue capital) does not
appear in the interim at all.** **On balance, share-supply pressure is lower than previously judged —
conditional on clarifying the placement.**

**Q8 — Governance: absolute control, chairman-and-CEO in one person, and a ten-day aborted deal.**
Guo Jianting held 89.53% pre-IPO and still controls 71.26% while holding both top jobs. Two new items
in the interim: **director Li Bingfeng resigned in July 2026 for work reasons** (staying on as head
of technology R&D management within the strategic business development centre), and the 31 July EGM
elected **Li Qixiong** in his place explicitly to "strengthen the modular strategic business". The
historical item stands: Liu Ping, registered at the 1994 founding, transferred her stake to Guo in
February 1995, treated by the sponsor as **unwinding a nominee arrangement**, with pre-IPO media
describing the dispute as unresolved. On 29 September 2025 the company signed a framework agreement
to acquire 55% of Shanghai Canshi, halted trading on 30 September and **terminated on 10 October —
ten days**. On the positive side: an **interim dividend of ¥0.50 per 10 shares (¥26.31m, 30.59% of
first-half profit)**, ¥365m distributed since listing, and a Wind ESG "A" rating two years running.

**Q9 — The incentive plan: the 2025 targets were missed, now confirmed and quantified.** The previous
version flagged this as unverified. The interim answers it **in the company's own words**: the share
movements of directors and executives "were caused by the company's 2025 performance failing to meet
the assessment requirements of the 2024 Restricted Share Incentive Plan".

| | People | Shares cancelled | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| First grant, second vesting tranche | 92 | 1,415,059 | failed the FY2025 test |
| Reserve grant, first vesting tranche | 27 | 400,782 | failed the FY2025 test |
| **Total** | **119** | **1,815,841** | bought back at ¥5.18, cancelled 18 June 2026 |
| of which directors and executives | 6 | 295,200 (35% of their holdings) | Shen Jinhuan, Lyu Guangshuai, Zhang Jiyong −64,800 each |

**This fact cuts two ways at once.** First, the scheme **does bind** — nothing was waived and senior
management lost 35% of their restricted shares, which is not common in the A-share market; count that
as a governance positive. Second, **the condition is "revenue *or* net profit" — and in 2025 revenue
was −21.19% and net profit −3.34%, so both failed.** The 2024 tranche vested precisely on revenue
growth of +31.78% in a year when profit fell 0.80%. **The scheme rewards scale, and the company could
not even hold scale.** **The remaining 1,815,839 restricted shares are tested on FY2026 — that target
is management's own profit commitment for this year and the cheapest available check on the ¥371m
consensus.**

**Q10 — Competitive structure and the peer read: the gap is widening.** The main players in
mid-to-high-end domestic cleanrooms are Shensanda A (China Electronics System, c.19.01% share),
Yaxiang (603929), Shenghui (603163), Jiangxi Hantang and Baicheng. **Yaxiang pre-announced 1H26 net
profit of ¥456–525m (+183% to +226%), driven by Singapore projects that are "large in amount with
relatively good margins" — five to six times Baicheng's ¥86m.** 2024 overseas gross margins: Yaxiang
21.0%, Shenghui 15.5%, against Baicheng's 15.07% in 2025. **The "cleanrooms plus semiconductors"
trade does produce large profits — for whoever is in the right geography on the right projects.**
Baicheng's overseas backlog is ¥351m (6.23%) and its overseas revenue fell in the half.

**Q11 — New businesses: both strategic curves lose money, and R&D is close to zero.** The modular
subsidiary **Baixin Zhizao** signed ¥31.73m of orders in the half (+123.49%, 78.61% overseas) on
revenue of ¥19.15m (+94.55%) for a **net loss of ¥11.98m** (¥11.74m a year earlier); the chemical
molecular filtration JV **Qiyi** (formed April 2025 with Taiwan's Qiyi Technology) lost **¥4.07m
attributable** (¥1.15m a year earlier). **Together they cost ¥16.05m, 19% of first-half profit, and
the drag is widening.** Meanwhile **R&D expense was ¥3.58m, 0.13% of revenue**, and the patent estate
is mostly utility models (97 utility, 3 invention). **For a company priced as a technology stock,
that R&D intensity needs an explanation.** Electing Li Qixiong as a director to "strengthen the
modular strategic business" suggests the company knows this curve needs help.

**Q12 — Source quality and remaining gaps.** **Parts 1–4 of the 1H26 interim report (pages 1–184 of
230) have now been obtained in full**; part 5 (the tail of the financial statement notes) has not.
**All 1H26 data in this memo comes from the report itself.** Three prior judgements based on
second-hand summaries are now corrected or confirmed:
1. "End-2025 backlog: domestic ¥210m / overseas ¥2,230m" — **confirmed to have been printed the wrong
   way round**. On the interim's basis, domestic is 93.77% and overseas 6.23%.
2. "1Q26 operating cash flow −¥800m" — the half-year figure is −¥606m, which is compatible (Q2 was a
   c.¥190m inflow), **but the quarterly figure remains second-hand and unconfirmed.**
3. "2025 incentive targets missed" — **now confirmed by the interim**, with people and share counts.
   Still not obtained: **gross margin by business (secondary fit-out vs primary), top-five customer
   revenue concentration and the identity of "Customer 2" and "Customer 3", the status of the ¥1.2bn
   placement, the year-by-year conversion schedule of the backlog, and bid margins on 1H26 orders.**

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **The 3Q26 report (late October) — the single most important event.** Q2 proved orders convert into
   revenue; Q3 must answer whether that conversion carries profit. Three numbers: **quarterly gross
   margin back above 11%**, quarterly net margin, and whether operating cash outflow narrows. **If Q3
   revenue grows strongly again with gross margin stuck around 10%, the 2024 script is confirmed.**
2. **Contract assets, level and composition.** +41.37% to ¥2,751m in the half. **If they keep growing
   far faster than revenue into Q3 — particularly if the completed-but-unsettled portion passes
   ¥1.2bn — the cash quality of this growth has already broken.**
3. **Full-year signings and the monthly pace.** Known: c.¥1,110m/month (Jan–Apr), c.¥730m (May–Jun),

## 4. Provisional read

**On the business, the interim makes the quality clearer than before — on both sides.** The good:
backlog of ¥5,629m, up 222% year on year; ¥5,833m of first-half signings, an all-time high; Q2
revenue +29.2% — **the conversion is real, not a story**; secondary fit-out is 50.51% of backlog and
84.58% of July–August signings, **the one substantive difference from the 2023–24 cycle**; zero
interest-bearing debt; an interim dividend at 30.59% of profit; a controlling shareholder who has
neither sold nor pledged. And when the 2025 targets were missed, the company cancelled 119 people's
restricted shares by the book — **that constraint is real**. The customer list, from Samsung, SK
hynix, SMIC, Hua Hong, CXMT and YMTC through to BOE, WuXi Biologics and AstraZeneca, is thirty years
of accumulation, not packaging.

**On the stock, Q2 delivered the key piece of counter-evidence.** In the same quarter revenue grew
29.2%, the gross margin fell from 11.94% to about 10.14% and the net margin to 3.06% — **a replica of
2024's "revenue +31.8%, profit −0.8%, gross margin 10.00%", with this ramp only beginning.** The
first-half blended gross margin of 10.72% is a full point below the 11.75% implied by applying 2025's
sub-margins to the first-half revenue mix, **which says project-level pricing and execution are
deteriorating and eating the mix dividend.** Meanwhile contract assets swelled 4.4x faster than
revenue to ¥2,751m (receivables plus contract assets are 56.33% of total assets), cash fell ¥631m in
six months, and annualised ROE is below 6%.

**And the simplest arithmetic is the hardest to argue with: ¥86m earned in the first half against a
¥371m consensus requires ¥285m in the second — 3.31x the first half, where the last two years
delivered 1.13x and 1.46x.** On 2025's seasonality the year lands near ¥212m. Across the ¥12.98bn–
¥22.16bn market cap range that is **61x–105x earnings and 4.25x–7.26x book, on an ROE below 6%.**

**Governance this time gives mixed signals, and they should be recorded honestly.** For: targets
missed meant shares cancelled, with directors and executives losing 35% of their restricted holdings;
no selling or pledging by the controlling shareholder; the interim dividend was paid. Against: the
test is "revenue **or** net profit" — a scheme that rewards scale, at a company with three years of
revenue growth without profit growth; the ¥1.2bn placement has disappeared from the interim while the
remaining ¥135m of over-raised IPO money went into the same project category; the IPO R&D centre
slipped to 2028; and R&D is 0.13% of revenue.

The honest formulation: **a real industry upcycle, a real all-time-high backlog, a real revenue
conversion — and a market pricing a 10%-gross-margin contractor like a technology manufacturer.**
What would flip the conclusion is narrower and more specific than before: **a Q3 gross margin back
above 11% (ideally 12%) with revenue still growing and contract assets growing more slowly than
revenue.** Those three together would show the secondary fit-out dividend genuinely outweighing the
price given away at tender. **As long as Q3 gross margin stays near 10%, a larger backlog makes the
next two years' margins worse, not better — because the volume is itself the reason for the
discount.**

**One next step: wait for the third quarter, and before then get two numbers out of the company — the
first-half gross margin split between secondary fit-out and primary systems, and the exact status of
the ¥1.2bn placement.** Until those two are in hand, this valuation is buying a conversion-rate
assumption that was tested once in 2024 and re-tested in Q2 2026, both times with the same answer.
