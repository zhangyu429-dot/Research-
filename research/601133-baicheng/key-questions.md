# 柏诚股份 (Baicheng, 601133.SH) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 30 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Baicheng is **a cleanroom engineering contractor earning roughly a 5% net margin, sitting inside a
genuine semiconductor fab-building cycle, priced by the market like a high-margin technology
manufacturer**. It makes no product. It delivers design–procurement–modularisation–construction
(EPFC) cleanroom systems to wafer fabs, display plants and pharmaceutical plants; revenue is
recognised on project progress, profit is the spread between the bid price and execution cost, and
cash is set by the owner's payment schedule. Founded in 1994, it listed on the SSE main board on
10 April 2023 (one of the first cohort under the full registration system).

The whole investment compresses into one question: **how much profit will the ¥4.325bn of new
orders signed in the first four months of 2026 actually turn into — and has 2024 already answered
it?**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. How much *profit* does ¥4.325bn of new orders become — 2024's answer was "almost none"

This is where the whole thesis lands, and it is the only question with a historical control group.

The orders are real and well corroborated: ¥4,325m signed ex-VAT between 1 January and 28 April
2026, more than FY2025's new orders (¥3,762m) or revenue (¥4,133m), with domestic semiconductor work
at 80.33%. The demand behind them is equally real: cleanrooms are 15%–18% of total fab investment;
2026 global semiconductor capex is put at roughly US$200bn, up about 20%; Hua Hong's Fab9A is
expected to reach full capacity in 3Q26 and Fab9B broke ground in March 2026 (c. US$6bn); CXMT is
pushing DRAM phase 3 and a Shanghai HBM packaging plant; YMTC's 3D NAND phases 4–5 are in planning;
and the CXMT/YMTC listings will fund more capex. **Nobody disputes the demand.**

What is disputed is the conversion rate. Put three years side by side:

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (¥m) | 3,980 | **5,244** | 4,133 |
| Revenue YoY | +44.6% | **+31.8%** | −21.2% |
| Net profit (¥m) | 214 | 212 | 205 |
| Gross margin | 11.33% | **10.00%** | 11.93% |

**In 2023–24 the company pushed revenue to ¥5.2bn and earned not one yuan more.** Its own
explanation at the time: larger scale, more large projects, intensifying competition, elevated raw
material costs. In other words, **in this business scaling up is itself margin-dilutive** — big
projects are bid harder, run longer and absorb more working capital.

So "¥4.3bn of orders equals an earnings breakout" needs first to explain why it did not in 2024.

**Ask:**
- **Backlog at 30 June 2026 and new orders signed in H1.** Was the ¥4,325m a burst of pent-up
  signings or a new run rate? That one number decides 2027.
- **The bid gross margin on the new orders versus the 2023–24 vintage.** If the answer is "in line
  with history", then ¥4.3bn of orders at c.11% gross margin and the historical net/gross conversion
  of about 0.42 is **roughly ¥200m of profit**, spread over a 6–12 month execution window.
- **How much of the ¥4,325m is secondary fit-out versus primary systems** — that alone drives the
  conversion rate (14.43% vs 11.05%).
- **Revenue recognition schedule.** On a 6–12 month execution window, orders signed in January–April
  should show up from 2H26 into 1H27. **The 3Q26 report is the first real test.**
- **Full-year revenue guidance.** Q1 revenue was still −17.1%; getting back above ¥5.2bn for the year
  requires roughly +40% YoY over Q2–Q4. Has management ever framed anything of that magnitude?

### Q2. Reverse the valuation: what profit does the market cap require, and does the bridge exist?

No forecast — just arithmetic. Give a cleanroom EPC contractor a defensible terminal multiple of
**20x** (Chinese engineering names typically get 15–25x in good times; 20x is already generous for a
business with no repeat purchase, no patent and no pricing power).

| Market cap | Net profit required at 20x | Multiple of 2025 (¥205m) | Multiple of the 2022 peak (¥251m) | Revenue needed at a 5.0% net margin | Revenue needed at 9.12% (best ever) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **¥13.0bn** (21 Jul) | ¥650m | **3.2x** | 2.6x | ¥13.0bn | ¥7.1bn |
| **¥17.0bn** | ¥850m | **4.1x** | 3.4x | ¥17.0bn | ¥9.3bn |
| **¥22.0bn** (29 Jun) | ¥1,100m | **5.4x** | 4.4x | ¥22.0bn | ¥12.1bn |

**Read the table carefully: even at the lowest market cap of this cycle you need ¥7.1bn of revenue
(1.35x the all-time revenue peak of ¥5,244m) *and* a net margin restored to the best figure the
company has ever printed, 9.12% — both at once.** Hold the net margin at 2025's 5.0% and ¥13bn of
market cap requires **¥13bn of revenue, 3.1x 2025**.

Cross-check on book value. BVPS ¥5.67; weighted ROE 4.54% for 9M25 (roughly 6.9% for the full year).
Using P/B = (ROE − g)/(r − g) with a 10% cost of equity and 5% perpetual growth:
- 4.4x P/B (¥24.66) implies **ROE ≈ 27%**;
- 7.4x P/B (¥42.11) implies **ROE ≈ 42%**.

**The company actually earns about 7%.** There is no industry precedent for a 27%–42% ROE in a 5%
net margin, capital-advancing, people-and-project-management business — unless it becomes a
different business.

**Ask:**
- What does management believe the **normalised net margin** is? 8%? 10%? On what basis?
- How was 2022's 9.12% net margin achieved (¥2,751m revenue, 14.67% gross margin), and is that
  project mix repeatable? **This is the single most important historical question at the company,
  because it is the only existence proof of a high margin.**
- If 2027 revenue reaches ¥8bn, what gross and net margins does management expect? Ask for a range.

### Q3. Is the 11%–12% gross margin the industry's ceiling, or Baicheng's?

The bull case is mix: secondary fit-out from 13% of revenue in 2023 to 28% in 2025 (14.43% gross
margin vs 11.05% for primary systems), and overseas to 8.66% (15.07% vs c.11.63% domestic).
**The direction is right. Size the effect before believing it:**

Weighting 2025's disclosed sub-margins: 0.28 × 14.43% + 0.72 × 11.05% = **12.00%**, against a
reported 11.93%. The fit is close, so those two lines do explain the *level* of the gross margin.
The next step is the one that matters:

- Moving secondary fit-out from 13% to 28% — fifteen points of mix — is worth only about
  **+0.5pp** of blended gross margin on the same arithmetic.
- Yet the actual path was **11.33% (2023) → 10.00% (2024) → 11.93% (2025)** — a swing of about two
  points, **four times the contribution of mix**.

**So the year-to-year gross margin is set not by mix but by project pricing and execution** — bid
competition, the share of large projects, settlements and claims, raw materials. Put differently,
**secondary fit-out and overseas are real but slow (a few tenths of a point per year); what will
decide 2026–27 is bidding discipline inside this boom.** And the lesson of 2024 is that the year
with the most orders and the hottest demand had the lowest gross margin on record (10.00%).

The ceiling arithmetic: even at 40% secondary fit-out, blended gross margin is 0.4 × 14.43% + 0.6 ×
11.05% = **12.4%**; at 100% it is **14.43%**. **This business is structurally capped below 15%.**

**Ask:**
- **1H26 secondary fit-out share and its gross margin.** If the share keeps rising while its margin
  falls, secondary work is being competed away too and that improvement path closes.
- **Bid gross margins on 2026 orders** versus the 2023–24 vintage. The hardest number to get and
  the most valuable.
- The 15.07% overseas margin against peers: in 2024, **Yaxiang's overseas gross margin was 21.0% and
  Shenghui's 15.5%**. Baicheng is a follower in South-East Asia — is its overseas premium durable?
- Decompose 2025's +1.93pp margin improvement into **mix / price / settlement / the denominator
  effect of falling revenue**.

### Q4. Cash: why does a company with no interest-bearing debt and ¥2.15bn of cash raise ¥1.2bn at a high share price?

The point is not to question the legality of the raise; it is to use it to measure how capital-hungry
this business really is.

The facts: the company says it has **no interest-bearing debt and over ¥2.15bn of cash**; FY2025
operating cash flow was **¥77m** (2024: ¥215m, −64.15%), **under 40% of net profit**; 1Q26 operating
cash flow was **−¥800m** against −¥678m a year earlier — **the outflow widened by ¥122m in a quarter
when revenue fell 17%**. And nearly **¥400m of IPO proceeds remain unspent**.

Only one explanation is self-consistent: **in this business, growth must be funded in advance.** The
faster orders grow, the more is locked up in receivables, contract assets and retentions. ¥4,325m of
new orders plausibly ties up working capital in the **billion-yuan range** — and the ¥1.2bn placement
(¥360m of it explicitly for working capital) confirms rather than contradicts that.

It also explains how "revenue up, profit flat" and "cash on the balance sheet" coexist: **the profit
is on paper, the cash is inside the order book.**

**Ask:**
- **The combined balance at 30 June 2026 of receivables, contract assets and long-dated retentions
  (booked in other non-current assets)**, and the trend against revenue. In 2025 the company booked
  ¥23.73m of bad-debt provisions on project receivables (¥31.30m less than the prior year), after
  ¥54.57m in 2024 and ¥62.95m in 2023 — **is the lower charge better collection, or simply the
  arithmetic of lower revenue?**
- **How much working capital does each ¥100m of orders absorb?** Ask for the company's own rule of
  thumb. At 10%–15%, ¥10bn of annual signings needs ¥1.0–1.5bn of net funding — **which means this
  growth requires a capital raise every year.**
- **Dilution mechanics** of the ¥1.2bn placement. At the 80%-of-VWAP floor, ¥1.2bn is about
  **60.8m shares (+11.6%)** at ¥24.66 and about **35.6m shares (+6.8%)** at ¥42.11. **The higher the
  share price, the smaller the dilution — that is a direct motive for pushing a placement at a high
  price, and it deserves to be stated openly.**
- Three years post-IPO with nearly ¥400m unspent, why is another ¥1.2bn of *similar* projects needed
  now?

### Q5. The customers are the toughest buyers on earth — that is both the moat and the answer on pricing power

The customer list is genuinely hard to replicate: Samsung, SK hynix, SMIC, Hua Hong, CXMT, YMTC,
Nexchip, XMC, Silan, CR Micro, CRRC Times. Cleanrooms are a precondition for yield, and owners are
extremely conservative about vendor qualification. **This is a real, durable barrier to entry.**

The same fact read the other way: **these are the most capable price-squeezers in the world.** They
tender, they subcontract, they pay on milestones and they hold retentions — and they run the same
tender past Shensanda A (China Electronics System Engineering, the leader at c.19.01% share),
Yaxiang, Shenghui and Jiangxi Hantang. Baicheng's 11%–12% gross margin is not a sign of poor
management; **it is the price the buyer sets.**

So the question here is: **has this build-out cycle briefly handed pricing power back to the
contractors?** If it has, 2026 bid margins are visibly better than 2023–24. If it has not, ¥4.3bn of
orders is simply a bigger version of the same business.

**Ask:**
- **Top-five customer concentration** over three periods, and the largest single customer. Very high
  concentration is inherent to EPC; the trend matters more than the level.
- Are there **framework or annual agreements**, or is every project separately tendered?
- **The domestic/overseas split of backlog.** Secondary sources give "domestic ¥210m / overseas
  ¥2,230m", which cannot coexist with overseas revenue of 8.66% (c.¥358m) in 2025 — **the split has
  almost certainly been printed the wrong way round (domestic ¥2,230m / overseas ¥210m) and must be
  checked against the annual report.**
- Are the Samsung and SK hynix orders for their **mainland China** plants or their **South-East
  Asian** ones? Only the latter evidences the overseas thesis.

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Where in the cycle is 2026: a new one, or a rerun of 2023–24?** The company has already shown
one full cycle: 2021–22 (revenue ¥2,742m → ¥2,751m, profit ¥152m → ¥251m, gross margin 14.67%), the
2023–24 volume push (¥3,980m → ¥5,244m, profit ¥214m → ¥212m, gross margin down to 10.00%), then the
2025 fade (¥4,133m, ¥205m). **Note the shape: peak profit came in a low-revenue year (2022), trough
profit in the high-revenue year (2024).** That is not coincidence; it is the physics of the business
— volume is bought with price. If 2026–27 repeats it, revenue could reach ¥7–8bn while profit stalls
at ¥300–400m (in line with the ¥371m consensus), **which still leaves today's market cap at 35–60x
peak-cycle earnings.**

**Q7 — Share supply is the most quantifiable item here, and the least favourable.** Four things at
once: (a) **71.02% of the shares (375m) unlocked on 10 April 2026**, worth ¥9,255m, taking the float
from 149m to 524m shares — **essentially the whole company is now tradeable** (margin balance of
¥385m on 26 June was 1.92% of float, implying a float of about ¥20bn); (b) the employee platform
**Wuxi Rongji filed on 6 July 2026 to sell up to 5,262,025 shares (1.00% of capital)** in a window
running 28 July–27 October; (c) executives Hua Xiaoling and Zhu Chenguang filed on 14 January 2026
to sell up to 54,000 shares and **let the plan lapse unexecuted** on 6 May (a separate director/
executive selling plan had been filed on 29 August 2025); (d) the placement is capped at
**158,405,527 shares, 30% of pre-issue capital**. **None of this is a fundamental argument, but
together it says that after a 237.96% rise since November 2025, the best-informed holders have been
on the selling side.** The one counter-signal — the lapsed 54,000-share plan — is worth about ¥1.6m
at ¥30 and carries little information.

**Q8 — Governance: absolute control, chairman-and-CEO in one person, and a ten-day aborted
acquisition.** Guo Jianting held 89.53% before the IPO and still controls about 71%, while serving as
**both chairman and general manager**; minority holders have no mechanism to change anything. There
is a disclosed historical **nominee-shareholding dispute**: Liu Ping, registered as a shareholder at
the 1994 founding, transferred her entire stake to Guo in February 1995; the sponsor's interviews
explain this as unwinding a nominee arrangement (Guo asked colleague Zhu Jiang to hold for him; Zhu
lacked the required documentation, so Zhu's spouse Liu Ping was registered instead). Pre-IPO media
(Time Weekly) described the dispute as unresolved. **It is a disclosed, sponsor-verified historical
item and should not be overstated — but it is background you need.** More informative is what
happened recently: **on 29 September 2025 the company signed a framework agreement to buy 55% of
Shanghai Canshi Engineering Equipment and halted its shares on 30 September; on 10 October it
announced termination and resumed trading — ten days from start to collapse**, over "core terms the
parties could not agree". A deal pursued under a trading halt that fell apart in ten days says
something about deal preparation, worth remembering when judging execution on a ¥1.2bn placement.
The company also proposed the **by-election of a non-independent director** on 15 July 2026, with the
shares down 5.42% that day; the reason for the board change should be confirmed in the interim
report.

**Q9 — The incentive-plan test condition is the sharpest detail in this memo.** The 2024 restricted
share plan granted at ¥5.45 (reserve tranche adjusted to ¥5.23; 801,564 shares to 27 people on 24
June 2025). Its company-level performance condition is **"revenue *or* net profit"** — *or*, not
*and*. The result: **the 2024 tranche vested on revenue growth of +31.78%** (board approval 8 August
2025, 1,886,744 shares released to 92 people) **in a year when net profit was −0.80%.** In other
words, **the plan rewards revenue scale, not profit** — which is exactly what 2023–24 delivered.

**That makes one number essential: the FY2025 (second tranche) assessment result.** FY2025 revenue
was −21.19% and net profit −3.34% — **both negative**, so unless the target is an absolute threshold
or a cumulative measure it is hard to see how it was met. Some secondary sources assert that "2025
missed the targets, hence the buyback and cancellation", but the verifiable filing says the July 2026
cancellation covered **only 26,244 shares from two departing grantees** (0.005% of capital), with the
buyback price adjusted from ¥5.18 to ¥5.11 for the ¥0.07 dividend. **"Missed targets" and "departure
buyback" are two different things, and the current record supports only the second; the first must be
verified against the interim report or a dedicated filing.** It is the cheapest available test of
whether management will be held to profit.

**Q10 — Competitive structure: this is not a winner-take-all industry.** The main players in
mid-to-high-end domestic cleanrooms are **Shensanda A (China Electronics System, c.19.01% share and
first), Yaxiang Integration (603929), Shenghui Integration (603163), Jiangxi Hantang** and Baicheng.
In a 2022 comparison Shenghui's gross margin was 15.44% and Baicheng's 14.67% — **Baicheng is not the
highest-margin operator in its own industry.** The overseas gap is wider: 2024 overseas gross margins
were 21.0% at Yaxiang and 15.5% at Shenghui, against Baicheng's 15.07% in 2025. For scale, **Yaxiang
pre-announced 1H26 net profit of ¥456–525m (+183% to +226%), driven by large, well-priced Singapore
projects** — several times Baicheng's half-year profit. **So "cleanrooms plus semiconductors" can
absolutely produce large profits — for whoever is in the right geography on the right projects.**
Baicheng is 8.66% overseas; Yaxiang runs a two-engine model centred on Singapore.

**Q11 — Dividends and capital allocation as a statement.** FY2025 cash dividends totalled ¥63.36m,
**30.88%** of net profit (¥0.7 per 10 shares), with ¥328m distributed cumulatively since listing; at
the results briefing management said plainly that **the 2025 payout was cut to reserve funds for
expanding the core business**. Together with the ¥1.2bn placement and nearly ¥400m of unspent IPO
money, that is a coherent growth-first policy. **It is not wrong in itself — but it means
shareholders are funding, at 4.4x–7.4x book, a business earning about 7% on book.** The return on
that reinvestment has to be asked for separately.

**Q12 — Source quality and disclosure gaps (unusually important this time):**
1. **The 1H26 report was published on 25 August 2026 and none of its contents could be obtained in
   this environment** — every primary and secondary disclosure site (cninfo, SSE, Sina Finance,
   Eastmoney, CFi, 10jqka, STCN, Xueqiu) is blocked by the network egress proxy, and no accessible
   secondary source has yet summarised it. **Every 2Q26 datapoint is therefore missing, not
   unchanged.**
2. The "backlog at end-2025: domestic ¥210m / overseas ¥2,230m" split contradicts overseas revenue of
   8.66% and **has been treated as printed the wrong way round, and not used.**
3. "1Q26 operating cash flow −¥800m" is second-hand; its magnitude is corroborated by 1Q25's −¥678m,
   so it is used here but **must be checked against the quarterly filing.**
4. AI-generated "why it hit limit-down" articles about 21 July (citing "project delays" and
   "guarantee risk") have **no filing support and have all been discarded.**
5. Items that could not be obtained and therefore became questions: **1H26 financials and backlog,
   top-five customer concentration, bid margins on new orders, receivable and contract-asset
   balances, the FY2025 incentive assessment result, placement review progress, and current total
   assets and gearing.**

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **The 1H26 report (published 25 August 2026; not obtainable for this memo) — read it first,
   ahead of everything else.** Priorities: H1 revenue and profit (Q1 was −17.1% / −29.7%; did Q2
   turn?); **blended gross margin**; **backlog at 30 June and H1 new orders**; **receivables +
   contract assets + retentions**; operating cash flow; secondary fit-out and overseas shares; the
   top five customers.
2. **The 3Q26 report (late October) — the first genuine test of order conversion.** On a 6–12 month
   execution window, the ¥4,325m signed in January–April should be visibly in revenue by Q3. **If Q3
   revenue is still not growing year on year, the order-to-revenue chain is broken, not merely
   early.**
3. **The cadence of full-year signings.** After ¥4,325m in four months, the monthly rate from May to
   December is the only way to tell a pent-up release from a run rate. ¥5.5–6.5bn for the year says
   the first four months were a catch-up; ¥9bn or more is a trend.
4. **Whether gross margin holds near 11.9% while volumes rise.** The 2024 lesson is that the volume
   year printed 10.00%. **In a quarter when revenue grows again, gross margin not falling is worth
   more than any order headline.**
5. **Placement progress:** after the 11 June EGM, SSE acceptance, queries and responses, then CSRC
   registration — and finally **the issue price and the actual number of shares**. The price sets the
   dilution and is the market's own vote on the growth story.
6. **The FY2025 (second tranche) vesting decision under the 2024 incentive plan.** Vesting in a year
   of falling revenue *and* falling profit would show the conditions bind on nothing; not vesting
   would be a point in management's favour. **Ask at the same time for the FY2026 target — that is
   management's own profit commitment for this year, and more reliable than any sell-side number.**
7. **Execution of Wuxi Rongji's sales (28 July–27 October)** and **whether Baiying Baicheng or Guo
   Jianting file any sale, pledge or pre-disclosure post-unlock.** This is the first full observation
   window after the lock-up.
8. **The frequency and size of major project win announcements.** There was one on 30 September 2025,
   one on 29 October and a ¥325m (VAT-inclusive) contract in December. A visibly faster cadence with
   larger single tickets in 2026 is independent evidence of improving order quality.
9. **Downstream capex actually landing:** Hua Hong Fab9B, CXMT DRAM phase 3 and the Shanghai HBM
   packaging plant, YMTC phases 4–5. **Cleanrooms are the front-loaded spend, so their orders lead
   equipment orders and lead earnings.**
10. **Yaxiang's and Shenghui's gross margins and overseas orders in the same periods.** Peer margins
    at the same moment are the cleanest way to separate "industry-wide pricing improvement" from a
    Baicheng-specific problem.

## 4. Provisional read

**On the business:** this is a real, substantial contractor, qualified by the most demanding
customers in the world. Thirty years of accumulation; a customer list running from SMIC, Hua Hong,
CXMT and YMTC to Samsung and SK hynix; integrated EPFC capability; secondary fit-out from 13% to 28%
of revenue; overseas revenue doubling at a higher margin; no interest-bearing debt and ¥2.15bn of
cash. None of that is packaging. The cycle is real too: cleanrooms are 15%–18% of fab investment and
mainland China's build-out is accelerating. **The ¥4,325m signed in the first four months of 2026 is
the best order intake in the company's history, and that is not in dispute.**

**On the stock:** nearly every difficulty sits in one place — **this business converts orders into
profit very inefficiently, and 2024 already proved it.** Revenue went from ¥3,980m to ¥5,244m and
profit went from ¥214m to ¥212m; peak profit still stands at ¥251m in 2022 and has fallen for four
consecutive years; net margin is 5.0% and gross margin 11.93%, while the structural improvements
(secondary fit-out, overseas) contribute a few tenths of a point a year against a ceiling below 15%.
For that asset the market has paid **98–105x trailing earnings and 4.4x–7.4x book, on an ROE of about
7%.** Reverse the valuation at 20x and **even at the 21 July low you need profit at 3.2x the 2025
level — ¥7.1bn of revenue with the net margin back at its all-time best of 9.12%, both at once.**
The consensus mid-point for 2026 is ¥371m — **not even half of that bridge.**

**Governance does not settle the conclusion, but it tilts the scale the same way.** 71% of the shares
came free in April; the employee platform has filed to sell 1%; the placement is capped at 30% of
capital and dilutes least at a high price — **the relationship between the timing of the raise and
the share price deserves to be discussed openly.** And the incentive plan tests "revenue **or** net
profit", which let the 2024 tranche vest on +31.8% revenue in a year of −0.8% profit — **a scheme
that rewards exactly the revenue-without-profit growth the company then delivered for three years.**

The honest formulation: **a real industry upcycle, a real top-tier operator, and a market pricing an
engineering contractor like a technology manufacturer.** What would flip the conclusion is narrow and
specific: **revenue returning to year-on-year growth in 2H26 while blended gross margin rises above
13% and holds for two consecutive quarters, with operating cash flow positive.** Those three together
would say this cycle's orders differ in kind from the last one's — that the boom briefly handed
pricing power back to the contractor. If gross margin instead slides back toward 10% as volumes
rise, ¥4.3bn of orders ends where ¥5.2bn of 2024 revenue ended.

**One next step only: get the 1H26 report (published 25 August) and read three numbers first — Q2
gross margin, backlog at 30 June, and receivables plus contract assets.** Until those three are on
the table, any position is a fresh bet on an experiment that was run once in 2024 and came back
negative. And at this valuation, a gross margin miss is not an earnings miss — it is a re-rating.
