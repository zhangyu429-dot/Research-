# 洁特生物 (Jiete / Jet Bio-Filtration, 688026.SH) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 13 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Jet Bio-Filtration is a **contract manufacturer of disposable plastic lab consumables that is
marketed as an import-substitution story, wrapped around a balance sheet whose defining feature
is a ¥440m convertible bond that will almost certainly never convert.** The products are cell
culture plates and flasks (生物培养类) and pipette tips, tubes and plates (液体处理类) —
genuinely consumable, genuinely repeat-purchase, and made at scale in a new 160,000 m² Zengcheng
park. But roughly **57% of the group's revenue and the majority of its gross profit is exported,
mostly as ODM** — white-label manufacturing for the very Western lab-supply houses (Thermo
Fisher, VWR, Corning-type distributors) that the "国产替代" narrative says Jiete is displacing.
Meanwhile the equity story sits on top of a security structure that has quietly turned into
straight debt: **洁特转债 is at ~28% of its conversion price, a putback right went live this
week, and the board has now refused three times to reset the conversion price.**

So the investment is three separate bets that are usually conflated: (1) that the export/ODM
engine survives a ~35% US tariff; (2) that the domestic own-brand business scales fast enough
to replace it; and (3) that the company funds a **¥484m cash redemption in June 2028** without
the equity issue that would reprice everything.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. Is 洁特转债 debt or equity — and what does the board's refusal to reset tell you?

This ranks first because it is the only question with a hard, dated, unavoidable cash number
attached, and because it is almost entirely absent from the promotional coverage of this name.

The arithmetic is not complicated, and it points the opposite way to the alarmist reading:

- **The putback is live but probably not the danger.** Putback price is face plus accrued
  interest on the fifth-year coupon (1.80%) — roughly **¥100.3 per ¥100** in August. Holding
  instead pays the **¥1.80 coupon in June 2027 and ¥110 at maturity in June 2028** — about
  **¥111.8 over ~22 months, ~5.8% annualised**, on an A+ credit. **A rational holder does not put
  back at 100.3 to give up 110.** The bond traded at ¥109.4 in mid-2025, consistent with the
  market pricing maturity value, not putback value. So the base case is that **most of the ¥440m
  is not put back in August — it is deferred to a ¥484m redemption in June 2028.**
- **Which means the real event is 2028, not this month.** ¥484m against a ¥1.93bn market cap and
  ~¥1.24bn of equity. Reported cash was **¥438m at Q3 2025**, of which **¥104m was restricted at
  year-end 2025** (plus another ¥100m of restricted other current assets). Free cash is therefore
  materially below the redemption obligation, and the company still has a second-phase 80,000 m²
  plant under construction.
- **The board has now refused to reset the conversion price three times** — the November 2025
  trigger, the decision of 16 March 2026, and the decision of 8 July 2026, the last of which
  additionally **commits not to reset through 8 October 2026**, i.e. straight through the putback
  window. Resetting to something near the market price (subject to the floor at audited book
  value per share, ~¥8.8) would convert ¥440m of debt into roughly 30–33m shares — **21–24%
  dilution** — and make the 2028 obligation disappear. They have chosen the cash liability over
  the dilution, three times, while the founder holds 29.26%.

**Ask:**
- **The maturity redemption price and the putback price, side by side, in the company's own
  numbers** — and management's own estimate of how much of the ¥440m they expect to be put back
  in this window. If they expect a large putback, they are contradicting the bond's own math.
- **What is unrestricted cash today, and what is the funding plan for June 2028?** Bank
  facilities, the placement, operating cash flow — name the mix and the amounts.
- **Why not reset?** Three refusals is a policy, not a judgement call. Is it (a) a view that the
  share price recovers to ¥48 — which would require a 3.5x move — (b) unwillingness to dilute the
  founder's 29.26%, or (c) the book-value floor making a useful reset impossible? These have very
  different implications and only one of them is about shareholders.
- **The 23 July 2026 conversion-price adjustment** following the 2025 dividend: what is the
  current conversion price, to the cent, and the current outstanding bond balance.

### Q2. Did the export franchise break, or is it just tariffs?

Overseas revenue fell **8.81% in 2025** while domestic grew **18.21%**. That single crossing is
the most consequential operating fact of the year, because overseas is the **larger** half, the
**higher-gross-margin** half (41.83% vs 40.37%), and — through ODM — the source of **57% of
gross profit**.

Management's framing is that this is tariffs and destocking, and that it is recovering: at a
**~35% tariff** they say Chinese-origin product still prices competitively against other origins,
that H1 was hit but **major US customers' order volumes have progressively recovered**, and that
full US–China decoupling in lab consumables is unlikely. That is a coherent story and may be
right. But three things have to be separated before you can price it:

1. **Tariff incidence.** A 35% tariff on ~30%+ of group revenue is not absorbed by goodwill. Who
   pays — Jiete via price concession, the ODM customer, or the end lab? The 2025 overseas gross
   margin fell only 1.47pp, which on its face says Jiete did *not* eat most of it. Verify that
   against 2026, when the tariffs were in force for a full period.
2. **ODM structural risk.** ODM customers are lab-supply distributors and multinationals whose
   own incentive under tariffs is to **re-source out of China entirely**. That is not a pricing
   negotiation, it is a qualification decision, and once made it is slow to reverse. The company
   has said it is screening **overseas acquisitions — factories or distribution channels — to add
   a non-China origin.** That is the correct response and it is also an admission of the risk.
3. **Which customers left.** 2024's top-five industrial customers included one that entered the
   list purely because it **pre-ordered to stockpile**. Stockpiling pulls revenue forward; the
   2025 decline may be partly that unwinding rather than share loss.

**Ask:** overseas revenue **split US / Europe / APAC / other, for 2024, 2025 and H1 2026**;
**top-five customer concentration for 2025 and the trend over three years**, with the same
customers identified period to period; realised **ASP vs volume** for the ODM book; and the
status, size and jurisdiction of any **overseas plant or distributor acquisition** under
negotiation — this is the pending corporate action most likely to appear without warning.

### Q3. Is the Q1 2026 loss absorption, or is it demand?

Q1 2026: revenue **¥108m (−12.8% YoY)**, net loss **¥4.22m**, against Q1 2025's **¥16.46m profit
(+101.3%)**. Management gives three reasons, and they are all absorption reasons:

1. the automated lines came **fully into use in Q1 2026**, raising depreciation and electricity;
2. the lines were still being commissioned, so **headcount and labour cost did not fall** as
   planned — some steps were still being done manually;
3. Spring Festival meant the **scale benefit of the automated lines was not realised**.

Every one of those is temporary *if* volume arrives. None of them explains revenue being down
12.8%. And there is a prior-year distortion pushing the other way that management has also
acknowledged: Q1 2025 was flattered because the **overseas collection-bank review problem** of
2024 pushed a large receivable catch-up into Q1 2025 — which affects cash, not revenue, but is
the reason the Q1 2025 comparison base was described as high.

The capacity commitment makes this question expensive. The Zengcheng park is **160,000 m²** in
operation with a **second phase of 80,000+ m² under construction, designed for ¥2.0bn of output**
— **3.5x current revenue.** Depreciation on that does not wait for demand.

**Ask:** **capacity utilisation by product line for 2025 and H1 2026**, and the utilisation
required for the automated lines to be gross-margin accretive; the **incremental annual
depreciation** from the lines entering service in Q1 2026 and from phase two; **headcount at
end-2025 vs end-Q2 2026** and the labour cost saving actually banked; and **monthly revenue
through Q2 2026**, since the whole management case is that Q1 was a commissioning quarter.

### Q4. Does management's own guidance survive contact with its own results?

This is a credibility question, and there are four independent readings on file.

**(a) The 2026 guidance.** Management has publicly framed 2026 as expecting **revenue growth
above 30%**, with **new products above 25% of revenue**, off a 2025 that grew **0.95%** and a Q1
2026 that fell **12.8%**. To hit +30% for the year from a −12.8% Q1, Q2–Q4 must average roughly
**+47%**. That is not a stretch target; it is a different company.

**(b) The 2024 annual report drew a regulatory enquiry letter.** The SSE STAR market department
issued 上证科创公函【2025】0242号 on the 2024 annual report; the reply was published 5 July 2025.
The subject matter was the combination of **high overseas revenue share, receivables growing
faster than revenue, and large uncollected balances (挂账)** — caused, the company said, by its
overseas collection banks changing their review procedures. The company reported top-five
customer collection at **97.25% by 31 May 2025**, and 2025 OCF of ¥192m (+255%) is consistent
with that catch-up having landed. **The issue looks resolved. That it happened at all is the
information** — and it is the reason Q2 below matters.

**(c) The 2025 annual report was delayed**, from 23 April to 30 April 2026, "to further improve
the preparation and review work" — routine language, procedurally ordinary, and worth exactly one
line unless something else in this list rhymes with it.

**(d) An unexplained −36.23%.** The 2025 annual report states net profit to parent of **¥48.24m,
down 36.23%**. But ¥48.24m against the **reported** 2024 figure of ¥72.19m is **−33.2%**. A
−36.23% decline implies a 2024 base of **~¥75.6m**. The most likely explanation is benign and
mechanical: the **70% of 洁科膜 acquired from the controlling shareholder in December 2025 is a
business combination under common control**, which requires retrospective restatement of the
comparative period. If so, the 2024 comparative was restated up by ~¥3.4m and every "2024" number
in circulation is the pre-restatement one. **This is a hypothesis, not a finding — confirm it,
because if it is not the explanation, something else moved the base.**

**Ask:** the **restated 2024 comparatives** and the reconciliation to the originally reported
figures; whether the ">30% revenue growth" framing is **formal guidance or aspiration**, and what
it assumes for overseas; and the **2026 H1 numbers against that guidance**.

### Q5. What has to be true for ~¥1.93bn to be cheap?

Reverse the valuation rather than forecasting into it. Here the honest answer is more interesting
than the bear case, because the multiple is **not** the problem.

At a terminal **25x** — defensible for a consumables manufacturer with real repeat revenue, a
41% gross margin and a genuine domestic tailwind — ¥1.93bn requires **~¥77m of net profit.**
That is:

- **1.6x** FY2025's ¥48.2m,
- **2.8x** trailing-twelve-month ~¥27.6m,
- **still 55% below** the 2021 peak of ¥171m,
- and **below** the sole broker's 2026E of ¥82m.

So unlike most import-substitution stories, **this one does not require a heroic number.** ¥77m
is a level the company has already exceeded twice (2020, 2021) and approached once (2024, ¥72m).
The bridge exists in principle.

The problem is that the bridge has to be crossed **twice**, and the second crossing is the one
nobody models:

> **The equity has to earn ~¥77m sustainably, AND the company has to fund ¥484m in June 2028.**
> Cumulative 2026–28 operating cash flow at 2024's ~¥54m run rate is ~¥160m; at 2025's one-off-
> flattered ¥192m it is ~¥575m but that number is not repeatable. Against that sit second-phase
> construction, a ¥10.4m dividend, and a ¥15–30m buyback. **If operating cash flow does not
> roughly triple and hold, the ¥484m is funded by equity or by bank debt** — and the equity route
> at ¥13.71 against a ¥300m/20%-of-net-assets placement authorisation already on the table is
> ~15–20% dilution at a one-year-low price.

**The question is therefore not "is 40x FY25 too expensive." It is: do you believe ¥77m of
normalised profit is reachable from a base of two consecutive loss-making quarters, in a business
where the larger and more profitable half is shrinking under a 35% tariff — and if it is, does
the shareholder keep it, or does it go to redeeming a bond that was sold as equity?**

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — The controlling shareholder has been a persistent seller for five years, and just sold the
bond too.** 袁建华 founded the business in 2001, listed it on the STAR Market in December 2019,
and holds **29.26%** directly, with actual controllers and concert parties at **44.37%**. The
selling history is long: in **March 2021**, at the top of the COVID cycle, 5%+ holders 广州海汇财富
创业投资企业 and **李明志** — Yuan's earliest business partner, who with affiliates had put ¥30m
in for 24% in 2011 and was sitting on ~¥936m, more than **30x** — filed to sell up to 5% of the
company, ~¥296m at the then price. There is also a **chairman's own block-trade sale plan** on
file, and a further **shareholder reduction plan filed June 2025**. Then, on **7 January 2026**,
Yuan sold **200,000 convertible bonds (¥20m face, 4.55% of the issue) by block trade**, taking his
combined interest from **29.26% to 27.86%** and the controllers' combined interest from **44.37%
to 42.05%**. **A second filing of the same type appears dated 31 July 2026** — I could not open it
to establish whether it is a new sale or a re-publication of the January event, and **that is a
question, not a finding.** Note what the January sale means in context: the founder sold the
convertible **twelve months before the company chose to keep the bond outstanding rather than
reset it.** Ask what his current bond and share holdings are, and whether any further disposal
plan is live.

**Q7 — The company bought a business from its own controlling family, and it probably restated
the accounts.** On 29 December 2025 the board approved buying **70% of 广东洁科膜分离技术** for
**¥7,919,870** from 广州拓展投资管理, **袁建华, Yuan Ye James and Dannie Yuan** — an explicit
related-party transaction, within board authority, no shareholder vote. Registration completed and
the target consolidated by early March 2026. The **size is trivial** — ¥7.9m is 0.4% of market cap
— and membrane separation is at least adjacent to a company originally named "生物过滤"
(bio-filtration). Two things nonetheless deserve answers. First, **the accounting consequence is
not trivial**: a common-control combination restates comparatives, which is the leading candidate
explanation for the −36.23%/−33.2% discrepancy in Q4 above. Second, **the direction of travel is
the family selling assets into the listed company** rather than the reverse, at a price set by a
board on which they sit. **Ask for the valuation basis, 洁科膜's revenue and profit for 2024–25,
who recused, and whether further family-held assets are candidates for injection.**

**Q8 — The 蓝勃生物 impairment is 55% of the year's profit decline and nobody has explained the
asset.** On 27–28 February 2026 the company announced a **¥26,301,158.21** impairment against its
long-term equity investment in associate **广州蓝勃生物科技**, driving asset impairment losses up
**145.39%** and accounting for most of the gap between 2025's ¥48.2m and 2024's ¥72.2m. Lanbo is
an IVD/diagnostics-adjacent investment — precisely the sort of COVID-era adjacency that has
impaired across the sector. **Ask: what was the original cost, what is the remaining carrying
value, what is Jiete's stake, what is Lanbo's current revenue and profit, and is the remaining
balance at risk?** A second write-down of similar size would consume half of 2026's consensus.

**Q9 — 2025's spectacular cash flow is a one-off and the market is treating it as a trend.**
Operating cash flow of **¥191.76m (+255.43%)** on **¥48.2m** of net profit is a 398% conversion
rate that would be remarkable if it were operating performance. It is not: the company states
plainly that it is the recovery of receivables stranded by the **2024 overseas collection-bank
review problem** — the same issue that drew the SSE enquiry letter. The implied 2024 OCF is
**~¥54m**, which is the more honest base rate. **Normalised operating cash flow for this business
is therefore somewhere between ¥54m and ¥100m, not ¥192m** — and that is the number that has to
service the 2028 redemption. **Ask for OCF excluding the catch-up, receivable days for 2024, 2025
and H1 2026, and 合同负债 (contract liabilities), which I could not obtain and which is the
cleanest public read on forward order cover.**

**Q10 — Capital is being returned and committed simultaneously, ahead of a ¥484m bill.** The 2025
distribution is **¥0.74 per 10 shares** (~¥10.4m, a 22% payout), with cumulative three-year cash
dividends stated at **over 55%** of profit. The buyback, approved 29 December 2025 for
**¥15–30m**, had spent **¥23.60m on 1.42m shares (1.01%)** by 13 May 2026 at prices between
**¥14.71 and ¥18.43** — i.e. above the June price of ¥13.71, so the buyback is currently
underwater — for a future ESOP or incentive plan that **does not yet exist**. Simultaneously the
board asked shareholders on 28 April 2026 to authorise a **simplified-procedure placement of up to
¥300m and no more than 20% of prior year-end net assets** (~¥207m on derived equity), valid to the
2026 AGM. **Returning ~¥34m while pre-authorising a ~¥207m raise and carrying a ¥484m 2028
redemption is a coherent policy only under one of two assumptions** — that profit inflects hard,
or that the placement is the redemption plan. **Ask which.**

**Q11 — There is no equity incentive plan with targets, and that removes the best available check
on management's forecast.** The buyback is explicitly for a *future* employee share ownership or
incentive plan. Until that plan is published, there is **no management-set, externally scored
performance target** anywhere in the file — which is precisely the disclosure that, on other names
in this format, has proved the single most useful discriminator between what management says
publicly and what it believes. **When the plan lands, its performance conditions are worth more
than the broker forecast.** If a plan is announced whose 2026–27 targets are materially below
"+30% revenue", that gap is the answer to Q4.

**Q12 — Competition: 40+ players, imports still hold ~60%, and the localisation tailwind is real
but is not Jiete's alone.** In the domestic lab consumables and equipment market, **imported
brands hold roughly 60% share**, with **more than 40 significant vendors** including **Thermo
Fisher, VWR, Corning, Cytiva (思拓凡) and Sartorius** alongside Jiete. The localisation trend is
genuine and accelerating — tariff retaliation and supply-security concerns have measurably reduced
end-user willingness to buy imported product, and Jiete's domestic revenue **+18.21%** in a flat
year is the evidence. But every domestic competitor is pointing at the same 60%, the products are
commodity injection-moulded plastics differentiated mainly by surface treatment and validation
data, and Jiete's own disclosure shows **own-brand gross margins (42.9% overseas, 39.3% domestic)
are not above ODM's 44.2%.** That last point deserves emphasis: **the strategic shift to own-brand
is not, so far, a margin upgrade.** Ask for own-brand vs ODM revenue and gross margin for 2024,
2025 and H1 2026, and for domestic market share by product category.

**Q13 — Coverage is one broker, and that is itself a risk factor.** A single institution appears
to carry a published 2026–27 forecast (¥82m / ¥95m). One estimate is not a consensus; it cannot be
dispersion-checked, and a single downgrade is a 100% change in "consensus". Institutional research
activity on the name is thin — the November 2025 record notes **one institution** attending. With
a ~¥1.9bn market cap, a putback-triggered convertible and two loss quarters in the last three, low
coverage means price can move a long way before anyone publishes a revision.

**Q14 — Source quality and disclosure gaps.** Primary filings on the SSE STAR disclosure site,
cninfo, dfcfw, cnstock, cfi.net.cn, xueqiu, jisilu, 10jqka, sina and the rating agency's own site
were **all unreachable from this environment**; every figure here comes from secondary coverage of
those filings. Specific items I could not verify and which therefore become questions: **year-end
2025 cash, total and interest-bearing debt, and net assets** (I have Q3 2025 cash of ¥438m and
mid-2025 equity of ¥1,234.57m, and derive the rest); **合同负债**; **FY2025 top-five customer
concentration**; **capacity utilisation**; **the exact current conversion price after the 23 July
2026 adjustment**; **the outstanding bond balance today**; **the 31 July 2026 controller filing**;
and **any share price after 12 June 2026** — the market data in this memo is two months stale, and
in a name whose central issue is a bond putback, that is a material limitation. One secondary
source reports a −80.88% sequential revenue decline in Q1 2026 that fails arithmetic against the
company's own quarterly progression; **disregard it.**

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **The putback declaration result (late August 2026).** The nearest hard number in the file.
   **How much of the ¥440m is actually put back** settles Q1 empirically. A small putback confirms
   the maturity-value reading and moves the problem to 2028; a large one means holders doubt the
   2028 credit, and that is a different investment entirely.
2. **The H1 2026 interim report (due late August).** Answers Q3 and Q4 together. Look for: Q2
   revenue and whether it recovered from Q1's −12.8%; **capacity utilisation**; the incremental
   depreciation number; overseas vs domestic split and overseas gross margin under a full period
   of tariffs; **合同负债**; receivable days; and **unrestricted cash**.
3. **Whether Q2 2026 returned to profit at all.** Two consecutive quarterly losses would make the
   +70% full-year consensus arithmetically unreachable and force the question of whether 2026 is
   a loss-making year — which would in turn change the 2028 funding conversation.
4. **Any equity incentive or ESOP plan, and its performance targets.** The buyback has been
   accumulating shares for one since December 2025. Its targets are the single most informative
   forthcoming disclosure on this name.
5. **The conversion-price reset commitment expiring 8 October 2026.** The board is bound not to
   reset through that date. **What they do in Q4 2026 is the tell** on whether they intend to
   redeem the bond in cash or dilute their way out of it.
6. **Any overseas acquisition announcement** — a plant or a distributor, to establish a non-China
   origin. Management has said it is screening. This is the pending corporate action most likely
   to arrive unannounced, and the most likely use of the ¥300m placement authorisation.
7. **Whether the simplified-procedure placement is actually used**, at what price and for what
   stated purpose. Used at ~¥13–14 it is ~15% dilution at a cyclical low.
8. **US tariff policy on Chinese-origin lab consumables**, and any re-sourcing announcement by a
   major Western lab-supply distributor. Jiete's ODM book is the exposure and it is invisible
   until an order stops.
9. **The remaining carrying value of the 蓝勃生物 investment**, in the H1 report, and whether it
   takes a further write-down.
10. **Q3 2026 report (late October).** Cumulative nine-month profit materially below ~¥50m makes
    the ¥82m full-year forecast unreachable and forces a downgrade from the only broker covering.
11. **Further 袁建华 disposals**, of shares or of bonds, and clarification of the 31 July 2026
    filing.

## 4. Provisional read

**The business** is more solid than its share price suggests and less special than its narrative
claims. Disposable lab plastics are a genuinely good category: consumed, re-ordered, validated
into workflows, and structurally growing with Chinese life-science research spend. Jiete is a real
manufacturer with a real 160,000 m² plant, real automation, 41% gross margins, ¥29m of annual R&D
at 5.2% of revenue, and a domestic business that grew **18.21%** in a year when the group grew
**0.95%**. The localisation thesis is not fiction — imported brands still hold ~60% of the
domestic market and end-user willingness to buy them is measurably falling. If you want exposure
to that, this is one of the few listed pure plays.

But the story sold to investors and the business that exists are not the same shape. **The larger,
higher-margin, gross-profit-dominant half of this company is ODM export manufacturing for Western
lab-supply houses**, it is **shrinking (−8.81%)**, it faces a **~35% tariff on the US block that
is >50% of it**, and the own-brand business meant to replace it **carries lower gross margins than
the ODM work it would replace.** That is the central operating tension, and it is not resolved by
anything currently disclosed.

**The stock** is genuinely not expensive on normalised numbers — ~¥77m of profit justifies the
market cap at 25x, and this company earned ¥171m in 2021 and ¥72m as recently as 2024. What makes
it hard is that you are asked to underwrite that recovery **from two consecutive loss-making
quarters**, on **one broker's estimate**, with management guiding **+30% revenue** against a
**−12.8%** Q1, and with **¥484m coming due in June 2028** at a company whose repeatable operating
cash flow is closer to ¥54m than to the ¥192m it just reported.

**Governance is not alarming but it is not neutral either.** Control is 42%, the founder has been
a seller — of stock in 2021, of the convertible in January 2026 — the family sold an asset into
the listed company in December 2025, and the board has three times chosen a future cash liability
over present dilution. None of that is disqualifying. All of it points the same direction: the
people with the best information have been reducing, not adding.

The honest framing: **a decent business in a good category, at a reasonable price on normalised
earnings, whose two loss-making quarters, shrinking export engine and 2028 debt cliff all have to
resolve in the same 24 months — and whose management is publicly guiding to a number the first
quarter of the year already contradicted by 43 percentage points.** What would flip the
conclusion is narrow and specific: **Q2 2026 back in profit on recovering overseas revenue, with
capacity utilisation disclosed and rising**, plus **a small August putback** confirming the bond
is a 2028 problem rather than a 2026 one. Those two together convert this from a broken story into
a cyclical recovery bought at a low.

**Do not size a position before the H1 report and the putback result, both of which land within
weeks.** They are the two disclosures that separate "commissioning quarter" from "the export
business is going away", and they are free to wait for. If the H1 report shows Q2 revenue still
negative year-on-year, the ">30% growth" guidance stops being optimistic and becomes a governance
question in its own right.
