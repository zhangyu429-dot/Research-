## 0. What you are actually buying

Delong Laser is **a precision laser equipment platform with ¥787m of revenue and almost no
profit, and the market is paying more than ¥4.4bn for one of its product lines — the one that
has just received a "small-batch repeat order."** The core business is precision laser
processing equipment (73% of revenue), historically sold into display panel makers — BOE, CSOT,
Visionox. The company also makes its own lasers in-house (8% of revenue, 48% gross margin) and
sells laser processing services and parts/repair (16% combined, 51%–62% gross margin). What
supports the current share price is none of that: it is the **in-house LSD-6130 silicon wafer
laser stealth-dicing system, which in 2025 won the first domestically-made volume-production
order from a leading Chinese memory-chip maker, followed by a small-batch repeat order in early
2026.** Beyond this, management is also telling a perovskite-equipment story and a
solid-state-battery-equipment story. Any judgement on this stock is a judgement on two things:
**how big that memory order actually is and how fast it replicates; and how a company whose
profits have stepped down every year for five years is going to earn nearly twice its
all-time-best year in 2026–2027.**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1 — How big is that memory stealth-dicing order, actually?

This ranks first because **it is the only thing that explains the valuation**, and because the
company has already given an answer that is not especially bullish.

The confirmed facts are solid and should not be dismissed: the in-house **LSD-6130 silicon wafer
laser stealth-dicing system** supports both **SDBG and SDAG** processes and handles **35–85μm
ultra-thin wafers** for stealth dicing and pre-thinning; it won **the first domestically-made
volume-production order from a leading Chinese memory-chip maker in 2025 and passed the
customer's own production qualification**; and it received a **small-batch repeat order in early
2026**. Passing a top-tier memory fab's production qualification is not something a marginal
supplier achieves.

Sitting alongside that is what the company itself said in its 30 April 2026 investor Q&A:
**"capacity expansion is driving demand, but the order value is small."** And on 11 June 2026,
asked about Yangtze Memory and ChangXin specifically: **"commercial confidentiality — cannot
disclose."** The companion products — wafer laser grooving and laser marking systems — remain
**in qualification, with no orders**.

**So the public state of this product line is: one machine, one customer, one production order,
one small repeat order, with the value described by the company itself as "small."** The market
took the stock from ¥35 to ¥96 on it.

> **One piece of evidence added on 22 August, and it outweighs everything above: of the ¥300m
> being raised, the amount going to memory/semiconductor equipment capacity is zero.** 57%
> (¥170m) goes to **laser** capacity — a component business. The three frontier directions named
> for the R&D centre are **foldable-electronics structural parts, kW-class ultrafast lasers and
> SiC ingot laser slicing** — **none of them memory stealth dicing**. The remaining ~¥60m is
> working capital.
>
> This is management ranking its own opportunities with its own capital. **When a company decides
> where its next ¥300m goes and the answer is not the product the market is paying for, that
> signal is more credible than any investor-relations sentence** — it is a board resolution with
> a sponsor (China Securities) and an auditor (Reanda) attached, not an answer in a Q&A.
> **It points the same way as the company secretary's "the order value is small," and it upgrades
> that judgement from what the company says to what the company does.**

**Ask:**
- **What were the values of the 2025 production order and the early-2026 repeat order? How many
  tools? What is the ASP per tool? What share of FY2025 revenue and 1H26 revenue came from
  memory/semiconductor?** This single disclosure largely settles the whole investment case.
  If confidentiality is invoked, **ask at minimum for a range as a percentage of revenue — a
  refusal even to give a range should be priced as "no meaningful revenue yet."**
- **Is the repeat order an add-on to the same line, or a new line?** The first is replacement
  depreciation; only the second is a capacity ramp.
- **What is the acceptance cycle?** Equipment orders and revenue are separated by installation,
  commissioning and customer sign-off. **1Q26 revenue fell 11.93% YoY, which says the 2025
  orders had not become 1H26 revenue.**
- **Whom is this displacing?** The global incumbents in silicon-wafer stealth dicing are Japanese
  (laser source and dicing tool sit with different suppliers). **This is industry background, not
  something verified in this session — check it with the company or the supply chain.** The
  incumbent's share, price and lead times decide whether this is share-taking or gap-filling.
- **The memory customer's capex cadence.** Is stealth-dicing demand a **one-time install** or a
  **multi-year ramp**? The most common valuation error in equipment names is mistaking the slope
  of one install cycle for a terminal growth rate.

### Q2 — Revenue up 43%, profit down 71% — is this a spending cycle or the business model?

From 2021 to 2025 revenue went from ¥549m to ¥787m while net profit went from ¥87.7m to ¥25.8m
and net margin from 16.0% to 3.3%, with a loss in 2024. **Four years of one-way decline across
two downstream cycles is not cyclical.** Three explanations fit the data and imply very
different valuations:

1. **Spending ahead of revenue.** R&D and sales headcount plus new product lines (semiconductor,
   perovskite, solid-state battery) burn cash before revenue arrives; depreciation stepped up
   after phase one of the Jiangsu Delong site was completed. *Temporary — if the new lines
   deliver.*
2. **Impairments.** In 2024 the company took materially higher impairments on long-term equity
   investments, receivables and inventory, attributing this to intensifying competition. *If the
   impairments come from stalled acceptances and inventory write-downs, they are not one-off.*
3. **The equipment itself is deflating.** The precision laser equipment segment earned **only
   37.69%** gross margin in 2025 — against 61.61% for processing services, 51.51% for parts and
   repair, and 48.03% for lasers. **This company makes less money selling machines than selling
   services, spares or lasers.** *Terminal — it would mean equipment volume growth itself
   dilutes profitability.*

**The most awkward comparison for the bulls:** 1Q26 gross margin was **43.28%**, 2.4pp *above*
the FY2025 level of 40.88%, **and the company still lost ¥25.5m.** **Margin is not the problem;
scale and fixed cost are** — which turns the question from "can they raise prices" into "when
does revenue cover fixed costs," and only volume answers that.

> **Added 22 August, and it changes the nature of this question: FY2025 R&D expense was ¥129m,
> 16.33% of revenue — 5.0x that year's net profit of ¥25.85m.**
>
> Put that into the three explanations above and **the first (spending ahead of revenue)
> overwhelms the third (equipment deflation).** A rough calculation: take the R&D ratio back to
> 8% (~¥63m), hold everything else constant, and pre-tax profit rises by ~¥66m, putting net
> profit around **¥90m — right back at the 2021 peak of ¥87.7m.**
>
> **So this is not a business that cannot make money; it is a company spending all of its profit,
> and more, on R&D.** The implication cuts both ways. **For the bulls:** profit is being
> suppressed by choice, not by competition, and the operating leverage on any decision to
> converge R&D — or on R&D converting to revenue — is very large. **For the bears:** **a 16.33%
> R&D ratio has been sustained for years without producing one large business** — the memory
> order is "small," perovskite and solid-state orders are small, and the new-energy projects
> slipped. **The question becomes "what is the conversion rate on that ¥129m," which is the
> financial expression of Q7's "all four directions are real and all four are small."**
>
> **The first two numbers to ask for therefore change: the breakeven revenue level, and the
> five-year R&D-ratio series set against new-product revenue.**

**Ask:** the full three-year segment gross margin series, especially equipment from 2021 to 1H26.
In 2021 the company disclosed that **equipment carrying its own in-house lasers earned 56.2%
gross margin — against 37.69% for all equipment today, an 18.5pp gap. Is that falling in-house
laser penetration, or price erosion in the machines themselves?** Also: the 2024 impairment
breakdown by category; a bridge decomposing the 16.0% → 3.3% net margin decline into gross
margin / R&D / selling / admin / impairment; and above all **what revenue level is
breakeven** — the single most valuable number to get from this management.

**This table is both more forgiving and more damning than the version I wrote on 14 August.**

**More forgiving:** of the three explanations I offered, the real answer was a fourth I had not
guessed — **they could not get the land.** The two Suzhou projects have been unable to secure a
site since 2022, so the company redeployed the cash to Jiangyin and is now returning for funding
once a plot has been identified. That is procedurally coherent, and it is *not* "rotating from a
story that did not work to the next one." **Half of my "why raise while idle" objection is
answered by that single fact.**

**More damning, on three counts:**

1. **The land this ¥170m is meant to buy is described in the filing as "to be newly purchased" —
   i.e. as of the offering plan, still not bought.** The same management has spent four years
   looking for a site in the same city for the same project, and now wants shareholders to pay
   first, then buy the land, then build for two years. **Execution risk on these projects is not
   hypothetical; it has already happened once.**
2. **The substitute is late too.** The two new-energy projects that took the ¥175.93m are, three
   years on, only 50.7% and 64% complete and have just been pushed from May to December 2026 —
   while the Jiangyin new-energy programme signed with the local development zone in March 2023
   carried a headline investment of **¥1.08bn for a 420-unit-a-year line**. **The line in the 1Q26
   report about "higher depreciation and amortisation after completion of phase one civil works at
   Jiangsu Delong" is this project's clearest contribution to the income statement so far — it has
   delivered depreciation before it has delivered revenue.**
3. **The ¥264m over-raise is being swept into working capital.** ¥26.65m in April 2025 alone, with
   the filing's wording implying more in the preceding twelve months. **Over-raised expansion
   capital ending its life as "permanent working capital" is the plainest available reading of
   capital-allocation discipline.**

**The arithmetic of it: a company that could not deploy ¥713.82m in four years is applying for
another ¥300m.** And on a two-year build, **the ¥170m produces no revenue before 2028.**

> **An important correction from the second pass: operations are not burning cash, and my implicit
> assumption was wrong.** FY2025 operating cash flow was **+¥49.56m**, a **192% conversion** on
> ¥25.85m of net profit, against **−¥48.58m** in FY2024 — a ~¥98m swing in one year. Alongside it:
> **short-term borrowings down 66.67%**, **current ratio 2.66 / quick ratio 1.70**, and
> **inventory down ¥43.52m**.
>
> **"Working capital" is therefore unlikely to mean the company is short of cash to operate.**
> That materially weakens the third explanation in this question and leaves the weight on the
> first two — restricted use of proceeds, and project execution. **This is a correction in the
> company's favour, and it simultaneously raises the force of a different question: if operating
> cash flow is positive and the balance sheet is clean, why dilute shareholders now?**
>
> **But the same data carries one clear negative: at end-2025 contract liabilities were down
> 30.63% YoY, with their share of total assets down 2.66pp.** For an equipment company, contract
> liabilities are the cleanest externally available proxy for backlog. **They fell in 2025** —
> the very year the company won the memory production order and was telling the market its
> downstream markets were "blooming across the board." **That runs opposite to the "new orders
> growing quickly" line and should be the core follow-up in Q1 and Q4.**

**The termination still needs its own explanation.** An offering already priced with six
subscribers named (Nord Fund, Ningquan Asset, Caitong Fund among them) was voided because **an
intermediary did not meet the eligibility conditions** — procedurally possible, but it means
**issuer and sponsor did not check a basic qualification before launching.** That is an
observation about internal control and execution.

**Ask:** whether the Xinglong Street plot has actually been won at auction and the land grant
contract signed; the projects' **projected revenue, incremental profit and payback** (these belong
in the feasibility study, not obtained here); **the projected revenue attached to 2,500 lasers a
year** — which must be read against the FY2025 laser segment revenue of **¥62.59m**; the basis for
the ¥60m working-capital figure; and whether the two Jiangyin projects slip **a second time** in
December 2026. Still outstanding from before: the direction of **contract liabilities**,
**receivables ageing and inventory composition** (especially goods shipped pending acceptance),
and committed 2026–2027 capex against forecast operating cash flow.

explanation, not a neutral fact.**

### Q5 — What has to be true for a ¥4.5–5.0bn market cap?

Do not forecast into it. **Reverse it.**

| Terminal multiple | Required at ¥4.46bn | Required at ¥4.99bn | Multiple of FY2025 (¥25.8m) | Multiple of 2021 peak (¥87.7m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **30x** (through-cycle equipment) | ¥149m | ¥166m | **5.8–6.4x** | **1.7–1.9x** |
| **40x** (semi-cap in an up-cycle) | ¥112m | ¥125m | 4.3–4.8x | 1.3–1.4x |
| **50x** (only holds at the hottest moment) | ¥89m | ¥100m | 3.4–3.9x | 1.0–1.1x |

The row worth stopping on is the last one: **at 50x — a multiple that only survives at the peak
of a semi-cap cycle — today's price equals Guosheng's FY2026 estimate of ¥90m delivered in full,
this year.** In other words, **the market is not pricing "2026 delivers." It is pricing "2026
delivers and the multiple never compresses."** At 30x, closer to a through-cycle level, you need
¥149–166m — **the 2027 consensus delivered in full, undiscounted.**

Then the near-term arithmetic, which is where it gets uncomfortable:

> 1Q26 net profit to parent was **−¥25.5m**. Backing out from the reported "−55.01% YoY," 1Q25
> was about **−¥16.5m**, against **+¥25.8m** for FY2025 — so 2Q–4Q25 delivered roughly **+¥42.3m**.
>

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Control is thin, which inverts the usual A-share risk.** Zhao Yuxing is chairman, general
manager and legal representative at once, so supervision and execution are not separated; but
after selling he holds only about **21%**, against 9.99% for Beijing Woyan and 62.70% for the top
ten combined. This is the opposite of most family-controlled A-share manufacturers: **the risk
here is not that minorities cannot influence anything, but that control itself is fragile** — in
a company planning consecutive raises while its controlling shareholder sells, 21% plus a ¥300m
placement (≈6–7% dilution at the current price) keeps diluting. **Ask whether the controlling
shareholder is subscribing to this placement, whether there are concert-party arrangements, and
whether any shares are pledged.** None of the three was obtainable here.

**Q7 — Four stories, ¥787m of revenue and ¥25.8m of profit.** Display panel (BOE, CSOT, Visionox
— the base, demand flat), semiconductor (memory stealth dicing — the new story, order value
"small"), perovskite equipment, solid-state battery equipment. The three broker titles are
"ultrafast laser platform gorilla, downstream applications blooming across the board"
(Guosheng), "memory chip equipment set to ramp" (Aijian), and "memory chip equipment set to ramp,
perovskite + solid-state battery equipment a broad space" (initiation, March 2026). **In capital
equipment, "platform" and "blooming across the board" are usually two ways of saying the same
thing: R&D is spread thin across directions that have not been validated.** That is the most
natural explanation for net margin going from 16.0% to 3.3%. **Ask for backlog and R&D spend by
direction**, and translate "platform" back into the measurable number, which is the R&D expense
ratio.

**Correction, 22 August — calling these "stories" was too strong.** On further checking:
**perovskite** — the company has a second-generation integrated tool doing P1/P2/P3 scribing and
P4 edge cleaning, **with orders from multiple customers and units running in production lines**;
**solid-state battery** — the company is described as having a first-mover position in **laser
scribing/insulation of electrodes**, with **volume orders from several leading customers** and
entry into multiple customers' all-solid-state pilot supply chains. **These are not vapour; they
are real product lines carrying orders.** The correct criticism is therefore sharper and harder
to rebut: **all four directions are real, and all four are small.** A company that has won orders
in memory stealth dicing, perovskite and solid-state battery equipment and still produces only
¥787m of revenue and ¥25.8m of profit does not have a credibility problem in its technology —
**it has the problem that no single direction is yet large enough to move the income statement,
which is exactly where Q1 and Q2 meet.**

**Q8 — 37.69% equipment gross margin is the most-overlooked number here.** Across four business
lines, selling machines is the least profitable. That is the wrong shape for high-end equipment
import substitution: if the stealth-dicing tool really is replacing expensive Japanese systems,
and is the only domestic tool qualified at a top-tier memory fab, **the new product should carry
a materially higher margin and pull the segment up.** It did not in 2025. Two explanations: the
new revenue is too small to show in segment data — **consistent with "the order value is small,"
and bad news, because it means what is in the valuation is not yet in the accounts** — or legacy
display-panel equipment is deflating in a price war, also bad news. **A gross margin split by
product and by end market is the only disclosure that settles it.**

**Q9 — The vertical integration is real, but needs quantifying.** In-house lasers are this
company's genuine differentiator: the segment earned ¥62.59m at 48.03% gross margin, and in 2021
the company disclosed that **equipment carrying its own lasers earned 56.2%.** ¥170m of the ¥300m
placement goes to laser capacity. **What is unknowable from outside is the penetration rate of
in-house lasers in its own machines.** If penetration is already high and equipment margin is
still 37.69%, the integration benefit has been competed away downstream; if penetration is still
low, the ¥170m expansion makes sense and is a real path to margin recovery. **This is the one
concrete lead in this memo pointing to the company being undervalued, which is why it is worth
checking first.**

**Added 22 August: the placement filings make this lead measurable, and it matters more than I
thought.** At full ramp the project produces **2,500 lasers a year** (1,000 ns, 1,000 ps, 300 fs,
50 tunable-pulse-width, 100 semiconductor, 50 fibre) against a FY2025 laser segment of **just
¥62.59m** — **an order-of-magnitude expansion, not a top-up.** Two things follow:

- **The growth engine management is backing is lasers — components — not the memory
  stealth-dicing tool.** ¥170m of the ¥300m sits here. **That is a different investment case from
  the one the market is pricing**: a component supplier is valued differently, sells to different
  customers, and competes with different companies (Raycus, Maxphotonics, IPG, and equipment peers
  who also build their own lasers) than a semiconductor-equipment localisation play.
- **A two-year build means this money produces no revenue before 2028**, while diluting from the
  moment it is raised. **Any reading of this placement as good news has to answer that timing gap
  first.**

**So the first number to ask for in this question changes: what revenue does the company's own
model attach to 2,500 units of capacity?** If the answer is ¥300–400m, the laser business goes
from 8% of revenue to more than a third — **a change in what the company is, which deserves to be
priced separately and doubted separately.**

**Q10 — Where in the cycle is 2026, and what is normalised profit?** One full cycle has already
played out: peak in 2021 (¥87.7m), decline through 2022–2023 (¥67.4m, ¥39.1m), a loss in 2024
(−¥34.5m), a bare recovery in 2025 (¥25.8m). **The five-year average is about ¥36m.** If mid-cycle
is ¥60–90m — i.e. back to 2021–2022 levels — the current price implies a **50–83x normalised
P/E**. Whether memory-equipment localisation permanently lifts mid-cycle above ¥150m is the whole
disagreement.

**Q11 — Share supply is quantifiable and currently unhelpful.** Four things at once: (a) the
April 2025 expiry released 23.745m shares (22.97% of the total), roughly a 50% increase in free
float; (b) the controlling shareholder has sold 1.82% for ~¥61m, **his remaining 21.15% is fully
saleable, and he is not subscribing to this placement**; (c) the ¥300m placement in review is
authorised for **up to 31.008m shares (30.00% of pre-deal share capital)**, though the ¥300m
proceeds cap means actual dilution at ¥40–50 is ~6–7% (**30% is the authorisation ceiling, not the
expected dilution**), and the new subscribers become natural supply once locked shares free up; (d) the 350,000 buyback shares from
2024 remain unassigned. **Taken together: the best-informed holders have been realising, not
adding, and more supply is on the way.**

**Q12 — Related-party transactions and off-balance-sheet investing: size unknown, direction worth
watching.** In January 2026 the company invested in a newly formed private fund (Jiangsu Nantong
Yuanhe Chongyuan strategic emerging industries equity fund, alongside a Nantong
next-generation-IT industry fund of funds), **which constituted a related-party transaction**,
approved by a dedicated independent-directors meeting; **the company's commitment amount could
not be obtained.** There is also a 28 April 2026 announcement of expected FY2026 ordinary
related-party transactions (2026-010), **again with neither amounts nor counterparties
obtained**. For a company earning ¥25.8m while applying to raise ¥300m, **putting its own cash
into a government-guidance-fund-backed PE vehicle needs both a strategic rationale and a
number.** If the amount is material against profit, this moves up out of the second-order list.

**Q13 — Source quality and disclosure gaps.** In this environment, **every primary disclosure
site and PDF host — the SSE, cninfo, Eastmoney/dfcfw, Sina Finance, Xueqiu, THS, Sohu, Tencent
Finance — is blocked by the network egress proxy**, and all figures here come from search-engine
summaries of those filings. Gaps that convert directly into questions: **the full FY2025 balance
sheet and cash flow statement (operating cash flow, receivables, inventory, contract liabilities,
goods shipped, cash, interest-bearing debt), R&D spend and R&D ratio, top-five customer
concentration, 1H26 results, the memory order value, the PE fund commitment, related-party
transaction amounts, controlling-shareholder pledges, and an accurate close for a specified
trading day.** **Operating cash flow and contract liabilities are the two that matter most** —
for an equipment company they answer "is the profit real" and "is the backlog real," which are
precisely the first two questions in this memo.

**Updated 22 August, second pass:** following the prospectus filled in **operating cash flow, R&D
expense and ratio, inventory, the direction of contract liabilities, short-term borrowings, current

## 4. Provisional read

**The business** is better than its accounts. In-house lasers are genuine differentiation, and
the 48% laser segment margin corroborates the older disclosure that equipment carrying in-house
lasers earned 56.2%. Passing a top-tier domestic memory fab's production qualification, winning
the first domestically-made volume order and then a repeat order is not marketing language —
customers do not casually admit a new supplier to SDBG/SDAG at 35–85μm. The display-panel base
provides real cash and a real customer list (BOE, CSOT, Visionox), and years inside HiSilicon's
supply chain is evidence too. 1Q26 gross margin of 43.28% says the products are not in a price
war.

**After the second pass, this paragraph has to be revised upward, and not slightly.** Three numbers:
**FY2025 operating cash flow of +¥49.56m (192% conversion on net profit), a ~¥98m improvement on
FY2024's −¥48.58m; short-term borrowings down 66.67%, current ratio 2.66 and quick ratio 1.70; and
R&D of ¥129m, 16.33% of revenue and 5.0x that year's net profit.** Together they say: **this is not
a company that cannot make money — it is a company reinvesting all of its profit into R&D, and its
operating cash flow and balance sheet can carry that.** Take the R&D ratio back to 8% and profit
returns to roughly the 2021 peak. **This is the part I underweighted on 14 August.**

**The stock** is a much harder question. You are buying at roughly **173–193x trailing earnings**
and **702–786x trailing earnings ex-exceptionals**: a company whose **FY2025 profit
ex-exceptionals was ¥6.35m**; whose **profit peak sits in 2021 and has not been matched in five
years, with a loss year in between**; whose **1Q26 revenue fell 12% and which lost ¥25.5m**;
where **consensus needs 2Q–4Q at 3.9x last year**; and whose forecasters, the last time they
projected two years out, called **−¥34.5m** at **¥206m**. **Even if this company returned
tomorrow to its best-ever year, at 30x that is ~¥2.6bn — about 45% below today.**

**But the same data carries one new negative, and it lands on the most sensitive spot: at end-2025
contract liabilities were down 30.63% YoY, their share of total assets down 2.66pp.** Contract
liabilities are the cleanest external proxy for an equipment company's backlog, and the year they
fell is the year the company won the memory production order and told the market its end markets
were blooming. **The technology is real, the cash flow is real, the R&D spend is real — but as of
the latest annual report, the orders have not grown in the accounts.** That is where the entire
disagreement lands.

**After the 22 August revision the direction of the conclusion is unchanged, but its centre of
gravity has moved.** On 14 August I put the fulcrum on "how big is the memory order." Having read
the placement filings, **the fulcrum is a more fundamental question: how well does this company use
shareholders' money?** The answer is on the record and it is not good — ¥713.82m net at the 2022
IPO (including a ¥263.82m over-raise); four years on, the two Suzhou projects it was raised for are
**unbuilt because the company could never obtain the land**; ¥175.93m was redirected to Jiangyin;
the Jiangyin projects are 50.7% / 64% complete and have slipped again; the over-raise is being swept
permanently into working capital; the residual sits in wealth-management products; **and the company
is now raising ¥300m to do the original two projects, ¥170m of it to buy a plot it has not yet bought
and then build for two years.** **That is not a record explicable by "the cycle" or "downstream
demand."**

Equally important is where the ¥300m goes: **nothing to memory or semiconductor equipment capacity.**
57% goes to laser-component capacity — an order-of-magnitude expansion to 2,500 units a year against
a segment that did ¥62.59m in 2025 — and the R&D centre names foldable electronics, kW-class
ultrafast lasers and SiC ingot slicing. **Management is voting ¥300m in a different direction from
the one the market is pricing at ¥5bn.** I would weight management's vote more heavily: it is a board
resolution with a sponsor and an auditor attached, not a line in a Q&A.

**Governance does not decide the conclusion, but it tilts the scale visibly.** The controlling
shareholder started selling within four months of his lock-up expiring, **sold 300,000 shares
during a window in which the exchange had flagged the stock for abnormal volatility**, and
realised ~¥61m between ¥29 and ¥47 — six months before the stock traded at ¥96. Meanwhile the
¥10.06m of stock the company repurchased in 2024 sits in treasury with no incentive plan after
two years. Layer on the eighteen-month financing sequence — **idle proceeds into wealth-management
products, funded projects delayed, a fully-priced placement voided over an intermediary's
eligibility, then an immediate pivot to a ¥300m raise with "working capital" newly added** — and
**this is not the pattern of a cash-rich, backlog-full company being discovered by the market.**

**Three corrections in the company's favour, all from this revision, belong here too:** management's
own guidance is accurate (FY2025 guided ¥21m–30m attributable / ¥5.0m–7.5m ex-exceptionals, delivered
¥25.85m / ¥6.35m — both mid-range, so my 14 August inference of systematic over-forecasting is
withdrawn); perovskite and solid-state battery are order-bearing product lines, not stories; and the
direct cause of four years of poor deployment was **an inability to obtain land**, not strategic
drift — which is more benign than "rotating from one failed story to the next." **None of this flips
the conclusion, but together they are the reason this company deserves continued tracking rather than
outright exclusion.**

The honest statement: **several real technologies, a real localisation breakthrough, several real but
uniformly small orders, a four-year record of failing to deploy ¥713.82m, and a market cap already
priced for ¥150–170m of profit.** What would flip
this is narrow and specific: **the interim report (or an IR record) disclosing the revenue amount
or share from semiconductor/memory, contract liabilities up sharply YoY, and 1H26 back in
profit.** All three together rewrite the story from "a small order was talked into ¥4.5bn" to
"the localisation revenue curve has begun" — **worth far more than any order headline or broker
title.**

**Do not build a position before the interim report** — the statutory deadline is 31 August and as
of 22 August it has not appeared. It can answer more
than most interim reports can: contract liabilities answer the backlog, operating cash flow
answers earnings quality, segment gross margin answers the business model, and 1H profit or loss
answers whether the full-year number stands. **The second document to read is the SSE's comment
letter on this placement**, which will force the company to answer in public the parts of Q3 and
Q9 that could not be obtained here. **Between those two filings, more than half of the open
questions in this memo get settled, and they may be less than two months apart.** At this valuation, **any miss is a re-rating, not an
earnings disappointment** — the stock demonstrated exactly that between May and July 2026, going
from ¥96.21 to ¥48.26 in six weeks.
