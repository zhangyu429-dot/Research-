# 洁美科技 (Jiemei, 002859.SZ) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 12 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Jiemei is a **dominant, structurally deflating cash cow funding three capital-hungry options
with debt and equity**. The cow is paper carrier tape — roughly 70% claimed global share, 83%
of revenue, and a gross margin that has fallen every year for five years. Option one is MLCC
release film, currently in the best demand environment it will ever see. Option two is
composite current collectors (柔震科技, acquired Dec 2024), pre-commercial and loss-making.
Option three was announced in March 2026 and is still pending: a **¥915m all-share acquisition
of 埃福思科技, an ion-beam polishing equipment maker for lithography optics** — a business with
no operational connection to anything Jiemei does. Any view on the stock is a view on whether
the options arrive before the cow finishes melting, and on whether this management should be
allocating capital across four unrelated industries at once.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. Is the margin decline mix, price, or structure — and does release film actually reverse it?

This outranks the growth story, because five consecutive years of gross margin erosion
through both an up-cycle and a down-cycle is not a cyclical signature. Three explanations
fit the data and they have completely different valuations attached:

1. **Mix.** Low-margin new products diluting a high-margin core. **Now strongly supported by
   the data — but with the opposite meaning to the bull case.** See the box below. *Not benign.*
2. **Depreciation absorption.** Jiangxi, Guangdong, Tianjin and BOPET lines depreciating before
   they produce. *Temporary, but only if utilisation actually arrives.*
3. **Price.** Carrier tape ASP deflating structurally as the product commoditises.
   *Terminal — and it would mean the 70% share is share without pricing power.*

The single most damaging datapoint for the bull case: **Q1 2026 gross margin of 32.59% is
below FY2025's 33.41%**, in a quarter where management says core products were at full
production and full sales *and* release-film prices were being raised.

> **Revised after the industry deep dive (see [mlcc-film-deepdive.zh.md](mlcc-film-deepdive.zh.md)):**
> **In 2018–2021 the release film business ran at a 9%–15% gross margin, against 34%–43% for
> paper carrier tape and 35%–45% for cover tape.** Run the 2025 mix: 82.8% × 36% + 12.4% × 15%
> + 4.8% × low ≈ **33.2%**, against an actual blended margin of **33.41%** — a close fit.
>
> **Release film is not lifting profitability. It is diluting it.** Every point of revenue mix
> shifting to film costs roughly 0.2pp of blended gross margin. That also explains the Q1 2026
> print directly: **film volume is growing over 100% at a lower margin than the core, so the
> ramp itself pushes the blended margin down.**
>
> **The necessary hedge:** 9%–15% dates from when the business was sub-scale and buying base
> film externally. The company has since integrated backwards into its own BOPET, stated
> publicly that film margins will keep rising, raised prices 5–18%, and shifted to mid/high-end.
> Current margin should be materially above 15%. **Nobody outside knows by how much — and that
> is the pivot of the entire investment case.**

**So the question changes character: not "when does the margin recover" but "can it."** If the
film segment is already at 30%+, the bull case works. If it is still below 20%, **the faster it
ramps, the worse the blended margin looks.**

**Ask:**
- **Gross margin by segment** — 电子封装材料 vs 电子级薄膜材料, for FY2024, FY2025 and H1 2026.
  This one disclosure settles the question. If it is not broken out, that absence is itself
  information.
- **Carrier tape ASP vs volume** for the last three years. Volume up and ASP down is the
  terminal case.
- Decompose the 2026 margin move into **price / mix / capacity absorption**. Price and mix in
  a shortage reverse; absorption does not.
- How much **depreciation on not-yet-utilised assets** hits 2026–27? Tianjin phase 1 only
  entered trial production in H1 2026 and phase 2 has already started construction.

### Q2. Is the 2026 release-film surge a *cycle* or a *franchise*?

The demand shock is real and verifiable. Murata / Samsung Electro-Mechanics / Taiyo Yuden
are running >95% utilisation with book-to-bill of 1.25–1.31; AI high-capacitance lead times
have gone from 8–12 to 16–24 weeks; MLCC prices are up 15–35% (50%+ on AI parts). Jiemei's
own July 2026 investor records say release-film **volume is up more than 100% YoY since
March**, Tianjin's first two lines are in trial production, and management expects
**shortage conditions to persist through H1 2027**. Prices on high-end grades were raised
5–18% in April–May. Monthly shipments passed **27m m² in April 2026** against FY2025 film
revenue of only ¥260m.

Management is also deliberately **exiting low-end grades to concentrate on mid/high-end** —
the right decision, and one that makes segment-margin disclosure (Q1) more informative, not less.

Against that, the announced domestic capacity is the problem:

| Player | MLCC release film capacity | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **洁美科技** | 400m m²/yr design; Tianjin ph.2 adds 240m m² + 20kt BOPET | Fully integrated: own BOPET base film → silicone coating |
| **双星新材 (002585)** | ph.1 500m m² full by end-2026; ph.2 500m m² building; **2.0bn m² long-term** | Claims #1 domestic share (~33%) |
| **斯迪克 (300806)** | ~30m m² → 100m m² in 2026 | Ultra-thin, ≤50nm smoothness, 800+ layer |
| **东材科技** | ~32m m² | Mid-end |
| **康辉新材** (Hengli Petrochemical) | Base film: high-smooth in production, ultra-smooth small batch | Petrochemical-backed potential entrant |

> **Revised after the deep dive: the earlier framing overstated 双星's near-term threat.**
> 双星's MLCC release film was **~0.3% of its 2025 revenue and is currently gross-margin
> negative**, with the company describing it as "still in the cultivation and construction
> phase." That implies roughly **¥15m of revenue — Jiemei's ¥260m is ~17x larger.** On
> customers, Jiemei is in **batch supply** at Samsung, Murata and Taiyo Yuden while 双星 is
> still at **validation**. **Jiemei leads through 2026–27; the threat lands around 2028.**
>
> But **双星 running negative gross margin is itself a warning about the industry's
> economics** — without scale and yield, this product loses money. That corroborates Q1.
>
> **Also note the TAM is uncertain by an order of magnitude.** The widely cited "15.58bn m²
> global demand in 2024" fails a materials balance: at 25μm it implies ~545kt of PET film,
> 11–14% of all global BOPET output and all of it at the highest cleanliness grade. Bottom-up
> from MLCC unit output gives only **1.0–1.5bn m²**. **If true demand is ~3bn m², 双星's 2bn m²
> long-term plan alone is 67% of the world** — an unambiguous glut signal. Until the TAM is
> resolved, "60% of the substitution runway remains" is not actionable.

**Sub-questions:**
- Decompose the 2026 gain into **price / volume / mix**. A qualified socket at Samsung or
  Murata survives the cycle; a price increase taken in a shortage does not.
- **Repeat-order behaviour at the Japanese and Korean accounts.** Samsung, Murata and Taiyo
  Yuden have all validated and taken batch supply — the most valuable fact in the file,
  because those three audit harder than anyone. But is it production volume or qualification
  volume, and is it only at their overseas (non-Japan) bases?
- How much of the win is **"the incumbent could not supply"** rather than price or
  performance? Japanese base-film makers are reportedly prioritising domestic customers.
  A qualification won on scarcity is weaker than one won on merit.
- **What is realised ¥/m² and gross margin per m²?** Nobody outside the company knows. Note
  that widely circulated retail figures (e.g. "¥68/m² for 12μm high-end") fail arithmetic by
  roughly two orders of magnitude against quoted resin prices — do not model them.

### Q3. Is the profit turning into cash, and who is paying for the capex?

This is the clearest amber flag on the balance sheet, and it is the question that decides
whether the growth is self-funding or borrowed.

- **Accounts receivable ¥608m — reportedly ~277% of net profit**, and 8.4% of total assets.
- **Inventory ¥524m** (7.2% of assets).
- **在建工程 (construction in progress) +184.1% YoY** — Jiangxi relocation, Tianjin North China
  R&D/production base, and electronic-grade BOPET film phase 2.
- **Long-term borrowings +145.5% YoY.** Liability ratio **43.6%**. Cash ¥596m, which is only
  **60.5% of current liabilities**.
- FY2025 OCF of **¥326m against ¥220m of net profit** — 148% conversion, which is genuinely
  good and partially rebuts the above. But OCF has to cover a capex programme that is
  visibly larger than it.

The honest reading: cash conversion at the operating line is fine; the problem is
**the financing structure of the expansion**. A third-party analysis puts it bluntly — the
company's results are "driven by capital expenditure and debt financing."

And the answer to "does the next phase need another raise" is now **yes, and it has already
been announced.** The 埃福思 acquisition issues 34.45m shares (7.6% dilution) and carries
supporting fundraising of up to ¥915m more. So the funding stack for the current expansion is:
operating cash flow ¥326m, long-term borrowings +145.5%, ¥600m of convertible debt converted
to equity in June, and now an equity issuance. **Four funding sources in eighteen months, for
a company whose gross margin has fallen five years running.**

**Ask:** committed capex for 2026–27 against forecast OCF, **stated separately from the
acquisition and its supporting raise**; **合同负债 (contract liabilities)** trend, the cleanest
public backlog proxy and a figure I could not obtain; AR **ageing buckets** and concentration
(as of 2021, the top five debtors were 56.6% of receivables); and the intended use of the
supporting funds — whether they finance 埃福思, the core capex programme, or working capital.

### Q4. Is 70% global carrier-tape share *pricing power*, or just share?

82.8% of revenue and effectively all of the profit sits here, and it grew only **+10.7% in
2025** — in a year when MLCC output was accelerating hard. That gap is the whole question.

Two structural points make it worse than it looks:

- **Carrier tape demand tracks component *units*; the AI cycle is a *value* story.** A Rubin-class
  rack uses 600k MLCCs at high unit value, but the unit-volume engine is still consumer
  electronics. So the 83% of revenue that carries the profit **participates far less in the
  thesis than the headline MLCC narrative implies.** Release film is the opposite — high-cap
  MLCCs consume enormous film area per finished part.
- **Overseas revenue has fallen from 72.4% (2016) to 56.2% (2019) to ~26% (2025).** Some of
  that is customers localising production, which is neutral. Some of it may be share loss or
  price competition offshore, which is not. These have very different implications and the
  annual report distinguishes them.

**Ask:** carrier-tape **volume vs ASP** split over three years; whether the base-paper
integration advantage (electronic-grade base paper made in-house) still translates into a
gross-margin premium over competitors, and by how much; and the reason for the export-share
decline, in the company's own words.

### Q5. What has to be true for ~¥14bn to be cheap?

Reverse the valuation rather than forecasting into it.

At a terminal **25x** — defensible for a materials company with a five-year record of
declining margins and mid-single-digit ROE — ¥14.4bn requires **~¥576m of net profit**.
That is **2.6x** 2025, **1.5x** the all-time peak, and roughly the **2027 sell-side
consensus** of ¥579–650m. **So the current price is approximately the full 2027 consensus,
capitalised at 25x, discounted at zero.**

Then check the near-term arithmetic, which is where this gets uncomfortable:

> Q1 2026 net profit was **¥47.75m**. In 2025, Q1 was **15.3%** of the full year. On identical
> seasonality, 2026 lands at **~¥312m (+42%)**. Consensus is **¥470–501m**. Getting there
> requires Q2–Q4 to average **~¥141–151m per quarter — about 3x the Q1 run rate.**

That is not impossible: price increases landed in April–May, Tianjin volume is ramping, and
H2 is seasonally stronger. But it is a hockey stick, and **this sell-side has been wrong in
exactly this shape before:**

| Forecast vintage | Year forecast | Estimate | Actual | Miss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 reports | 2024 | ¥433m | **¥202m** | **−53%** |
| 2025 consensus | 2025 | ¥301m | **¥220m** | **−27%** |

**The question is not whether this is a good business — the carrier-tape franchise plainly
is. It is: do you believe a company that has missed by 53% and 27% will now deliver +114%,
and what do you get paid if it delivers +42% instead?** At +42%, the stock is on ~46x 2026
earnings with the margin question unresolved.

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — The controlling shareholder: a trader who built a manufacturer, now buying his way into
deep tech.** 方隽云, born September 1964, holds a master's degree, an EMBA and an economist
qualification. His career before Jiemei was **trade, not materials**: department manager at
中国包装进出口浙江公司 (China Packaging Import & Export, Zhejiang), then GM and chairman of
杭州轩星贸易. He founded 浙江洁美电子 in **April 2001** and has been chairman *and* general
manager ever since. That background explains the company precisely: the founding insight was
**import substitution in a consumable whose trade flows and customers he already knew**, and
executing it produced a genuine ~70% global share. It also frames the current risk. Every one
of the three growth options — BOPET base film chemistry, composite current collectors, ion-beam
polishing — is **deep process technology being acquired rather than built**, by a founder whose
demonstrated edge is commercial rather than technical. That is a defensible strategy (buy what
you cannot build) but it makes integration capability, not vision, the binding constraint.

Three specific governance facts to price: (a) **control is 55.69%** via 方隽云 personally,
浙江元龙股权投资管理集团 and 安吉百顺 — minority shareholders have no mechanism to affect any
outcome; (b) **chairman and GM are the same person**, with no separation of oversight from
execution, in a company now running four unrelated businesses; (c) **succession has begun** —
daughter **方骥柠** is on the board and holds 1.00% of 元龙 but no direct shares. 方隽云 is 62.
**Ask what the succession plan is and what 安吉百顺's shareholder list looks like**, which I
could not obtain.

**Q7 — Related-party transactions: a family-linked steam monopoly, sitting directly on the
gross margin line.** This is the item that connects governance to Q1. On 17 April 2026 the board
approved 2026 related-party **steam purchases of ¥125m, up 133.3%** from ¥53.58m actually
purchased in 2025 — with 江西洁美电材 alone going from ¥0.98m to a budgeted ¥50m, roughly
**50x**. Directors **方隽云 and 方骥柠 both recused themselves**, confirming the counterparty is
family-connected. The company's stated justification is genuine and probably correct on
necessity: both subsidiaries sit in locations where **the related party is the only centralised
heat supplier**, with no substitutable third party.

Necessity is not the issue. **Price is.** Steam is a direct manufacturing input that flows
straight into cost of goods sold — which means a family-owned utility charging the listed
company is **a channel through which gross margin can leak to the controlling shareholder**, and
this memo's central finding is a five-year gross margin decline. Scale it: ¥125m is **~6.0% of
2025 revenue**. A 10% overcharge would be ~¥12.5m, or **5.7% of 2025 net profit** — not fatal,
but not trivial either, and it grows with the Jiangxi and Tianjin ramps. **Ask for the pricing
basis** — government-guided tariff, benchmarked market price, or negotiated — and the per-tonne
price versus what an arm's-length industrial user in the same municipality pays. If the answer
is "government-guided price," this concern closes entirely; if it is "negotiated," it stays open
and should be monitored every year as volumes scale.

**Q8 — Equity incentives: the 2025 target was already missed, which makes the 2026 target the
most honest forecast available.** The company has run incentive plans since 2018. The current
one — the **2025 employee share ownership plan**, drafted August 2025, covering **no more than 81
people at ¥13.10 per share** — has assessment years 2025 and 2026. Its **2025 targets were
revenue growth ≥8% and net profit growth ≥20%** off a 2024 base.

| 2025 ESOP target | Target | Actual | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | ≥8% | **+15.6%** | Met |
| Net profit growth | **≥20%** | **+8.7%** | **Missed** |

That result deserves emphasis. The plan was drafted in **August 2025 — eight months into the
year**, when management already had good visibility on the outcome, and they still set a +20%
profit target and delivered +8.7%. **A company that misses a target it set with two-thirds of
the year already known has a demonstrated tendency to over-forecast its own profit.** That is
the single most relevant base rate for judging whether the 2026 sell-side consensus of +114% is
achievable — and it points the same way as the two documented forecast misses in Q5.

The **2026 assessment target is the number to obtain, and it is not yet in my hands.** It is
worth more than any analyst estimate: it is management's own committed profit growth, set with
full knowledge of the MLCC cycle, and it is a genuinely falsifiable read on their internal view.
**If the 2026 ESOP target is materially below the consensus +114%, that gap is management telling
you what they actually believe.** Note also that the 2025 plan's ¥13.10 transfer price against a
¥31.79 share price means participants are up ~143%, and the **second ESOP sold out and terminated
in January 2026** — insiders have been realising, not accumulating.

**Q9 — The 埃福思 acquisition: better arithmetic than the headlines, worse fit than the pitch.**
The deal is to buy 100% of **长沙埃福思科技** (founded Aug 2018) from 周林 and four others for
**¥915m**, entirely in shares — **34.45m shares at ¥26.56**, plus supporting fundraising of up to
100% of deal value. 埃福思 makes **ion-beam and magnetorheological polishing machines** for
ultra-precision optics, including lithography optical elements; customers include 福晶科技,
茂莱光学, 波长光电 and CAS institutes, and are **concentrated in research institutes and military
units**. Founder 周林 holds a master's and PhD from **国防科技大学**, taught there, and was a
visiting scholar at Brookhaven National Laboratory. This is a real hard-tech team, not a shell.

**The "506.95% premium" headline is the wrong lens** and worth correcting: that premium is over
**book net assets of ¥151m**, which is a meaningless denominator for an asset-light R&D equipment
business. On earnings the deal is unremarkable, even cheap:

| 埃福思 | 2024A | 2025A | 2026E (committed) | 2027E | 2028E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ¥80.5m | ¥130m | — | — | — |
| Net profit | ¥26.1m | **¥45.3m** | **¥63.0m** | ¥73.0m | ¥84.4m |
| Implied multiple on ¥915m | — | **20.2x** | **14.5x** | 12.5x | 10.8x |

And the accretion arithmetic is favourable: Jiemei is **issuing stock valued at ~55x its own
earnings to buy an asset at 20x**. Adding ¥45.3m to a ¥220m base is **+20.6% of profit for +7.6%
of shares** — roughly **+12% to EPS** before any synergy, on 2025 actuals. On that basis the deal
is not the value destruction the "cross-industry, 500% premium" framing implies.

**The real objections are three, and they are about fit and risk, not price:**
1. **Goodwill of ~¥764m** — **3.5x** 2025 net profit and roughly 16–19% of equity. A single
   impairment would erase several years of earnings from the core.
2. **Zero operational overlap.** Carrier tape, release film, battery foil and lithography-optics
   polishing share no customers, no channel, no process and no raw material. The only genuine
   synergy on offer is capital, and the ¥220m of committed cumulative profit covers just **24%**
   of the ¥915m price.
3. **A target whose customers are research institutes and military units** has lumpy, tender-driven,
   politically-timed orders — the hardest revenue in existence to underwrite against a three-year
   earnout, and the reason the ¥63.0m 2026 commitment (+39% on 2025) deserves scrutiny.

**And the pattern matters more than the deal.** 柔震科技 (Dec 2024, ¥30m for 56.83%, composite
current collectors, still pre-commercial and loss-making at the minority line) and now 埃福思
(¥915m, semiconductor optics) are **two cross-industry acquisitions in twenty months, while the
core business's gross margin fell every year.** Two readings fit equally well: management is
intelligently redeploying capital out of a maturing core, or management has stopped believing in
the core and the market has not noticed. **The 2026 ESOP target in Q8 discriminates between
them** — a low internal target for the core would confirm the second reading.

**Watch the process.** The board approved the draft on **7 August 2026**; on **11 August** the
company published the draft summary and simultaneously announced it **would not yet convene the
shareholder meeting**, citing "the overall work arrangements for the transaction." That phrasing
is boilerplate and the deferral is procedurally ordinary. It is still worth tracking: a deal
first announced in March that reaches draft stage in August without a shareholder vote scheduled
is not yet done. Market reaction on the March resumption was lukewarm — the stock opened higher
and closed **+1.08%**.

**Q10 — Customer concentration is extreme and the top three are the competition's customers too.**
Top-five customers have run at roughly **67%** of revenue for three years — materially higher than
most listed peers — with the top three stably **国巨 (Yageo), 华新科 (Walsin) and 三星电机 (Samsung
Electro-Mechanics)**, alongside Murata, Panasonic, Taiyo Yuden, 风华高科, 三环集团 and 顺络电子.
This cuts both ways: it is a roster no newcomer can replicate, and it means three customers can
reset the price of 83% of revenue in a single annual negotiation. **Get the single-largest
customer share and the FY2025 top-five figure from the annual report**, and establish whether
release film is being sold into the *same* accounts (cross-sell, sticky, weaker pricing) or new
ones (genuine expansion).

**Q11 — Where in the cycle is 2026, and what is normalised earnings?** This company has now shown
you two full cycles: 2020–21 peak (¥289m, ¥389m), 2022 trough (¥170m), 2023–25 plateau (¥256m,
¥202m, ¥220m), 2026 boom. Note what that plateau means — **the last up-cycle's peak earnings have
not been matched in four subsequent years.** If ¥470m is a peak-cycle 2026 and mid-cycle is
¥280–330m, the stock is on **44–51x normalised**. Management's own guidance that shortage
conditions persist "through H1 2027" is, read literally, **a statement that they expect the
tightness to end in 2027**. Make them confirm whether that is guidance or caution.

**Q12 — Share supply is quantifiable and currently unhelpful.** Four live items: (a) the ¥600m
洁美转债 was force-redeemed in June 2026 after converting deep in the money — the dilution is in
the count and converting holders are natural sellers into strength; (b) the controlling
shareholder **浙江元龙 has pledged 62.95m shares — 31.25% of its own holding and 13.88% of shares
outstanding**, with pledges and releases running through 2025 and 2026; (c) the **second ESOP
completed its sale and terminated in January 2026**; (d) the 埃福思 consideration shares carry
lock-ups of **12 months** for counterparties holding the target over a year and **36 months**

## 4. Provisional read

**The business** is genuinely good and genuinely improving. A ~70% global share in paper
carrier tape with in-house base paper is a real, durable franchise that no one is about to
attack. The release-film import substitution is a real multi-year opportunity, it is arriving
at the best possible moment in the MLCC cycle, and validation plus batch supply at Samsung,
Murata and Taiyo Yuden is not something a marginal supplier achieves. Volume up over 100%
since March is not an accounting artefact. Management deliberately exiting low-end grades is
the right call and shows they understand the margin problem.

**The stock** is a different question, and the answer is harder. You are paying ~65x trailing
and ~29–31x a 2026 consensus that requires **+114% profit growth from a Q1 that ran at a
+42% pace**, from a company whose gross margin has declined **every year for five years**,
whose forecasters have missed by **53% and 27%** in the last two attempts, whose growth
segment is **12% of revenue** facing a competitor announcing **5x its capacity**, and whose
expansion is being funded by long-term debt growing at 146% while the dividend was cut.

**Governance does not settle the question, but it tilts it.** Control is absolute (55.69%,
chairman and GM the same person), so there is no mechanism by which minority holders change
anything. The related-party steam contract is defensible on necessity and unexamined on price,
which matters more than usual because it sits directly on the gross margin line that is the
whole investment case. The 埃福思 acquisition is **better than its press** — 20x trailing
earnings paid in stock worth 55x is accretive arithmetic, and 周林's team is real — but it puts
¥764m of goodwill and a fourth unrelated industry onto a management team that has not yet fixed
the margin in its first one. **The most damning single governance fact is the smallest one:
management set a +20% profit target in August 2025, with eight months of the year already
visible, and delivered +8.7%.** A company that misses its own target under those conditions is
not a company whose +114% consensus should be taken at face value.

The honest framing: **a real franchise plus a real cyclical up-leg, at a price that requires
the up-leg to become a permanent margin re-rating within two years — run by an owner-operator
who is diversifying away from the franchise at the same time.** What would flip the conclusion
is narrow and specific: **segment gross margin for 电子级薄膜材料 disclosed at 35%+ with blended
margin turning up**, sustained across two quarters. That single disclosure converts the story
from "capex has been eating the P&L for five years" to "the mix shift is finally arriving," and
it is worth a great deal more than any amount of shipment-volume news flow.

**Do not size a position before the H1 report.** It is days away, it contains the segment margin
number that settles Q1, and — if the ESOP's 2026 target is disclosed alongside it — management's
own view of the year, which is worth more than the consensus. At this multiple the downside on a
margin disappointment is a de-rating rather than a miss.
