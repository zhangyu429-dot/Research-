# 洁美科技 (Jiemei Technology, 002859.SZ) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 11 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Jiemei is **not** a single business. It is a mature, dominant, slow-growing cash cow
funding a capital-hungry option on import substitution, plus a second, much rawer option
on batteries. Any view on the stock is really a view on how those three pieces net out.

| Segment | 2025 revenue | YoY | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 电子封装材料 (paper/plastic carrier tape, cover tape, base paper) | ¥1.739bn (83%) | +10.7% | ~70% claimed **global** share of paper carrier tape. The cash cow. |
| 电子级薄膜材料 (MLCC / OCA / polarizer release film 离型膜, BOPET base film) | ¥0.260bn (12%) | +48.0% | The growth option — displacing Toray / Mitsui / Lintec / SKC |

**Market:** ~¥31.8/share, ~¥13.5bn market cap (~426m shares, after the ¥600m 洁美转债 was

**~35x** the 2021 peak earnings the company has not since matched.

> **The single fact that frames everything:** revenue is +12.8% since 2021, net profit is
> **-43%**. Four years of heavy capex converted growth into margin. The entire bull case is
> that 2026 is the year that reverses — the entire bear case is that it is the fourth
> consecutive year the reversal is one year away.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. Is the 2026 release-film windfall a *cycle* or a *franchise*?

MLCC is in a violent up-cycle. Murata / Samsung Electro-Mechanics / Taiyo Yuden utilisation
>95%, book-to-bill 1.25–1.31, high-cap AI lead times stretched from 8–12 to 16–24 weeks,
Q1–Q3 2026 price increases of 15–35% (50%+ on AI high-cap parts). Jiemei raised high-end
release film prices 5–18% in April–May 2026 and shipped **27m m² in April alone**, versus
full-year 2025 film revenue of only ¥260m.

That is a genuine, verifiable demand shock. It is also exactly the environment in which a
marginal supplier gets qualified, and exactly the environment that reverses.

**Sub-questions to answer before underwriting any 2027+ number:**
- Decompose the 2026 gain: how much is **price**, how much **volume**, how much **mix**
  (mid/high-end share)? Price gains reverse; qualified sockets at Samsung/Murata do not.
- Once qualified at a Japanese/Korean MLCC maker in a shortage, does the share **stick**
  through the next glut, or is it a swing-supply relationship priced off spot?
- What share of 2026 film revenue is **AI/high-cap** (structural) vs consumer
  (cyclical)? Management says mid/high-end is 70%+ of the 400m m² design capacity — verify
  against actual shipped mix, not design intent.

### Q2. What is the unit economics of release film — and what is it after everyone's capacity lands?

**Nobody outside the company knows the realised ¥/m² or gross margin per m².** This is the
single largest disclosure gap and the number the entire valuation rests on. Back out:
2025 film revenue ¥260m; April 2026 run-rate 27m m²/month ≈ 324m m²/yr. Those two figures
only reconcile at a low blended ASP, which means the 2026 revenue ramp should be violent —
or the mix disclosure is not what it appears.

Meanwhile, announced domestic capacity is alarming relative to demand:

| Player | MLCC release film capacity | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **洁美科技** | 400m m²/yr design; Tianjin ph.2 adds 240m m² + 20kt BOPET | Full vertical: BOPET base film → silicone coating |
| **双星新材 (002585)** | Ph.1 500m m² full by end-2026; ph.2 500m m² building; **2.0bn m² long-term** | Claims #1 domestic share (~33%) |
| **斯迪克 (300806)** | ~30m m² → 100m m² in 2026 | Ultra-thin / ≤50nm smoothness, 800+ layer |
| **东材科技** | ~32m m² | Mid-end |

Japanese/Korean incumbents still hold ~70–80% of the *high-end* global market, so the
substitution runway is real. But if 双星 alone executes its plan, domestic supply
multiplies several-fold into a market whose true size is genuinely disputed (one widely
circulated figure of 2.15bn m² 2026 global demand looks inconsistent with these capacity
numbers and with published ASPs — **do not underwrite it without a primary source**).

**Ask:** at what capacity utilisation and price does the marginal domestic producer break
even, and where does Jiemei sit on that cost curve? Vertical integration into its own
BOPET base film is either a durable cost + quality advantage (base film smoothness /
particle control is the actual technical barrier, and Japanese base film supply is being
withheld) — or a fixed-cost trap that turns a price war into an operating-leverage disaster.

### Q3. Where did four years of capex go, and what is incremental ROIC?

2021 net profit ¥389m → 2025 net profit ¥220m, on higher revenue. ROE went from ~18% to
roughly 6–8%. Gross margin held up reasonably (35.8% in 2023 → 32.6% in Q1 2026), so the
compression is **below** the gross line: depreciation from Guangdong Zhaoqing, Tianjin, base
paper lines and base film lines landing before the revenue does, plus R&D and the 柔震
start-up drag.

This is the question that separates "operating leverage about to inflect" from "serial
capital destruction dressed as growth."

- What is the **cumulative capex 2021–2025 vs. cumulative incremental EBIT**? If capex has
  run well ahead of operating cash flow (2025 OCF was only ¥326m), the company has been
  funding an option with shareholder capital for five years.
- What is the **specific IRR / payback** management underwrites on Tianjin phase 2
  (240m m² film + 20kt BOPET)? Ask it on an investor call, in ¥ of incremental EBIT.
- **How much depreciation is still to come online?** Tianjin phase 1 only entered trial
  production in H1 2026. Depreciation on a not-yet-utilised asset is a 2026–2027 headwind
  even if demand is strong.
- Reality-check the sell side: in 2022 analysts modelled 2024 net profit of ~¥433m.
  Actual was ¥202m — **a 53% miss**. Discount current 2026–2028 forecasts accordingly.

### Q4. Is 70% global share in carrier tape *pricing power*, or just share?

83% of revenue and effectively all of the profit comes from the cash cow. It grew only
+10.7% in 2025 — in a year when MLCC output was accelerating. That gap matters.

- Is carrier tape volume tracking MLCC/chip-resistor unit output, with **ASP deflating**
  the difference away? If yes, "70% global share" is share without pricing power, and the
  cash cow is a slowly-melting one.
- Carrier tape demand is driven by **component units**, not component value. AI is a value
  story (a few very expensive high-cap MLCCs), consumer is the unit story. So the AI cycle
  helps release film far more than it helps carrier tape. Does the 83% of revenue actually
  participate in the thesis at all?
- Base paper (电子专用原纸) self-supply is the historic moat. What is pulp cost pass-through,
  and has any competitor closed the gap?
- **Can the cash cow fund the options?** If cow EBIT is roughly flat and capex keeps rising,
  the answer is dilution or leverage — see Q9.

### Q5. What has to be true for ¥13.5bn to be cheap?

Reverse the valuation rather than forecasting into it. At a terminal 25x — generous for a
materials company with this ROE history — ¥13.5bn requires **~¥540m of net profit**, i.e.
**2.5x** 2025 and **1.4x** the all-time peak.

A plausible bridge: carrier tape ¥2.0–2.2bn revenue at ~12% net → ~¥260m; release film
¥1.0–1.2bn at 25–30% net → ~¥300m. That works — but only if film revenue grows **4–5x from
2025** *and* holds a margin that no domestic release film producer has yet demonstrated at
scale, *and* 双星's 1bn m² does not arrive first.

**So the question is not "is it a good company." It is: which of those three conditions do
you believe, and what do you get paid if only two of them happen?** At 61x trailing, the
answer is: not much. Note the stock already re-rated on the MLCC news flow (limit-up in May
2026), so the cycle is at least partly in the price.

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Customers.** Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Murata validation + batch supply is the
most valuable single fact in the 2025/Q1-26 disclosures, because those two audit harder
than anyone. But: is it **production volume or qualification volume**? What products, what
share of their spend, and is it at their overseas (non-Japan) bases only? Get customer
concentration and the top-5 revenue share from the annual report.

**Q7 — The de-Japanisation tailwind.** Domestic MLCC makers are reportedly under supply-chain
localisation pressure, and Japanese/Korean base film suppliers are prioritising their own
customers. That is a genuine, policy-plus-shortage tailwind. **How durable is it if the
shortage ends?** A qualification won because the incumbent could not supply is weaker than
one won on price or performance.

**Q8 — 复合集流体 (composite current collector): option or distraction?** 7 new PET-Al lines
added, 5 in trial, 11 customers qualified, 3 at small batch. This industry has consumed
enormous capital across multiple Chinese listcos with commercialisation repeatedly slipping
on welding, yield and cost-per-kWh. Ask: what is cumulative invested capital and cumulative
losses in 柔震? Is there a stated kill-criterion? Assign it a **zero or negative** value in
the base case and treat any positive surprise as free.

**Q9 — Balance sheet and funding.** The ¥600m 洁美转债 was force-redeemed in June 2026,
meaning it converted at a large gain — good (debt → equity, deleveraging) but the dilution
is now in the share count. Given Tianjin phase 2 has *started construction*: what is net
debt, what is the 2026–2027 committed capex schedule, and **does OCF cover it?** If not,
expect another raise — and price that in now rather than being surprised.

**Q10 — Governance and alignment.** Check controlling shareholder stake and **pledge ratio**,
any share incentive plan and whether its performance targets are demanding or trivially
achievable, buyback history, and the falling dividend (¥2.0/10sh in 2021 → ¥1.2/10sh for
2025) against rising capex. The dividend cut *is* the capital-allocation statement.

**Q11 — Source quality.** A large share of the bullish 2026 narrative in circulation is from
retail-facing 财富号 / self-media posts, and some numbers in them do not survive arithmetic
(e.g. a widely repeated "¥68/m² for 12μm high-end film" is inconsistent with quoted resin
prices by roughly two orders of magnitude). **Verify every operating number against the
定期报告 and 投资者关系活动记录表 before it enters a model.**

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **H1 2026 interim report (due late August 2026 — imminent).** The single highest-value
   event. Look for: 电子级薄膜材料 revenue *and* segment gross margin; whether Q2 net margin
   expanded above Q1's 8.95%; Tianjin ramp commentary; capex and net debt.
2. **Monthly release-film shipment trajectory** past the 27m m²/month April print — and
   whether ASP rose with it or volume was bought with price.
3. **Segment gross margin for film specifically.** If it is not disclosed, that absence is
   itself information.
4. **双星新材's phase-1 completion and pricing behaviour** in late 2026 — the clearest
   leading indicator of whether this becomes a commodity.
5. **MLCC lead times and book-to-bill at Murata / Samsung Electro-Mechanics.** When these
   normalise, the release-film price gains reverse first.
6. **Carrier tape ASP vs volume split** in the annual report — settles Q4.
7. **Any equity raise announcement** — settles Q9 unfavourably.

---

## 4. Provisional read

The business is real, the moat in carrier tape is real, and the release-film import
substitution is a genuine multi-year opportunity arriving at the best possible moment in the
MLCC cycle. **The problem is entirely price and history.** You are paying ~61x trailing
earnings for a company that has grown revenue 13% and shrunk profit 43% over four years,
whose sell-side forecasts missed by half last cycle, and whose growth segment is 12% of
revenue facing a competitor announcing 5x its capacity.

The honest framing: this is a **cyclical up-leg layered on an unproven structural story, at
a valuation that requires the structural story to work.** The asymmetry only becomes
attractive if (a) H1/Q3 2026 prove film gross margin is genuinely 30%+ at scale, or (b) the
price de-rates materially on a cyclical scare while the Samsung/Murata sockets hold.

**Do not size a position before the H1 report.** It is days away and answers Q2 and Q3
more cheaply than any amount of further modelling.
