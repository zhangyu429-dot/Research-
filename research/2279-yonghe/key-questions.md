# 雍禾医疗 (Yonghe Medical, 2279.HK) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 18 September 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

*Second revision, same day: the **full 2026 interim report (pages 1–82) has now been read**. Every financial, ownership and operating figure in this memo is now primary. Two first-draft conclusions were corrected (segment gross margins are disclosed; gross profit per transplant customer fell 3.5% rather than holding flat), and **every item previously marked "not obtained" is now closed**: selling and marketing expense, interest-bearing debt (zero), lease liabilities, contract liabilities, capex, CPE Yuanfeng's current stake (34.97%), and share capital. **The most important new finding: capex is 11% of depreciation — the falling depreciation that supports the gross margin is the asset base being consumed.***

## 0. What you are actually buying

Yonghe Medical is **a chain of surgical clinics that has finished shrinking and started earning
again, whose growth is driven entirely by cutting price to buy volume — and which the market
still prices as though this round of profit will not last.** It is China's largest hair-medical
chain: 63 hair-transplant clinics across 61 cities at end-2025, plus 7 Svenson hair-care stores.
It listed in December 2021 at a HK$8.631bn market cap; it is now roughly HK$1.1bn, **down about
87%**. What happened in between is not in dispute: three consecutive loss years in 2022–24
totalling about ¥859m, clinics cut from 75 to 63, Svenson stores from 13 to 6–7, doctors from
350 to 241, headcount from 4,395 to 3,383. In 2025 the company returned to profit (net profit
¥73.56m); in H1 2026 it made ¥75.46m — **more in six months than in all of 2025.**

The investment therefore compresses to one question: **how much of the gross-margin repair from
56% to 69% is repeatable, and how long that curve can hold while the average ticket price falls
20.8% in two years.** This is not a question about whether a growth story will be delivered.
It is a question about whether profit that has *already happened* is real — the opposite
direction, and a different test.

> **China's hair-transplant market would grow from ¥17.3bn in 2021 to ¥47.0bn by 2026 at a 22.1%
> CAGR**, with the broader hair-medical services market reaching **¥138.1bn by 2030.** Five years
> on, **Yonghe's own revenue is still 16.6% below its 2021 level**, and the **average transplant
> ticket has fallen from ¥22,987 in H1 2024 to ¥18,214 in H1 2026, −20.8% in two years.** A later
> third-party forecast now puts the transplant market at "¥75.6bn by 2030, 27.6% CAGR from 2025"

> was overstated by roughly a factor of two from the start, or the leader lost a great deal of
> share in a fast-growing market.** This memo leans to the first — but either way, **any bull
> case that opens with "penetration is only 0.21%, the runway is enormous" has already been
> falsified once, by five years of data.**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1: Of the gross-margin move from 56.1% to 69.0%, how much is repeatable?

This is the pivot of the entire investment, because **the whole profit recovery came from gross
margin.** Between 2023 and H1 2026 revenue went from ¥1,777m to an annualised ~¥1,960m, while
gross margin rose from **56.11%** to **69.0%** — 12.9 points, worth about ¥250m of gross profit
on ¥1,960m of revenue, against an H1 2026 net profit of only ¥75.5m. **Without those 12.9 points
there is no turnaround.**

The interim report gives the full cost-of-sales breakdown, so **this question can largely be
closed — and the answer is unfavourable to the bull case.**

| Cost of sales and services (RMB'000) | H1 2026 | % | H1 2025 | % | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Staff costs | 140,618 | 46.3% | 125,701 | 40.4% | **+11.9%** |
| Amortisation and depreciation | 101,663 | 33.5% | 112,318 | 36.1% | **−9.5%** |
| Inventories and consumables | 37,972 | 12.5% | 52,518 | 16.9% | **−27.7%** |
| Operation related expenses | 14,130 | 4.6% | 14,391 | 4.6% | −1.8% |
| Other expenses | 9,341 | 3.1% | 6,299 | 2.0% | +48.3% |
| **Total** | **303,724** | 100.0% | **311,227** | 100.0% | **−2.4%** |

Split the RMB126.6m gross profit increase into a revenue effect and a cost effect: **about
RMB76.1m came from revenue growth at the old margin, and about RMB50.5m from costs not rising
with revenue.** Of that RMB50.5m of cost saving:

| Source of the cost saving | RMB'000 | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Amortisation and depreciation** | 26,180 | **51.8%** |
| **Inventories and consumables** | 21,805 | **43.2%** |
| Staff costs | 2,458 | 4.9% |
| Operation related expenses | 2,250 | 4.5% |
| Other expenses | −2,171 | −4.3% |

> **The conclusion after reading the interim report: depreciation and consumables together account
> for 95.0% of the cost saving.** Those are precisely the two least repeatable lines:
>
> - **D&A −9.5%** is the mechanical result of closures and assets running off. **It is a
>   depleting stock, not a capability**, and it reverses the moment the "1+N" clinics open.
> - **Consumables −27.7%** — about RMB1,568 down to RMB931 per procedure, **−40.6%**. The company
>   attributes this to "optimisation of inventory cost structure and cost savings achieved through
>   bulk procurement" — i.e. explanation 1 above (procurement leverage), **not smaller procedures**.
>   That is good news for clinical-quality risk but **not for durability**: a bulk-buying benefit
>   is banked once, and consumables are now only 12.5% of cost of sales and 3.9% of revenue —
>   **the base is too small for further cuts to matter.**
> - **Staff costs actually rose 11.9%**, which the company attributes to performance-linked pay
>   rising with revenue. **It is the one large line that tracks revenue, and it is 46.3% of cost
>   of sales.**
>
> **So the answer to Q1 is: very little of the 12.9 points is repeatable.** Once depreciation
> bottoms and the procurement benefit is banked, with staff costs still growing, the only
> available directions for gross margin are flat or down.

> **The second finding from the full report nails Q1 shut: capex is 11% of depreciation.**
>
> H1 2026 depreciation and amortisation totalled **RMB115.4m** (right-of-use assets RMB61.2m,
> property plant and equipment RMB51.9m, intangibles RMB2.4m), against **net capex of just
> RMB12.8m** (RMB13.7m of PP&E plus RMB0.1m of intangibles, less RMB1.0m of disposal proceeds).
> Net PP&E fell from RMB286.7m to **RMB250.1m**; right-of-use assets from RMB425.7m to
> **RMB382.7m**. **Contracted capital commitments are RMB3.0m.**
>
> **In other words: depreciation is falling because the company is consuming its asset base and
> barely replacing it.** That is not efficiency, it is **harvesting**. It explains three things at
> once — why gross margin rose, why free cash flow looks so strong, and why both have a definite
> expiry date: **once the "1+N" network is actually built, depreciation and staff costs rise
> together and capex jumps off RMB12.8m.**

**And the answer on selling and marketing (interim report): it did not fall — it got more
expensive.**

| Selling and marketing expenses (RMB'000) | H1 2026 | % of revenue | H1 2025 | % of revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing and promotion | 299,379 | 30.5% | 254,047 | 29.5% | **+17.8%** |
| Staff costs | 126,406 | 12.9% | 109,966 | 12.8% | +14.9% |
| Other | 17,655 | 1.8% | 17,395 | 2.0% | +1.5% |
| **Total** | **443,440** | **45.2%** | **381,408** | **44.3%** | **+16.3%** |

> **This is the single most damaging table in the report for the bull case: selling and marketing
> grew 16.3%, faster than revenue's 13.8%, and the ratio rose from 44.3% to 45.2%.** The company
> attributes it to "adjustments in channel structure and increased investment in high-output
> channels" and "a phased increase in brand-building". **So not one yuan of the H1 2026 profit
> growth came from marketing efficiency — all of it came from gross margin, and 95% of that came
> from depreciation and consumables.** General and administrative expenses of RMB117.3m (+4.6%,
> 12.0% of revenue against 13.0%) are the one line genuinely diluted by growth, but they are too
> small to change the conclusion.

### Q2: Cutting price to buy volume has pushed gross profit per customer against the wall — where does this curve end?

The first draft ran this line using group gross margin as a proxy. **The interim report discloses
gross profit by segment, so it can now be computed exactly — and the exact answer is worse than
the approximation.**

| Hair transplant (interim report) | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Procedures | 33,504 | **40,793** | **+21.8%** |
| Average ticket | ¥19,761 | **¥18,222** | **−7.8%** |
| Segment gross profit (RMB'000) | 439,595 | 516,261 | +17.4% |
| Segment gross margin | 66.4% | **69.5%** | +3.1pt |
| **Gross profit per customer** | **¥13,121** | **¥12,656** | **−3.5%** |

| Medical hair care (interim report) | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers | 44,912 | **56,757** | +26.4% |
| Average ticket | ¥4,184 | **¥3,995** | **−4.5%** |
| Segment gross margin | 56.2% | **68.8%** | **+12.6pt** |
| **Gross profit per customer** | **¥2,351** | **¥2,747** | **+16.8%** |
| **Package repurchase rate** | **29.9%** | **25.9%** | **−4.0pt** |

> **Correction:** the first draft estimated gross profit per customer as "roughly flat (−0.5%)".
> **On segment data it actually fell 3.5%** — the 3.1 points of segment margin gain did **not**
> offset the 7.8% ticket decline. The direction of the conclusion is unchanged, but the pressure
> is greater than the draft judged.

**The two segments point in opposite directions, and that is the key:**

- **Hair transplant (75.8% of revenue): volume +21.8%, price −7.8%, gross profit per customer
  −3.5%.** Each additional procedure earns less than the one before it.
- **Hair care (23.1% of revenue): volume +26.4%, price −4.5%, but margin +12.6 points and gross
  profit per customer +16.8%.** **All of the group's unit-economics improvement sits in the
  segment that is under a quarter of revenue.**

**And there is a direct counter-indicator on that line: the package repurchase rate fell from
29.9% to 25.9%.** A business that defines itself as "long-term membership-based hair health
management" grew customers 26.4% while repurchase fell 4 points — meaning the increment is mostly
new customers, who renew less than the existing base. **That turns the "second curve" question
from "can it scale" into "will what it scales stick"** — and a 12.6-point margin jump that comes
from package mix or revenue-recognition timing rather than genuine operating leverage will not
survive a falling repurchase rate.

The 2027 condition can now be solved exactly:

> If the transplant ticket falls another 7.8% (to about ¥16,801), holding gross profit per customer
> at ¥12,656 **requires a segment gross margin of about 75.3%**, against 69.5% today. Q1 has
> already shown that the margin gains came from depreciation and consumables, both nearly spent.
> **If the segment margin instead stops at 69.5%, gross profit per customer falls to about
> ¥11,669 (−7.8%), and volume must grow a further ~8.5% just to hold segment gross profit flat.**

That is the real disagreement in this stock: **trading price for volume is a sustainable strategy
only while gross margin is still rising — and gross margin is now near its all-time high.** Once
margin tops out, price declines pass one-for-one into gross profit.

**Ask:**
- **The composition of the ticket-price decline**: lower price per follicular unit, smaller
  procedures, or a higher share of fixed-price packages? The company launched transplant
  "one-price" packages back in 2023.
- **The quarterly path of the ticket price** — Q1 versus Q2 2026. If it stabilised in Q2 the story
  is materially different.
- **Do female patients carry a different ticket?** Female procedures exceeded 14,000 in H1 2026,
  about 34.4% of volume, with female revenue up ~40%. **If the female ticket is lower, then
  "female-led growth" and "falling ticket price" are the same phenomenon.**
- **Repeat purchase and lifetime value**: 56,757 hair-care customers versus 40,793 transplant
  customers — what is the cross-conversion rate? It is the only structural variable that can
  offset a falling ticket.

### Q3: ¥496m of operating cash flow against ¥73.6m of net profit — where does the 6.7x come from, and how much cash is left?

FY2025 operating cash flow was **¥496m, 6.7x net profit**; H1 2026 was ¥264m, 3.5x. **A ratio
like that is a red flag in manufacturing but often means something else in a prepaid consumer
service** — it has to be taken apart line by line:

- **Depreciation and amortisation, including right-of-use assets.** FY2025 EBITDA of ¥364m
  against ¥73.6m of net profit leaves a ~¥290m gap, much of it D&A. **Under IFRS 16 rent does not
  appear in EBITDA at all — it is split into ROU depreciation and lease interest — and repayment
  of lease principal sits outside operating cash flow.** For a chain running 63 surgical centres
  plus 7 stores, **both EBITDA and operating cash flow are systematically overstated, by roughly
  the amount of the rent.** **Required: total annual lease payments, and free cash flow defined
  as operating cash flow less lease principal less capex.**
- **Contract liabilities / customer prepayments.** Hair care is sold as prepaid packages and
  transplants often carry deposits. **Prepayments swing operating cash flow directly as the
  network contracts or expands.** Hair-care revenue fell 8.2% in 2025; if prepayments rose at the
  same time, 2026 revenue has already been sold; if they fell, part of the ¥496m is not repeatable.
- **One-off working-capital release.** Inventory run-down and stretched payables during a
  contraction both generate cash once.

**This question now has a complete answer (interim report, cash flow statement and balance
sheet).**

| H1 2026 cash flow (RMB'000) | |
|---|---|
| Net cash generated from operating activities | **263,732** |
| Less: payments of lease liabilities | **(78,583)** |
| Less: net capex (13,820 purchased, 1,027 disposals) | **(12,793)** |
| **= Free cash flow after rent and capex** | **172,356** |
| Investing: net placement of term deposits | (90,000) |
| Financing: dividends paid | (38,008) |
| Net increase in cash for the period | 44,348 |

**The 6.7x puzzle resolves, and in the benign direction:** the gap is mostly D&A of RMB115.4m
(including RMB61.2m of right-of-use depreciation) plus the increase in contract liabilities
(RMB259.7m → RMB288.2m, +RMB28.5m). **Lease principal of RMB78.6m does sit in financing rather
than operating cash flow — the IFRS 16 overstatement this memo's first draft flagged is real —
but after deducting it, half-year free cash flow is still RMB172.4m.**

> **Against a market cap of about RMB1,004m, RMB172m of half-year free cash flow implies an
> annualised FCF yield of roughly 34% (about 62% on the RMB556m enterprise value). A number that
> large demands an explanation.**
>
> **The explanation is in the table above: net capex of RMB12.8m is 11% of depreciation.** A
> meaningful part of this free cash flow was not earned — it was **not reinvested**. At the current
> rate the clinic asset base depreciates away in roughly a decade. **The true maintainable free
> cash flow needs a normalised maintenance capex deducted, and the company does not disclose it.
> It is now the single number this memo most wants.**

**And who the cash belongs to can now be computed.** Cash and equivalents of RMB743.2m plus term
deposits of RMB140.3m = **RMB883.6m, against no interest-bearing debt whatsoever**. Deduct lease
liabilities of RMB435.6m and it is RMB447.9m; deduct contract liabilities of RMB288.2m — services
collected but not yet delivered — and it is still **RMB159.7m, positive**. **On even the harshest
definition, this is a net cash company.**

**The only remaining ask is the normalised maintenance capex, plus the per-clinic investment and
build schedule for the "1+N" network.** Capital commitments of RMB3.0m confirm **the expansion has
not started** — which means the current cash flow is clean, and also that it has not yet been
tested by a new capex cycle.

### Q4: 241 doctors performed 40,793 procedures — is that a capacity ceiling or a compliance ceiling?

This is the only question in the memo that could destroy the thesis in a single event, and the
arithmetic points straight at it:

- At end-June 2026 the medical team was 1,410 people: **241 doctors**, 1,068 nurses, 101 other
  medical professionals (**interim report, page 15**, consistent with the secondary reporting).
  **A doctor-to-nurse ratio of 1:4.4.**
- Doctor headcount is **essentially unchanged** from 242 at end-2024, and **down 31%** from 350
  in 2023.
- H1 2026 delivered **40,793 procedures** — **about 169 per doctor per half-year, ~338 annualised**,
  or roughly **1.35 per working day** on 250 working days.

A hair transplant is a multi-hour procedure. **At 1.35 per doctor per day, the doctor's actual
involvement in any one operation is necessarily limited, and much of the extraction and
implantation is done by nurses.** That is not speculation — **the company has already been
penalised for exactly this: in February 2024 for "nurses impersonating doctors to perform
hair-transplant surgery".** Several clinics also carry advertising violations (the Shanghai
Yonghe Aimu clinic was most recently fined ¥150,000 for an advertising breach; the Guangzhou,
Taizhou and Beijing entities have penalties as well).

> **Put the two facts together: the sole growth engine of this recovery is surgical volume
> (+21.8%), and that volume was delivered with a flat doctor headcount.** So "efficiency gains"
> and "compliance risk" are not two separate topics at this company — **they are two readings of
> the same number.** In May 2025 SAMR issued its *Guidelines on Medical Advertising Supervision*;
> the regulatory direction is tightening, not loosening.

**The offsetting evidence that has to be stated (interim report, page 11):** the report discloses
consumers by service tier, and it points the other way — consumers served by **business deans**
rose from 7,458 (22.3%) to **12,644 (31.0%)**, up 69.5%, while those served by business chiefs went
from 24,293 (72.5%) to 26,021 (**63.8%**), with "Yongxiang" steady at 5.2%. **Customers are
shifting toward more senior physicians, not away from them.** That cuts against the dilution
concern and helps explain the 3.1-point gain in transplant segment margin, since senior tiers
price higher. **But it makes the falling ticket harder to explain, not easier: if the service mix
is moving up while the average ticket falls 7.8%, the discounting at the entry tiers is steeper
than the group average shows.**

**Ask:**
- **Physician hours per procedure**, and the written division of labour between doctors and
  nurses for extraction and implantation.
- **Procedures per doctor per year** for the last three years, and what the company regards as
  the ceiling.
- **Number of medical disputes and complaints, and amounts paid**, last three years.
- What changed in internal controls after the February 2024 penalty? Is there third-party audit?
- If volume is to grow another 20%, **does that come from more doctors or more procedures per
  existing doctor?** That answer is more or less the whole answer.

### Q5: For a ~¥1.0bn market cap to make sense, what has to be true? — reverse the valuation

Here it has to be run backwards. Jiemei was the familiar case — the market cap demands that
profit doubles, so ask whether the bridge exists. **Yonghe is the inverse: the market cap demands
that profit falls, so ask whether the reason exists.**

**With the full report, this sum can be finished exactly.**

Market cap is about **RMB1,004m** (525,472,416 shares × HK$2.10 × 0.91).
**Enterprise value = market cap − (cash RMB743.2m + term deposits RMB140.3m) + lease liabilities
RMB435.6m ≈ RMB556m.**

| Measure | Multiple |
|---|---|

> **RMB151m**; on 2025's seasonality, roughly **RMB200m** for the full year.

> numbers are real and can persist.**

**And having read the whole report, the market's scepticism has specific, defensible grounds —
just not the ones most people assume:**

1. **Earnings quality** (Q1, Q3). **The strongest, and now backed by primary data:** 95% of the
   cost saving came from depreciation and consumables; capex is 11% of depreciation; and the
   selling-expense ratio *rose* (44.3% → 45.2%). **This profit is harvesting the asset base, not
   improving operating efficiency.**
2. **Unit economics are still deteriorating** (Q2). Gross profit per transplant customer −3.5%;
   hair-care repurchase 29.9% → 25.9%.
3. **Whose cash is it, and what is it for.** RMB883.6m with no debt — but **the FY2025 dividend was
   paid out of share premium (note 13), not distributable profits**, since RMB446.9m of accumulated
   losses remains at company level, and **no interim dividend was declared.** Distribution capacity
   is an accounting constraint as much as a willingness one, and returning cash is plainly not this
   management's current priority.
4. **Liquidity and ownership** (Q6, Q7) — a free float of 20.8%.
5. **Compliance tail risk** (Q4).
6. **Management's record** — RMB859m of losses over 2022–24.

**Reason 1 is new, and it is more decisive than the first draft judged.**

**So the right question is no longer "can it grow", nor even "is the giveback justified" — the full
report shows the giveback is justified. The question is what is left when the harvest ends.**

A business with a RMB556m enterprise value, no debt, net cash, and RMB172m of half-year free cash

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Ownership: the fund has not sold a share, and it is almost exactly as large as the
founder.** The interim report gives the exact position at 30 June 2026: **the CPE / CITIC Private
Equity chain (CPEChina Fund II and IIA, held through Panmao Shanghai and CYH at 17.48% each) holds
183,733,336 shares = 34.97%**; **founder Zhang Yu holds 181,531,916 = 34.55%** (The ZY Trust
161,531,916 = 30.74%, plus 20,000,000 = 3.81% via Yunuo); brother **Zhang Hui holds 4.66%**. CITIC
Securities discloses 35.09% long **and a short position of 655,000 shares (0.12%).**

**Three judgements:**
(a) **CPE has not sold a share.** A fund of this vintage still holding in 2026, with the stock ~87%
below its issue price, **cannot exit in the open market at an acceptable price.** That is both an
overhang and a clue: **the exit, when it comes, is more likely to be a negotiated transfer, a block,
or a take-private than open-market selling.**
(b) **There is no controlling shareholder.** 34.97% against 34.55%, 69.5% between them. **Any major
decision needs both to agree, and minorities have no say in any outcome.**
(c) **Both executive directors have pledged stock for loans.** Zhang Yu has 2,250,000 shares pledged
through ZY Investment Capital and Zhang Hui 13,356,321 shares (55% of his holding) to Equities First
Holdings LLC. **Small in size, but the direction is extracting liquidity, not adding to positions.**

> **One clean positive: related-party transactions are essentially nil.** Note 23 discloses only key
> management compensation of **RMB4.64m** (RMB4.29m a year earlier) — no related-party purchases,
> sales or funding. **For a company jointly controlled by a PE fund and a founding family, that is
> an unusually clean disclosure** — the opposite of the family-owned steam supply sitting directly
> on Jiemei's gross margin line elsewhere in this repo. **Profit is not leaking out through related
> parties.**

**Q7 — Share supply and liquidity: the free float is 20.8%.** Adding up what is disclosed or locked
— CPE 34.97% + Zhang Yu 34.55% + Zhang Hui 4.66% + other directors 0.24% + employee share scheme
trust 4.78% + treasury 0.05% = **79.2%** — leaves **about 109m shares, 20.8%**, worth roughly
HK$230m at the current price. That is the arithmetic behind turnover in single-digit millions of
HK$ a day and a 52-week range whose high is 3.3x its low (HK$1.034–3.441). Southbound investors hold
13.745m shares (2.61%) and were net sellers on 15 September 2026. The company repurchased and
cancelled 100,000 shares for about HK$90,100 on 7 April 2025. **Liquidity is not a fundamental
argument, but here it is a quantifiable cost: a 20.8% float explains both why the discount exists
and why any re-rating would be violent — in either direction.**

**Q8 — Medical hair care: all of the group's unit-economics improvement is here, and the
repurchase rate is falling.** The path: FY2024 RMB446m (24.7%) → **FY2025 RMB409m (−8.2%, 22.6%)**
→ H1 2026 RMB227m (**+20.7%**, 23.1%). **The interim report sets this line out clearly, and it is
simultaneously the best and the most suspect part of the result:** segment gross margin jumped from
56.2% to **68.8% (+12.6 points)** and gross profit per customer from RMB2,351 to **RMB2,747
(+16.8%)** — **the only line in the group whose unit economics improved** — contributing 40% of the
RMB126.6m group gross profit increase. **But the ticket fell 4.5% at the same time (RMB4,184 →
RMB3,995), and the package repurchase rate fell from 29.9% to 25.9%.** A business that calls itself
"long-term membership-based hair health management" grew customers 26.4% while repurchase dropped 4
points, which means the increment is overwhelmingly new customers who renew less than the existing
base. **That converts the "second curve" question from "can it scale" to "will what it scales
stick"** — and a 12.6-point margin jump sourced from package mix or recognition timing rather than
real operating leverage will not hold against a falling repurchase rate. Svenson stood at 7
stand-alone stores, unchanged from end-2025.

> **One favourable cross-check:** the interim report puts total consumers served in the period at
> **65,708**, against 40,793 transplant plus 56,757 hair-care service counts = 97,550. **The
> 31,842 difference means about 48% of consumers used both services** — cross-selling is real and
> the rate is high. It is the one arithmetic-verifiable data point in the "diagnose, treat,
> maintain" narrative.

**Ask for:** the specific source of the 12.6-point hair-care margin jump (package mix / recognition
timing / procurement); the contract-liability balance; the reason for the repurchase decline, split
by cohort; and the share of hair-care customers converted from transplant patients.

**Q9 — Compliance: the interim report volunteers its rectification status, and the wording repays
close reading.** The known history includes the February 2024 penalty for "nurses impersonating
doctors to perform hair-transplant surgery"; the Shanghai Yonghe Aimu clinic with 7 administrative
penalties (the last three advertising breaches, one fined ¥150,000); a ¥19,000 fine in Guangzhou;
and penalties at the Taizhou and Beijing entities. **The report's compliance section lists six
rectification items, two of which bear directly on this memo:**

- **"During the Reporting Period, all the surgeries performed at our clinics for our patients were
  conducted *or supervised by* physicians possessing the Aesthetic Medical Attending Physician
  qualification."** Note "**or supervised by**". **This is precisely the structure Q4 identifies:
  the company does not claim every procedure is performed by a physician, but that it is performed
  or supervised by one.** That is a compliant formulation, and it also confirms the capacity model —
  241 doctors at ~338 annualised procedures each works through supervision, not personal operating.
  **This is disclosure, not concealment — but it places the compliance boundary precisely at
  "is the supervision adequate", which cannot be observed from outside.**
- **"As of the end of the Reporting Period, we had a total of 95 leased properties, among which the
  lease agreements of 41 leased properties were duly registered."** **More than half the leases are
  unregistered.** Individually minor, but a reading on internal-control granularity.

The other items: all operating clinics hold practising licences; all but Quanzhou (fire safety) and
Lanzhou (water discharge) have completed those procedures; all clinics have obtained advertisement
examination certificates from local health commissions. The Board states there was **no material
non-compliance** during the period. Set against SAMR's May 2025 advertising guidelines, **a medical
provider running a 45.2% selling-expense ratio still has its customer-acquisition model squarely in
the path of a tightening regulator.**

**Q10 — Competition: an industry that never consolidated.** The top four — Yonghe, Dr.Mai
(大麦), Bosley China (碧莲盛), Xinsheng — hold roughly 30% between them, while **long-tail
independents hold about 45%.** **Dr.Mai first filed in Hong Kong in June 2022, refiled after the
December 2022 lapse, and still has not listed** — which says both that public markets do not like
this model's economics and that **Yonghe's closest competitor has not obtained expansion capital**,
a near-term positive. But the structural problem is unchanged: **low barriers, weak
differentiation, customer acquisition bought through advertising, price as the main competitive
weapon** — which is the root cause of the 20.8% two-year ticket decline, rather than anything
Yonghe did wrong. **Ask what the company knows about competitors' tickets, and where it thinks
its own floor is in a fixed-price price war.**

**Q11 — Capital allocation: the first dividend was paid out of share premium, not profits.** The
December 2021 IPO sold 94.424m shares at HK$15.80 for about HK$1.492bn; RMB859m of losses followed
in 2022–24, and by end-2025 total assets were 46.8% and net assets 53.7% below the listing level.
**The full report adds three decisive details:**

1. **Where the dividend came from.** Note 13: the FY2025 final dividend of ¥0.076, **RMB39.92m in
   total, was "to be paid out of the share premium of the Company"**, approved at the AGM on 19 May
   2026 and paid in June. **RMB446.9m of accumulated losses remain at company level, so distribution
   capacity comes from share premium rather than distributable profits.** That does not make the
   cash less real, but it means **continuing to pay is an accounting question, not only a
   willingness question.**
2. **No interim dividend.** Having earned RMB75.5m in the half with RMB883.6m of cash on hand, the
   Board did not recommend one.
3. **Capital commitments are RMB3.0m.** The three-year "hub-and-satellite" network plan currently
   has **no contracted capex on the balance sheet**; HK$61.3m of IPO net proceeds also remains
   unutilised (to be fully applied by December 2028, with the HK$22.3m brand-awareness tranche
   untouched this period). **A company announcing a three-year build-out has RMB3.0m of contracted
   capex. The expansion has not begun.**

**Put the three together: management is neither expanding nor returning capital at scale — it is
accumulating cash.** That can be discipline (waiting for a better entry point), inertia, or **dry
powder held for some corporate action.** **The fact from Q6 — that CPE has held for nine years,
sold nothing, and cannot exit through the market — means the third reading cannot be dismissed.**

**Q12 — Source quality: the primary record is now complete and the gaps are essentially cleared.**
The first draft relied entirely on secondary reporting; **the full 2026 interim report (pages 1–82)
has now been read.** Every financial, ownership, operating and compliance figure in this memo comes
from that document and has been checked line by line; only FY2025-and-earlier annual data, the
penalty history and industry sizing remain secondary. **Every gap the first draft listed is now

**One genuine unknown remains, and the company will not disclose it: the normalised maintenance
capex.** Capex at 11% of depreciation cannot persist; the normalised level is what determines true
free cash flow, and it has to be inferred from peers or from management, not read off the accounts.

**The credibility problem with the industry forecasts is reinforced by the report itself.** Its

**and the company has quietly swapped the old one out.** The "RMB47.0bn by 2026" forecast no longer
appears in its own documents. **A company changing the industry forecast it cites is worth
remembering: not misconduct, but a sign the old projection could no longer be defended.**

## 4. Provisional read

*Rewritten against the full 2026 interim report. The first draft pointed the right way but two of
its numbers were optimistic; with the whole document read, both the bull and bear cases are stronger
than the draft had them — and they converge on the same conclusion.*

**On the business, this is a harvest, not a turnaround.** That is the hardest sentence to avoid
after reading the full report, and three independent pieces of evidence support it:

1. **Of the 12.9 points of gross margin, 95.0% of the cost saving came from depreciation (−9.5%) and
   consumables (−27.7%)** — both one-off;
2. **Capex is 11% of depreciation** (RMB12.8m against RMB115.4m); net PP&E fell from RMB286.7m to
   RMB250.1m in six months. **Depreciation is falling because the asset base is being consumed and
   barely replaced**;
3. **Selling and marketing rose rather than fell** (44.3% → 45.2% of revenue, +16.3% against revenue
   +13.8%) — **not one yuan of the profit growth came from marketing efficiency.**

Meanwhile **the core segment's unit economics are still deteriorating**: gross profit per transplant
customer −3.5% (the draft's "roughly flat" was too generous), with the ticket down 20.8% in two
years. **The only segment whose unit economics improved is medical hair care — whose package
repurchase rate fell from 29.9% to 25.9% over the same period.** Holding transplant gross profit per
customer through 2027 needs a segment margin of ~75.3%, and the two legs supporting margin are
nearly spent. **Slowing profit growth is therefore not a risk; it is close to arithmetic given this
cost structure.**

**On the stock, the same document supplies equally forceful evidence the other way — stronger than
the first draft could see.** **No interest-bearing debt; cash of RMB743.2m plus term deposits of
RMB140.3m = RMB883.6m, equal to 88% of the market cap; enterprise value of about RMB556m on the
standard definition, 1.53x FY2025 EBITDA; and RMB172.4m of half-year free cash flow after rent and
capex.** Even deducting RMB288.2m of contract liabilities as well, the company is still net cash.
P/B is 1.22x. **At 10x on enterprise value, the market implies normalised profit of about RMB56m —
less than it earned in the half just reported.**

**So the real shape of this investment is clear: a harvest that the market has correctly identified
and may be over-punishing.** The question is no longer whether profit declines (it likely will), but
**what remains when the harvest ends and the asset base needs rebuilding — and who gets the
RMB883.6m.** Both now have concrete observation paths: the first is when capex jumps off RMB12.8m;
the second is the FY2026 dividend decision.

**Governance here is not background, it is the variable.** CPE / CITIC Private Equity holds
**34.97%, nine years in, not a share sold**; founder Zhang Yu **34.55%**; 69.5% between them and
**no controlling shareholder**; a **20.8%** free float; both executive directors pledging stock to
Equities First Holdings; and a first dividend **paid out of share premium**, with no interim
dividend. **A nine-year-old fund that cannot exit through the market, alongside a debt-free investee
holding 88% of its market cap in cash and having just stopped expanding, is a combination that
suggests a possibility — though not a prediction.** In fairness, one thing should be recorded
plainly: **related-party transactions are essentially nil (RMB4.64m of key management pay), so
profit is not leaking to the controllers.**

The honest statement: **a chain harvesting its own asset base to produce reported profit
improvement; its operating quality is worse than the headline numbers and its balance sheet is
better than the price — and this interim report proves both.** The conditions that would flip the
conclusion are now very specific: **a FY2026 transplant segment margin held at 69.5%, the ticket
decline narrowing to under 5%, and capex not jumping sharply.** All three, and the margin gain was
more than a mirror of depreciation, and 1.53x EV/EBITDA is plainly wrong. **Capex jumping while
margin falls back, and H1 2026 was the profit peak of this cycle — and the market was right all
along.**

**Concretely: the public-document research on this name is finished. Three questions remain, and
only time or management access can answer them.** **(1) What is normalised maintenance capex** (it
determines true free cash flow); **(2) what exactly drove the 12.6-point jump in the hair-care
segment margin** (it determines whether the one improving line is credible); **(3) how CPE exits**
(it determines the price at which that 20.8% float is eventually re-rated). **The first two are
management questions; the third can only be waited on. Until the first two are answered this is a
name to start a position in, not to size one in — because the reasons it is cheap are real, and so
is how cheap it is.**
