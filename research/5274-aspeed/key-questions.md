# ASPEED Technology (信驊科技, 5274.TWO) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 22 September 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

ASPEED is **a near-monopoly that consumes almost no capital, returns nearly all of its cash,
is in the strongest upcycle of its history, and has already contracted the next eighteen
months of it**. It does one thing: the server **baseboard management controller (BMC)** —
a chip independent of the main processor that can power-cycle a machine, flash its firmware
and read its temperatures and fan speeds after the operating system has died or the box has
been shut down. It is cheap, it does no computing, and **no server enters a datacentre
without one**. ASPEED's global share of it is roughly **70–80%**.

That position was not grown; it was **bought and then defended**. Founder Lin Hung-ming
started the company in 2004 with about NT$36m after being pushed out of his job in a
mid-career restructuring. In **2016 ASPEED paid US$30m (about NT$960m)** for Broadcom's
**Emulex Pilot** server-management chip business, which lifted BMC unit shipments 30–40%
overnight and handed it the brand-server accounts — Intel, Cisco, Fujitsu, NEC, EMC, Oracle,
Lenovo. **A US$30m purchase ten years ago now supports a roughly US$24bn market cap.**

**So you are not buying an AI growth story — that part has already happened and is partly
under contract.** The company states that **2027 backlog already exceeds full-year 2026
revenue**, which has never happened before. You are buying **how long this level of
earnings persists, and what multiple the market pays for it.** Every question below
converges on that.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Question 1: Where is the unit bridge — 23.7m → 44.6m → 52m chips, against a world that ships about 15m servers a year?

This is the root of everything. Every model and every target price rests on a unit curve
that more than doubles in two years: **23.668m in 2025 → ~31.0–32.7m in 2026 (+31–38%) →
44.6m in 2027 (one house) / over 50m (another)**. Over the same period the company's own
view of the end market is **general-purpose servers growing 5–6% a year, with AI-related
general servers up about 40%**.

**Those two curves do not reconcile unless the number of ASPEED chips per server is rising
structurally.** That is exactly management's own story, and exactly what deserves testing:

- **Rack-scale architectures genuinely raise content.** Public estimates put a GB300 NVL72
  rack at about **71 BMCs** (54 compute trays, 9 switch trays, 6 power shelves, 2 OOB
  switches); GB200 at about **89**; custom AI ASIC servers at about **22**; Vera Rubin NVL72
  at 70–85.
- **But those numbers cannot support the widely quoted 13m "AI-server BMC" figure.** At
  ~70 per rack, 13m units implies about **186,000 AI racks** — an order of magnitude above
  any plausible 2026 estimate for NVL72-class racks. **The "13m AI-server BMCs" number
  almost certainly folds in non-rack AI servers, or companion management silicon, or both.**
  This is not pedantry: it determines whether the growth is **AI** or **general-server
  recovery plus more part numbers per board** — two things with very different durability.
- **The cleanest explanation is management's own:** growth comes from **more ASPEED chips on
  the same board** — BMC (AST2700) plus bridge IC (AST1040) plus security (AST1080) plus
  power sequencing (AST1840). If so, the denominator has changed: **it is no longer "BMCs",
  it is "ASPEED chips"**, and the ratio to server units can exceed 1 without difficulty.

**Ask:**
- **Break the 2026 and 2027 unit forecasts out by part number**: AST2600 / AST2700 / AST2750
  / AST1040 / AST1080 / AST1840. This single disclosure largely settles the question.
- **What is the company's own "ASPEED chips per server / per rack" figure**, split across
  general-purpose, HGX-style AI and rack-scale AI?
- The 2030 TAM was raised from 46.5m to **65.77m units (+41%)**. **How much of that increase
  is more servers, and how much is more chips per server?** The first is customer capex;
  the second is architecture. That is the line between cyclical and structural.
- **How many AI-server BMCs actually shipped in 2025?** Actuals, not forecasts.

### Question 2: The 72% gross margin and the Q4 price increase — pricing power, or a cost pass-through with a timing lag?

Q2 2026 gross margin came in at **72%**, well above the company's own 67–68% guide and an
all-time high; net profit NT$1.841bn at a 47.6% net margin. It is a beautiful number. What
it is made of determines whether it is worth anything.

Two things are happening at once, in opposite directions:

1. **Mix moving up (durable).** AST2700/AST2750 carry materially higher ASPs than AST2600,
   and the company attributed Q3 2025's 69.26% gross margin (+1.38pp QoQ) to high-end BMC
   mix. The ASP path is **US$13.7 (2025) → US$16.1 (2026) → US$21.7 (2027)**, with
   management saying ASP "has a chance to challenge US$20".
2. **Cost pass-through (not durable).** On 4 September the chairman said plainly that
   **because suppliers are raising prices by double digits, ASPEED will raise product prices
   by double digits in Q4**. That is **pass-through, not pricing power**. Pass-through
   reverses when costs reverse, and it inflates the cost base at the same time — it dilutes
   gross margin from both numerator and denominator.

**Buried in the same disclosure is a negative the company volunteered: Q3 gross margin is
guided to 67–68%, four points below Q2's 72%.** That warning came from management, not from
the market.

**So the question is whether 72% is the new normal or a one-quarter peak.** If Q3 lands at
67–68%, a good part of Q2's 72% was timing (FX, costs not yet reflected, single-quarter
mix). If Q3 holds above 70%, the mix explanation stands.

**Ask:**
- **Decompose the move from Q1's 69.18% to Q2's 72% into mix / price / cost.** The three have
  completely different half-lives.
- **The net effect of the Q4 increase:** prices up X%, costs up Y% — what is X minus Y?
  **If X ≈ Y, this is not a price increase, it is a hold.**
- **Are companion chips (BIC/SMC) above or below BMC gross margin?** At the November 2025
  call the company flagged that **"BIC carries lower gross margin, and a rising mix will
  affect blended margin"** — and companion silicon is the main source of the growth story in
  Question 1. **The fastest-growing line carries below-average margin**: a structural
  tension that has to be priced.
- **The employee-compensation accrual and its ratio to pre-tax profit.** See Question 9.

### Question 3: The constraint is supply, not demand — can substrate and packaging go from 2–3m a month to 3.7m?

This is the most under-discussed and most easily verified question in the name. ASPEED's
bottleneck is **not demand and not wafers — it is packaging substrate and assembly/test**:

- The company puts Q3 substrate supply at roughly **2–3m units per month**, rising above
  **3m** once **E-glass** material is qualified, and is **adding a second Taiwanese
  packaging partner** to support Q4 wafer starts.
- Annualised, 2–3m/month is **24–36m units a year**. The 2026 forecast of 31.0–32.7m units
  **sits squarely inside that range — supply is output.**
- **But 44.6m units in 2027 needs about 3.72m a month, and 50m+ needs about 4.17m.** Against
  today's 2–3m that is **24–108% of supply expansion**, in a year when global ABF/BT
  substrate is in broad shortage and on price increases.

**This is why the Q3 revenue guide of NT$4.1–4.3bn was described by the company as
deliberately conservative, and why it was always going to be beaten: July NT$1.524bn plus
August NT$1.626bn is already NT$3.150bn, so September needs only NT$0.95bn to hit the low
end — and August alone was NT$1.626bn.** The information in the guidance is not the number;
**it is how much uncertainty the company has about its own supply.**

**Ask:**
- **What monthly substrate and packaging capacity is contracted for 2027, and how much is
  already locked?** This is the physical ceiling on the whole growth curve.
- **Name the second packaging partner, its qualification date, and its 2027 monthly
  contribution.**
- **Actual yield and unit cost after the E-glass transition.**
- What share of COGS is substrate and packaging? **Which link is the "double-digit supplier
  increase" — substrate, assembly/test, or foundry?**
- If 2027 supply tops out at 3.2m/month (38.4m/year), **do customers wait, or do they
  qualify a second source?** That runs straight into Question 4.

### Question 4: What is the moat made of, and is it still there after 2028?

State the moat honestly first, because it is real: **roughly US$20m of R&D per chip; a
customer qualification cycle of three or four years at best and more than ten at worst;
eight generations of silicon and firmware ecosystem.** BMCs are welded to server
architecture, BIOS and firmware, CSPs work on long-term relationships, and new entrants
find it near-impossible. Customers include Amazon, Microsoft and Meta, plus the Taiwanese
ODMs. **This is not a market anyone breaks into on price.**

But there are four erosion paths, of very unequal strength:

| Threat | Status | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Nuvoton (新唐)** | ~10–15% share, competing on price from the low and mid range; **already the key supplier of Microsoft's SCM (secure control module) BMC**, and pushing **OpenTitan** with Google (already in Chromebooks, datacentre next); 12nm next-gen BMC and CMS due Q4 2026 | **The most concrete of the four.** It has a position at two of the largest CSPs — not in ASPEED's core socket, but **in a socket that widens at the next architecture revision** |
| **Customer in-house silicon** (e.g. Dell) | Self-developed BMCs optimised for their own platforms | **Limited.** In-house BMCs cannot be sold across brands, so R&D and a decade of firmware maintenance never amortise. That is why nobody has succeeded in twenty years |
| **DC-SCM modularisation** (OCP) | Moves BMC, security and control onto a standard module; ASPEED's AST2700 already adopts OCP's **Caliptra** silicon root of trust | **Double-edged, currently positive.** Modularity lowers the design barrier for OEMs — **and lowers the cost of changing supplier.** ASPEED benefits from the standard today, but standardisation is the prelude to commoditisation |
| **China localisation** | The company attributed a Q4 2025 beat (NT$2.44bn vs NT$2.0–2.1bn guided) partly to **strong mainland Chinese customer demand** | **The least visible of the four.** Chinese customers are contributors today; under localisation policy they are also **the revenue most likely to disappear without warning** — and the company has never disclosed revenue by region |

**The 2028 visibility gap is real, and the company says so itself:** 2027 demand is locked
and backlog exceeds full-year 2026 revenue, **but "customers have not yet formally ordered
for 2028; design wins are coming in."** **A design win is not an order.** In an industry
that just demonstrated it can halve its own profit in twelve months, "designed in but not
ordered" is a state that deserves a discount, not an extrapolation.

**Ask:**
- **Revenue by region** (Americas / China / Japan-Korea / Europe) over three years.
  **What is China?**
- **Top-five customer concentration** and its trend; the split between direct CSP sales and
  shipments through ODMs.
- Which platform and which socket did Nuvoton win at Microsoft's SCM? Is ASPEED still on the
  same board?
- **How many 2028 design wins, by platform and customer, and what annual unit volume do they
  represent? What is the historical design-win-to-order conversion rate and lag?**
- Have Chinese customers begun **dual-sourcing** domestic BMC alternatives?

### Question 5: What has to be true for NT$743.7bn to make sense?

Do not forecast into the valuation — **reverse it.** Take the market cap and solve for the
profit each terminal multiple requires:

| Exit P/E | Required net profit (NT$100m) | Required EPS | x FY2025 | Implied revenue | Implied units (m) | **% of ASPEED's own 2030 TAM** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25x | 297 | 715 | 7.6x | 626 | 84 | **127%** |
| **30x** | **248** | **596** | **6.3x** | **522** | **70** | **106%** |
| 35x | 212 | 511 | 5.4x | 447 | 60 | 91% |
| 40x | 186 | 447 | 4.7x | 391 | 52 | 79% |
| **45x** | **165** | **397** | **4.2x** | **348** | **46** | **71%** |
| 50x | 149 | 358 | 3.8x | 313 | 42 | 63% |
| 55x | 135 | 325 | 3.4x | 285 | 38 | 58% |

*Implied revenue at the 47.5% 1H26 net margin; implied units at a US$24.6 ASP (NT$750,
another 48% above 2026); TAM denominator is ASPEED's own upgraded 2030 estimate of
65.77m units.*

**Read it directly:**

- **At 30x, the price requires 70m units — more than the entire 2030 market the company
  itself forecasts.** Not available.
- **At 45x, the required EPS of NT$397 sits at the top of the 2027 sell-side range
  (328–408).** So **today's price ≈ 2027 consensus delivered at the high end, capitalised at
  45x, undiscounted, with no growth thereafter.**
- This matches what the sell-side openly says: one house uses **55x the 2027–28E average**
  for 22,000; another uses **40–50x 2028E**. **Nobody is hiding it — the market is publicly
  paying a near-record multiple on 2028 earnings.**

**So the real question is not whether earnings arrive; it is whether the multiple holds.**
That is what makes this name different from most growth stories, and it has to be said
plainly:

> **Modelled bottom-up from units x ASP x gross margin, the base case gives FY2026 EPS of
> NT$200 and FY2027 of NT$361 — against consensus of NT$202 and NT$368
> (see [forecast-2026-2028.zh.md](forecast-2026-2028.zh.md)). I cannot manufacture a
> disagreement with the market on earnings. The disagreement can only be about the multiple.**
>
> Base-case 2028 EPS is NT$439. At NT$17,885 that is **40.8x**. The exit-multiple
> sensitivity dominates everything: 30x gives 13,159 (−26%), 35x gives 15,353 (−14%),
> 40x gives 17,546 (−2%), 45x gives 19,739 (+10%). **Within that range your view on the
> multiple matters roughly five times more than your view on units.**

**Ask yourself, not the company:**
- What multiple does a business that has shown it can lose 39.9% of revenue in a year deserve
  at a cyclical peak? **Even at the 2023 trough the market paid close to 90x trailing** —
  ASPEED has never been cheap, and today's ~131x sits at the **93rd percentile of its own
  history**.
- **If 2028 brings a repeat of 2023** (the bear case here), EPS returns to NT$152 while the
  multiple returns to 20x, and the price is NT$3,039. **That is not an imagined tail — it is
  what happened three years ago.**
- Conversely, if rack-scale content and companion silicon carry 2028 EPS to NT$573 and the
  market still pays 45x, the price is NT$25,803. **The two ends differ by more than 8x.
  At this price, position size matters more than direction.**

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Forecasting discipline: this management under-promises, and that is an asset worth
pricing.** It belongs first because it changes how you read everything else the company says.
The verifiable record:

| Period | Guidance | Actual | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q4 2025 revenue | NT$2.0–2.1bn | **NT$2.44bn** | **16% above the top end** |
| Q2 2026 gross margin | 67–68% | **72%** | **4pp above the top end** |
| Q3 2026 revenue | NT$4.1–4.3bn | Jul + Aug already NT$3.150bn | **September needs only NT$0.95bn for the low end; August alone was NT$1.626bn** |

**This is the opposite of most growth stocks: management systematically understates itself.**
Two implications: (a) FY2026 consensus of NT$202 is **more likely to be revised up than
down** — the FactSet median has in fact moved 174.98 → 185.71 → 188.19 → 194.98 → 200.90 →
202.31, **monotonically upward**; and (b) precisely because of that, **"beats consensus" is
already priced**. It is no longer a reason to buy, only a condition of standing still.

**Q7 — Cycle position and normalised earnings: 2023 is the most important year in this memo.**
The company has run a complete cycle: 2022 peak (NT$5.21bn revenue, NT$2.11bn profit) →
**2023 trough (NT$3.13bn, NT$1.01bn; −39.9% and −52.2%)** → 2024 (NT$6.46bn, NT$2.57bn,
revenue +106%) → 2025 (NT$9.08bn, NT$3.93bn) → 1H26 (NT$7.02bn, NT$3.26bn). Note what 2023
was: **ASPEED lost no share, lost to no competitor, and had no product displaced — CSPs
simply cut traditional server capex for twelve months.** A business with an intact moat can
halve its profit. **That is the boundary of the phrase "structural growth" in this case.**
The 2H26 run rate annualises to roughly NT$17–18bn of revenue; if the normalised mid-cycle
is NT$12–14bn of revenue and NT$5.5–6.5bn of profit, today's price is **114–135x normalised
earnings**.

**Q8 — FX: it already happened once, in Q2 2025, and nobody models it.** ASPEED sells in USD
and pays most of its costs and payroll in NT$. The evidence is in the 2025 numbers:
**Q2 2025 revenue of NT$2.247bn was 8.8% above Q1's NT$2.065bn, yet EPS fell from NT$23.46
to NT$16.65, −29.0% QoQ.** Revenue up, profit down a third — the likeliest explanation is
the sharp appreciation of the New Taiwan dollar in May 2025 and the resulting FX loss, but
**the non-operating detail for that quarter could not be obtained here; this is an inference
and must be checked against the filing.** If it is right, it means something important:
**a company with a 46% net margin and essentially no operating leverage risk can still lose
a third of a quarter's profit to the currency.** In building 2027 EPS of NT$361–408, the FX
assumption is badly under-weighted — the model here holds 30.5 NT$/US$ flat for three years,
which is itself an assumption that deserves challenge.

**Q9 — Employee compensation: a structural ceiling on operating leverage, at the company that
pays Taiwan's highest salaries.** Taiwan's Company Act requires the articles of incorporation
to specify a fixed amount or ratio of annual profit to be distributed as employee
compensation (1–15% in practice), with director compensation capped at 5%. **Operating
expense is therefore not fixed — it scales with pre-tax profit, which caps operating leverage
arithmetically.** ASPEED has been reported as paying the highest employee salaries in Taiwan
and is expanding (a Kaohsiung R&D centre opened in December 2025, which the chairman called
"not a short-term expansion but part of a long-term R&D footprint").
**Not obtained here: the charter ratio, the FY2025 accrual, and its share of pre-tax profit.**
This directly determines whether operating margin can keep expanding the way sell-side models
assume — the base case here has opex falling from ~16.0% of revenue in 2025 to 13.5% in 2027,
**and if the employee-compensation mechanism does not permit that, base-case EPS comes down.**

**Q10 — Founder, governance and Cupola360: an operator who has been right for twenty years,
and one newly created alignment question.** Lin Hung-ming joined SiS in 1993 and built its
multimedia product R&D team; in May 2003 he led that division out to found XGI Technology as
general manager; the 2004 UMC group restructuring left him unemployed in mid-career, and he
founded ASPEED with about NT$36m. He then executed the 2016 Broadcom Emulex Pilot acquisition
that made ASPEED number one. Distinguished alumnus of NYCU (2020) and NTHU (2021), Information
Month award (2021), ERSO Award (2023), and *Institutional Investor* Best CEO in Asia in both
2023 and 2024. **This is an unusually solid record and should not be discounted.**

What needs pricing is **Cupola360**: on 5 November 2025 the board approved carving the
RRM/Smart AV business into a wholly owned subsidiary, effective 31 December 2025;
**Lin Hung-ming chairs it** with Hsieh Cheng-ju as president; the company says it **will
release some shares to employees as incentive, targets breakeven by end-2026, and an IPO
within two to three years.** The carve-out itself did not affect ASPEED shareholders (still
100% owned), but **once shares start being released, ASPEED shareholders' economic interest
is diluted while the chairman sits on both sides.** This is not an allegation — group
carve-outs are common in Taiwan and often correct, and separating a pre-profit incubation
business has its own logic. **But the release percentage, the pricing basis and whether the
chairman personally holds shares must be asked before the IPO is priced.**

**Q11 — Share supply: the one genuine information gap in this memo.** Known: (a) the March
2026 capital-reserve capitalisation raised the share count 10%, and **that dilution is fully
reflected in the 41.583m count and the restated EPS**; (b) directors/supervisors hold 20.39%
and all insiders 20.57% (July 2026); (c) Cupola360 will release shares to employees (dilution
at the subsidiary level). **Not obtained, and therefore a question: have insiders filed any
transfers during the move from 7,085 to 19,880? What is foreign ownership and how has it
moved over six months?** On a stock up 143% year-to-date at the 93rd percentile of its own
P/E history, **insider behaviour is the cheapest piece of information available, and I do not
have it.**

**Q12 — Balance sheet: barely a risk, but confirm one thing.** Q4 2025 total assets were
NT$10.33bn. The company is fabless, capital expenditure is minimal, the cash payout ratio is
77%, and there is no known bank debt or convertible. **This balance sheet will not break.**
The one line worth watching is **inventory**: in an environment of constrained supply,
customers scrambling for allocation and a Q4 price increase, **double booking** in the channel
is a real risk — it was the direct cause of 2023. **Ask for the last six quarters of inventory
and days of inventory, and for the company's read on customer-side inventory levels.**

**Q13 — Source quality and disclosure gaps.** Every Taiwanese primary disclosure channel
(MOPS/公開資訊觀測站, TPEx, the company IR site, Goodinfo, StatementDog, MoneyDJ, cnyes,
Commercial Times, Economic Daily) **is blocked by this environment's egress proxy**, so all
figures come from secondary summaries. Three reconciliation conflicts were found and are
flagged in the text: (a) **Q2 2026's "EPS 40.87, +9% QoQ" contradicts "net profit NT$1.841bn,
+30% QoQ"** — resolved here as **restated Q1 34.01 / Q2 44.27, summing to the reported 1H
78.28**, with the two press figures mixing old and new share bases; (b) **the quoted 2025 ASP
of US$13.7 is ~13% above revenue ÷ units (NT$373 = US$12.0)**, so the model calibrates to
reported revenue; (c) **the "13m AI-server BMCs" figure fails a rack-count mass balance**
(Question 1). **Not obtained, and therefore questions: revenue by region and customer,
shipments by part number, the employee-compensation ratio and amount, non-operating detail

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **September revenue (around 10 October — imminent).** The cheapest test available.
   **At ≥NT$1.65bn**, Q3 is about NT$4.8bn, roughly **12% above the top of the company's own
   NT$4.1–4.3bn guide**, and the substrate bottleneck has materially eased. **Below
   NT$1.4bn** would say August contained pull-forward and supply is still binding.
2. **Q3 results and the earnings call (around November; last year's was 24 November).**
   Settles Questions 2 and 3 at once. Watch: **whether gross margin holds the 67–68% guided
   floor** (which determines what Q2's 72% was made of); **explicit Q4 revenue and price
   increase guidance**; **whether monthly substrate supply has passed 3m**; and whether the
   second packaging partner is named.
3. **The actual landing of the Q4 price increase.** The point is not whether prices rose, but
   **the increase net of cost inflation**. Gross margin back above 70% in Q1 2027 is pricing
   power; still 67–68% is pass-through.
4. **Quantification of the 2027 backlog.** All that exists today is the qualitative "exceeds
   full-year 2026 revenue". **Get the number: value, units, cancellability, and how far into
   the year it runs.** A non-cancellable order and a rolling forecast differ by an order of
   magnitude in value.
5. **The first 2028 design win converting to an order.** The company says customers have not
   yet formally ordered. **The date of the first 2028 order is the day visibility extends from
   two years to three** — the single highest-value positive event in this name.
6. **AST2750 ramp and next-gen AST1040 production preparation in H1 2027.** The companion-chip
   attach rate is the key assumption in the base case's 2027 revenue (12% of BMC revenue).
7. **The FY2026 annual report (around March 2027).** The only place to obtain **revenue by
   region, customer concentration, the employee-compensation amount and non-operating detail**.
   Questions 4, 8 and 9 all close here.
8. **Cupola360's share-release plan and its pricing.** The answer to Question 10, and it has
   to be seen before the IPO.
9. **Nuvoton's BMC revenue in absolute terms and the Microsoft SCM ramp.** Today there is only
   "BMC revenue targeting +50%". **If Nuvoton's BMC revenue reaches the 10% level of ASPEED's
   by 2027, the competitive narrative has to be rewritten.**
10. **A turn in CSP capex guidance.** The only variable that can hit EPS and the multiple in
    the same quarter. In September 2026 the market was already discussing "AI leaders calling
    for slower investment" — **watch the capex language, not the share price.**
11. **The New Taiwan dollar.** A repeat of May 2025's sharp appreciation means looking first
    at the Q2 2025 precedent (Question 8).

## 4. Provisional read

**As a business it is very good, and currently in an exceptional state — that is not in
dispute.** A 70–80% global share, 68–72% gross margin, 46% net margin, roughly 65% ROE,
almost no capital expenditure, a 77% cash payout and fifteen consecutive years of dividends.
The moat is real: ~US$20m of R&D per chip, three-to-ten-year qualification cycles, eight
generations of firmware ecosystem, and a customer list — Amazon, Microsoft, Meta — no entrant
can replicate. Rack-scale AI genuinely raises management-silicon content per machine, and the
companion chips (BIC/SMC/security) genuinely raise ASPEED dollars per board.
**Backlog for 2027 already exceeding full-year 2026 revenue is a state this company has never
been in.** And management has a scarce quality: **it systematically guides low and delivers
high** — 67–68% gross margin guided, 72% delivered; NT$2.0–2.1bn guided for Q4 2025,
NT$2.44bn delivered. **That is the opposite of most growth stocks and should be priced as an
asset.**

**As a stock the answer is much harder, and hard in an unusual way.** Modelled bottom-up from
units x ASP x gross margin, the base case gives FY2026 EPS of NT$200 and FY2027 of NT$361 —
**essentially identical to consensus at NT$202 and NT$368. I cannot disagree with the market
on earnings.** The disagreement can only live in one place: **the multiple.** And there the
market is not hiding what it is doing — one European house prices off **55x the 2027–28E
average**, another off **40–50x 2028E**. At NT$17,885 you are paying **~131x trailing,
~89x 2026E, ~49x 2027E and ~41x my base-case 2028E**, with the P/E at the **93rd percentile
of its own history**.

**Two specific, opposite conditions would flip the conclusion:**

- **Up:** the first **formal 2028 order**, combined with **Q4 gross margin back above 70%
  after the price increase** (proving pricing power rather than pass-through). Together those
  extend visibility from two years to three, and the multiple the market pays for three years
  of visibility is not the multiple it pays for two.
- **Down:** **Q3 gross margin below 67%**, or **a turn in CSP capex guidance**. Note the
  asymmetry — **the multiple does not need an earnings miss to compress.** The lesson of 2023
  is not that ASPEED lost to anyone; it is that **a business with a fully intact moat halved
  its profit in twelve months without losing a single customer.**

The honest statement: **this is an excellent business in a real upcycle that is already partly
contracted, at a price that requires the upcycle to continue past 2028 and the market to keep
paying 40–55x.** The earnings risk is modest; **the multiple risk is the entire risk** — and
the multiple is the one variable in this memo that does not depend on the company doing
anything right.

**Concrete next step: wait for September revenue (around 10 October) and the Q3 call (around
November).** The first takes ten minutes to check and settles Question 3 (is the supply
constraint gone). The second settles Question 2 (what 72% was made of) and gives the net
effect of the Q4 price increase. **Until the nature of the gross margin is established, size
any position against "the multiple could return to 30x", not against "will earnings hit the
number"** — because earnings will probably hit the number, and that is not where the risk in
this investment lives.
