# 不同集团 (Butong Group / BeBeBus, 06090.HK) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 23 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

The prospectus describes Butong Group as "the best-selling premium durable nursery products
brand in China by GMV." **That description stopped being accurate in 2025.** Durable
categories — strollers, car seats, baby carriers, cribs, sleep sacks — generated roughly
¥656m in 2025, down about 17% from roughly ¥794m in 2024. All of the growth came from
**diapers and wipes, which the company does not manufacture** (+61.0%), and from a
low-base feeding category (+147.5%). You are not buying a premium durable-goods company
expanding; you are buying a **premium durable-goods company using outsourced fast-moving
consumer goods to fill the hole that the birth curve is cutting in its durable business** —
wrapped in a stock with a ~12% free float that rose 44% on day one and is down roughly 75%
from its February high.

**Revenue by scenario (2025)**

| Scenario | 2025 revenue | YoY | Share | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hygiene care (diapers, pull-ups, wipes) | **¥625m** | **+61.0%** | 43.2% | **Entirely third-party manufactured** (Shandong Duoku, Hangzhou Ke'ai, Chongqing Baiya). FMCG, repeat-purchase driven |
| Travel (strollers, car seats, carriers) | ~¥463m | derived decline | **32%** (2022: **64%**) | The founding category; car seats and high chairs are made in-house |
| Sleep (cribs, sleep sacks, pillows) | ~¥194m (residual) | derived decline | ~13.4% | Derived by residual; not separately disclosed |
| Feeding (high chairs, tableware) | **¥165m** | **+147.5%** | 11.4% | 2024 base only ~¥67m |

**The durable-goods arithmetic (this memo's core derivation, checkable):** hygiene 43.2% +
feeding 11.4% = 54.6%, so travel + sleep = 45.4% × ¥1,446m = **¥656m**. For 2024: hygiene
625/1.61 = ¥388m, feeding 165/2.475 = ¥67m, so travel + sleep = 1,249 − 455 = **¥794m**.
**YoY −17.4%.** The derivation relies on only three disclosed numbers (total revenue; the
hygiene share and growth rate; the feeding amount and growth rate) and is insensitive to how
travel and sleep split between themselves — the combined figure is the same either way.

**2H2025 revenue ≈ ¥720m, roughly +8.0% YoY**. +24.7% then +8.0% — the 2025 deceleration
happened in the second half, before any 2026 macro excuse applies.

**Market:** approximately **90,285,500 shares** outstanding (June 2026 monthly return).
The most recent verifiable quote in this environment is **~HK$42.56 on 13 July 2026**;
a separate snapshot shows a market cap of about **HK$3.88bn** (implying ~HK$43). At
HK$3.88bn and HK$1 ≈ ¥0.91, market cap is about **¥3.53bn**: roughly **26x** 2025 adjusted
earnings and about **17.8x** the sell-side 2026 estimate. P/B cannot be derived from
reachable sources. **Sell-side coverage appears to be a single house: Shenwan Hongyuan
initiated Buy on 3 April 2026**, forecasting 2026–2028 net profit of **¥198m / ¥259m /
¥325m** (+45.8% / +31.0% / +25.5%), applying 38x 2026 P/E for a target market cap of
**HK$8.4bn**.

> **The single fact that frames everything:** China recorded **7.92m births in 2025, down
> 1.62m or −17.0%**. In the same year, Butong's durable revenue (travel + sleep) fell from
> about ¥794m to about ¥656m — **−17.4%**. The two numbers are essentially identical. In the
> categories the brand was built on, the company in 2025 neither beat nor lost to the
> population — **it *was* the population**; every point of the 15.8% top-line growth came
> from outsourced diapers and wipes, the products it does not make and where its moat is
> thinnest. The bull case is that "audience brand + AI" lets the company escape its
> denominator. The bear case needs one sentence: **the denominator falls again next year.**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1: Is this a premium durable brand, or is it becoming a white-label diaper company?

Everything else derives from this. In four years, travel fell from **64% of revenue to 32%**
and hygiene care rose from **8.3% to 43.2%**. The company still speaks the language of
durable goods — design, spinal support, safety certification, 223 domestic patents — about a
business that now takes **43%** of its revenue from diapers and wipes made by Shandong Duoku,
Hangzhou Ke'ai, Chongqing Baiya and other contract manufacturers.

The two readings differ by an order of magnitude in valuation terms:

- **Reading A (bull):** BeBeBus has turned "upper-middle-class new parents" into an asset.
  Durables are customer acquisition; care products are monetisation. A 53.3% private-domain
  repeat rate, ~657k customers reached, 2m+ online members — this is audience economics, so
  the denominator is wallet share per household, not births.
- **Reading B (bear):** once durable growth stopped, the company walked into one of China's
  most brutal FMCG categories — diapers, against P&G, Kimberly-Clark, Babycare, KUB and a
  long tail of ODM white labels — trying to hold a brand premium on an outsourced product.
  No Chinese company has sustained that for long.

**Ask:**
- **Gross margin by scenario**, not the blended number. If hygiene margin is materially
  *below* travel, the mix shift is diluting earnings quality while in-house car seats hold
  the blended figure up. If hygiene margin is *above* (the 1H2026 alert's reference to
  "higher-margin products contributing more" hints at this), then the next question is:
  **why can an outsourced diaper sustain a 50%+ gross margin, and for how many years?**
- **Repeat-purchase rate and customer acquisition cost for the care category**, disclosed
  separately from durables. Diaper economics live on repeat purchase, not on social seeding.
- **Realised price bands and discounting for care products.** Check actual transaction prices
  on Tmall and Douyin against list prices, and the depth of the 2026 618 discounts — the
  fastest external test of whether "premium" survives.
- **2025 revenue and YoY for travel and sleep individually.** The company has disclosed only
  the percentage mix, never the absolute decline in durables. **That non-disclosure is itself
  information.**

### Q2: After 7.92m births, what is normalised durable revenue?

Demand for strollers, car seats and cribs is set almost entirely by **the number of babies
born this year and last**, and that number is contracting unusually fast: 9.02m in 2023 →
9.54m in 2024 (Dragon-year rebound) → **7.92m in 2025**, a birth rate of 5.63‰. 2026 works
off the 7.92m base, and the one-off Dragon-year rebound has already been given back.

This is not a consumption-downgrade story; it is a **denominator** story. Even if BeBeBus
keeps taking share, durable revenue can fall for several consecutive years. Its 2024 share of
premium durable nursery products was **4.9%** (and 4.2% of the broader premium nursery
market, ranking second) — a low base means theoretical room, but it also means **the easy
share gains of the last four years have already been banked, and the pool is now shrinking.**

**Ask:**
- Management's **absolute guidance for travel and sleep revenue in 2026 and 2027** — not
  mix percentages, not "structural optimisation."
- **Lifetime-value decomposition**: what a new customer contributes in durables, what in care
  products, and over how many months. The audience-brand thesis rests entirely on these three
  numbers.
- **Evidence of age extension** beyond 0–3 (car seats run to 12, high chairs and beds to 6).
  Without it, the company must re-acquire a cohort that is 17% smaller every year.
- 2026 maternal-infant spending shows a structural split — **necessities trading up,
  discretionary trading down** — and durables sit squarely on the discretionary side. Verify
  whether durable ASPs rose or fell in 2025–2026.

### Q3: How much of reported profit is actually operating?

The positive profit alert of mid-August 2026 (reported as 13 August) guides 1H2026 profit
attributable to equity holders **up more than 90%** (1H2025: ~¥48.5m, implying **≥ ~¥92m**).
It gives four reasons: **higher gross profit; higher other income and net gains (government
grants and interest income); lower listing expenses; lower interest on redeemable preferred
shares.**

Only the first is operating:

| Item | Nature | Recurs? |
|---|---|---|
| Higher gross profit | Operating | Yes |
| Government grants | Non-recurring | Uncertain, and tied to the new plant |
| Interest income | Non-operating | Yes, while the HK$718m of IPO proceeds lasts |
| Lower listing expenses | One-time roll-off | **No** — 2026 is the last year of this base effect |
| Lower preferred-share interest | One-time roll-off | **No** — preferreds converted at listing |

**The alert does not mention revenue growth at all.** A positive profit alert that itemises
gross profit, grants, interest and expense bases while saying nothing about the top line is
the single most cautionary detail in this memo.

**Ask:**
- **1H2026 revenue and YoY** — the interim results answer this within days. If revenue growth
  has fallen to single digits, "+90% profit" is an accounting base event that cannot repeat
  in 2027.
- **The absolute figure for other income and net gains**, split between grants and interest.
  On roughly ¥650m of IPO proceeds, 1H2026 interest income is plausibly tens of millions of
  RMB — **not immaterial against ¥92m of profit.**
- **A full bridge from reported to adjusted net profit.** In 2024 the gap was ¥58.5m vs
  ¥111m — nearly ¥53m of listing expenses, share-based payment and preferred-share interest.
  **From 2026 the two measures should converge; if they do not, share-based payment has
  become a permanent cost.**
- **Operating cash flow against net profit**, plus inventory and receivables. This business
  has two opposing cash characteristics: online direct sales collect cash up front, while
  3,400 third-party stores consume it. Only the cash flow statement says which dominates.

### Q4: Cyannova, Caocao, the robotics subsidiary — business, or share-price engineering?

Within a year of listing the company did three things whose relationship to the operating
business needs testing:

1. **21 May 2026**: a strategic cooperation framework agreement with **Cyannova Capital**, a
   newly launched New York investment firm. Per public disclosure, **Cyannova undertakes to
   increase its holding in the company by no less than US$20m through open-market purchases
   within one year of signing.** The shares opened more than 6% higher on the news.
2. **18/24 June 2026**: a strategic cooperation framework agreement with Caocao Mobility
   (02643.HK) covering deployment of child car seats in ride-hailing, followed by media
   claims that "full deployment across its near-one-million vehicle network would add several
   hundred million RMB of potential revenue."
3. **April 2026**: incorporation of a smart-robotics subsidiary; the 2026 mission was restated
   as "redefining premium family living with AI," with AI upgrades announced for strollers,
   car seats, cribs and high chairs.

Item 1 is highly unusual in a developed market: **a listed company signing an agreement whose
substance is that a fund will buy its stock.** It contributes nothing to the income statement;
its only object is the share price. Items 2 and 3 may be genuine strategy, but their timing —
after durable revenue turned down and the shares fell roughly 75% from the high — requires
investors to treat **narrative migration as an independent variable**, not as good news to be
taken at face value.

**Ask:**
- Does the Cyannova agreement constitute a notifiable or connected transaction under the
  Listing Rules? **Has the buying happened, how much, and has any disclosure-of-interests
  threshold been crossed?** As of writing, no record of actual Cyannova purchases could be
  found in this environment. **Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — but it is the
  first item to verify.**
- The **specific purchase orders, pricing and settlement terms** under the Caocao agreement.
  Framework agreements are a cheap signal in the Chinese market; demand a signed amount, or an
  explicit statement that there is none.
- The robotics subsidiary's **registered capital, team provenance, 2026 budget and
  capitalisation policy**. R&D intensity fell from 3.2% of revenue in 2022 to 1.8% in 2024 —
  **on what basis does a company with falling R&D build robots?** If the answer is "we will
  raise spending sharply," the question becomes when margins take the hit.
- The company's own **revenue-recognition definition and 2026 target for AI products.** If AI
  revenue in 2026 is zero, this is a valuation story, not a business.

### Q5: What has to be true for a HK$3.9bn market cap?

Do not forecast into the valuation; reverse it.

Market cap of about **HK$3.88bn ≈ ¥3.53bn**. Apply an **18x** terminal multiple — neutral-to-
generous for a Chinese consumer brand whose growth engine is now outsourced FMCG and whose
end market shrinks annually — and the required net profit is **about ¥196m**.

**That is almost exactly the sell-side 2026 estimate of ¥198m.**

So the price says something fairly clear, and unlike many Hong Kong IPOs, **it is not
expensive**:

| Terminal P/E | Required net profit | Multiple of 2025 adjusted |
|---|---|---|
| 12x | ¥294m | 2.17x |
| 15x | ¥235m | 1.73x |
| **18x** | **¥196m** | **1.45x** (≈ 2026 consensus) |
| 25x | ¥141m | 1.04x |

**The market is currently paying for "the sell-side 2026 number, at a normal multiple, then
zero growth."** It is paying nothing for the AI story, nothing for robotics, nothing for the
audience brand. The risk is not in the multiple — **it is in the numerator**:

- If ¥30m or more of the ¥92m 1H2026 profit comes from interest and grants, sustainable
  operating profit is roughly ¥60m, annualising to ¥120–150m, and the current market cap is
  **24–29x** — no longer cheap.
- If durables keep shrinking ~15% a year while care growth decays from 61% toward 20–30% (the
  inevitable path in a red ocean), 2027 revenue growth approaches zero, 18x becomes 12x, and
  the downside is **30–35%**.
- Conversely, if care can double again while holding margin and durables stabilise, ¥198m is a
  starting point and Shenwan Hongyuan's HK$8.4bn target (38x 2026) is not absurd — **but that
  requires 38x, a multiple this stock printed once in February 2026 and then gave back 75%
  over five months.**

**The real question is not "is it cheap" but "what is the ¥198m made of, and which way does it
go after 2027."**

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — A 12% free float is the root of every price anomaly here.** The IPO sold only
**10,980,900 shares**; public float is about **12.1%**; cornerstones took ~1,642,500 shares
(~US$15m: Shanghai Tongyi + Huatai Capital, Great Praise Investment SPC–Selected AI Fund SP,
Cithara Global Multi-Strategy SPC–Bosideng Industry Investment Fund SP). Pre-IPO ownership was
highly concentrated: **Wang Wei held 52.95% through WANGBOYAN**, Shen Ling 6.77%, WEILING
(Futu Trust) 5.74%, Tiantu 9.24%, Gaorong 8.82% + 0.98%, Tembusu (Matrix Partners China)
7.84%, Taikang Life 5.15%.

The price history is a supply history: **listed 23 September 2025, +44% on debut → 5 January
2026, down more than 26% intraday and briefly through the HK$71.20 offer price (market cap
below HK$6.5bn) → 13 February added to the Hang Seng Composite series, 9 March added to Stock
Connect → high of the listing in late February (secondary quote sources show HK$138.30 on
23 February and HK$115.50 on 7 March; separately, media report a ~75% drawdown from a 20
February high, which reconciles only if that high was intraday at roughly HK$170 — verify
against primary quote data) → sharp fall in late March → +50% intraday on the annual results
in mid-April, −16.17% the next day → ~HK$42.56 in July.**

The timing matters: **the standard six-month post-IPO lock-up expired on 23 March 2026**, and
the company bought back shares on **24, 25, 26 and 31 March** (HK$4.527m/61,600 shares;
HK$3.913m/54,800 shares; HK$1.522m/21,200 shares; HK$10,300/200 shares — the 25 March range
was HK$69.65–78.60). Before that, on 2 and 25 February, shareholders deposited shares worth
about **HK$415m** and **HK$577m** into Futu Securities International (HK). **Depositing shares
into a broker is normally a precursor to selling.**

**Ask:** who sold after March and how much (disclosure of interests and CCASS movements);
**the controlling shareholder's intentions after the second milestone at twelve months, i.e.
23 September 2026** — one month away; and how much the company spent on buybacks and on what
basis. Under HK$10m of buying cannot offset an unlock on a ~12% float; **its signalling value
far exceeds its mechanical value.**

**Q7 — Growth was bought: selling costs above 30% of revenue, and they are not reversible.**
Selling and distribution ran ¥189m / ¥286m / ¥391m in 2022–2024, or **37.2% / 33.5% / 31.3%**
of revenue, and ¥224m (~30.9%) in 1H2025. **At least 70% goes to online platform promotion,
offline promotion and new-media marketing**; the company works with **more than 16,000
influencers**, over 20 of them with a million-plus followers, and the #bebebus topic on
Xiaohongshu has over 100m views. The ratio is falling — good — but it fell while revenue
growth went from 46.6% to 15.8%, which means **cutting spend and losing growth are the same
event.** Split selling costs into brand, performance and channel rebates, and demand a **CAC
time series**. For a brand built on content seeding, the biggest risk is not a competitor; it
is rising platform traffic costs and the fading of the seeding advantage. Note a conflict of
denominators: one source gives 2024 selling costs of ¥391m (31.3%), another ¥271m (30.6%) —
the latter is almost certainly 9M2024. **Both need checking against the prospectus and annual
report.**

**Q8 — Contract manufacturing and quality control are the weakest link in the premium
narrative.** Apart from car seats and high chairs, the main categories — above all the diapers
and wipes that are 43% of revenue — are outsourced. Media reports have alleged that **some
contract manufacturers' registered addresses belong to other companies and that hygiene
licence information is not publicly disclosed**, alongside persistent complaint coverage on
design and quality. Meanwhile **a new plant in Fenghua, Ningbo is scheduled to begin operating
in 2H2026** — the company is moving from asset-light to asset-heavy. **Ask:** the plant's
capex, capacity, categories and depreciation policy (this hits 2027 gross margin directly);
top-five supplier concentration; and whether any recall or failed regulatory inspection has
occurred. **For an infant brand selling safety, one serious quality event is a valuation
reset, not a one-off charge.**

**Q9 — Competition: a 4.9% share is both the opportunity and the fragility.** Ranking first in
premium durable nursery products means **4.9%** of GMV; second overall in premium nursery means
**4.2%**. This is a highly fragmented market where "number one" carries limited weight.
Competitors span three groups: imported premium (Stokke, Bugaboo, Cybex), domestic incumbents
(Goodbaby), and fellow content-native challengers (Babycare, KUB). **Diapers are worse**: P&G,
Kimberly-Clark, Babycare, KUB, plus capacity holders such as Hanhe and Baiya that run both ODM
and own-brand — and Baiya is simultaneously one of Butong's contract manufacturers. **Ask** for
a **share time series** by category, not a one-off ranking, and for price-band movement after
the 2026 618 festival.

**Q10 — Globalisation: target markets are North America, Europe and Southeast Asia.** This is
the least certain use of IPO proceeds. Child car seats are heavily regulated abroad
(FMVSS 213, ECE R129): long certification cycles, high liability, and own-brand entry takes
years of spending. **Ask** for 2025 overseas revenue in absolute terms and as a share (if it is
not separately disclosed, that itself indicates how small it is), certification progress by
market, and tariff assumptions. **With domestic durables shrinking, overseas is the only path
that makes durables grow again — it deserves its own diligence workstream.**

**Q11 — Source quality and disclosure gaps (read this before using any number above).** This
memo was written in an environment that **cannot reach HKEXnews, Xueqiu, AASTOCKS, CFi.cn,
Sina Finance or Securities Times** (blocked by the network egress proxy). **Every figure here
therefore comes from search summaries and secondary reporting and must be re-verified against
primary filings before it enters a model.** Items that are explicitly unverified or in conflict:

1. **2025 reported (IFRS) net profit could not be obtained at all** — coverage uniformly cites
   only "adjusted net profit of ¥135.6m." That an entire year's results coverage runs on a
   non-IFRS measure **is itself a fact requiring explanation.**
2. **Listing date**: most sources say 23 September 2025; one self-media piece says 24 October
   2025, which is almost certainly wrong.
3. **Market cap and price**: HK$3.88bn / ~HK$43 and the 13 July 2026 quote of HK$42.56 come
   from different secondary feeds; a third shows "market cap HK$3.13bn," inconsistent with
   shares × price and probably a free-float measure. **Take a live quote before trading.**
4. **The February high**: HK$138.30 (23 February close) and "−75% from the 20 February high"
   cannot both hold unless the high was intraday.
5. **Member-count conflict**: "over 2 million online platform members" (30 September 2024) vs
   "~657k customers reached in the private domain, 53.3% repeat rate in 2024." Different
   denominators, so not a contradiction — but **cite the basis**, and note that the 53.3%
   figure has no stated time window or category definition.
6. **2026 618 figures** (peak single-day Tmall sales near ¥40m; ~¥100m over the event) come
   from trade self-media with no company filing to corroborate them.
7. Since April 2026, portal and regional outlets have carried a dense run of near-identically
   framed positive articles ("the AI family-consumption company hidden behind a stroller
   label"). **The uniformity is inconsistent with independent reporting**, and such pieces
   should be discounted heavily.

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **1H2026 interim results (due under Main Board rules by 31 August 2026 — days away).** The
   single highest-value event. Four things: **(a) revenue and YoY**, which the alert
   conspicuously omitted; **(b) revenue and gross margin by scenario**, above all the absolute
   travel + sleep figure; **(c) the composition of other income and net gains** (grants vs
   interest); **(d) operating cash flow against the ~¥92m of profit.**
2. **23 September 2026: twelve months since listing for the controlling shareholder.** One
   month away. Watch disclosures of interests, block-trade discounts and CCASS movements. This
   is the variable most likely to drive the share price over the next six months.
3. **Whether Cyannova actually buys.** US$20m ≈ HK$156m, close to 4% of market cap — if it
   happens it must leave a trace. If a year passes with nothing, the nature of the agreement
   is settled.
4. **Gross margin and depreciation once the Fenghua plant starts in 2H2026.** In-house capacity
   is the most tangible investment in this cycle and the largest swing factor for 2027 margin.
5. **Care-category price bands at Double 11 2026 and 618 2027.** Visible discounting falsifies
   the "high-margin outsourced diaper" assumption.
6. **2026 birth numbers (published mid-January 2027).** The denominator of the durable business,
   full stop.
7. **The first annual report's operating cash flow, inventory and receivables**, plus channel
   inventory across the 3,400 third-party stores.
8. **Whether a second broker initiates.** Coverage is currently one house, whose HK$8.4bn target
   embeds 38x 2026 earnings. A materially lower multiple from a second house would say that 38x
   was underwriter-ecosystem pricing rather than market consensus.

## 4. Provisional read

**The business** is real, and sturdier than most Chinese new-consumption brands: ¥1,446m of
revenue in six years, roughly 50% gross margin, genuine brand recognition, a user base in the
millions, 223 domestic patents. But it is going through **a category substitution that has not
been properly explained**: the premium mindshare built on strollers and car seats is now being
used to sell outsourced diapers, while the original durable business shrank in 2025 at exactly
the rate of the national birth decline. That is not necessarily bad — shifting from durables to
repeat-purchase products is a rational answer to a shrinking cohort — but it means **the company
being bought is not the company described in the prospectus**, and the two do not deserve the
same multiple.

**The stock**, unlike the business, **is no longer expensive.** At roughly 26x 2025 adjusted and
about 18x the 2026 estimate, the price broadly reflects "consensus delivered, then zero growth."
The AI story, the robotics subsidiary, Caocao and Cyannova are all being valued at zero. The
risk is not the multiple; it is **the quality of the numerator**: how much of the 90%+ first-half
profit growth comes from gross profit versus interest, grants and vanishing listing costs — and
whether the durable decline was a one-off (Dragon-year base) or a trend (demographics).

**What would flip the conclusion:** interim results showing revenue growth back above 20% with
other income a small share of profit — that would make this a growth stock mispriced by an unlock
wave, and 18x materially too low. Conversely, single-digit interim revenue growth with profit
growth driven by non-operating items sets up a 2027 of flat earnings *and* multiple compression,
leaving 30%+ downside from here.

**One judgement that deserves stating separately:** signing a strategic cooperation agreement
whose substance is a fund's undertaking to buy at least US$20m of your stock is the most unusual
fact in this memo. It produces no revenue and no profit; its only object is the share price. This
is not an allegation of any breach — but on a stock with a 12% float that fell 75% in five months,
it must be carried as a **governance observation, not as good news.**

**Concrete next step: the interim results are days away — do not establish a position before they
land.** That one document answers Q1 (margin by scenario), Q3 (earnings quality) and half of Q2
(absolute durable revenue) at far lower cost than any further modelling. When it arrives, do one
calculation first: **strip other income and net gains out of profit, annualise what remains, and
compute the multiple on that.** That number is the price you are actually paying.
