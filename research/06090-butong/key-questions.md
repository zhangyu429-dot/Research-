# 不同集团 (Butong Group / BeBeBus, 06090.HK) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 23 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

*This version is rewritten against the **2025 Annual Report (Part 1, pages 1–40)**. The
back half of the report — cash flow statement, notes to the financial statements, share
schemes, substantial shareholders' interests — was not available; the memo flags
throughout which questions remain unanswered because of it.*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

The prospectus described Butong as "the best-selling premium durable nursery products brand
in China by GMV." **The 2025 annual report shows that description no longer holds.** Durable
revenue (travel + sleep) fell from ¥794.1m in 2024 to **¥656.1m in 2025, −17.4%**: strollers
−9.9%, car seats **−20.1%**, baby carriers **−31.4%**, sleep gear −15.0%. All growth came
from diapers/wipes (+61.0%) and feeding (+147.5%) — and the report states plainly that
**baby care products carry a lower gross profit margin**, which is why the blended margin
fell from 50.4% to 49.5%.

You are not buying a premium durable-goods company expanding. You are buying **a premium
durable-goods company backfilling a shrinking core with a structurally lower-margin
category** — wrapped in a stock with a ~12% free float that is down roughly 75% from its
February high.

> its performance discussion, it says in its risk factors.** That inconsistency is where
> every question in this memo starts.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1: How low is the care-category margin, and where does blended margin end up?

The annual report has half-answered this: gross margin fell from 50.4% to 49.5% "primarily
due to an increased portion of revenue contributed by baby care products which carry a lower
gross profit margin." That sentence kills the painless-pivot version of the bull case —
**the mix shift is diluting margin, and the company says so.**

The rest is arithmetic. Care is now 43.2% of revenue, up from 31.1%, and it is the only
large category still growing. Let *a* be the blended margin of everything else and *b* that
of care:

- 2024: 0.689a + 0.311b = 50.4%
- 2025: 0.568a + 0.432b = 49.5%

Solving gives **a ≈ 52.7%, b ≈ 45.3%** (assuming each is stable across the two years). If
care reaches 55% of revenue in 2026, blended margin falls to **about 48.6%**; at 65%, to
**about 47.9%** — before any price competition in diapers.

**Ask:**
- **Actual gross margin by scenario**, to test that decomposition. The report gives direction,
  not numbers.
- **Volume versus price in the care category.** The report attributes care growth to
  "successful online channel expansion driving significant sales volume growth" — **volume,
  not price**. A premium brand growing on volume alone needs to explain how much premium is
  left.
- **Repeat-purchase rate and acquisition cost for care**, separately from durables.
- **The durability of feeding +147.5%.** The report attributes it to "our new baby bottle
  series which became a market best-seller" — **a single hero SKU**. Natural decay in year
  two is the biggest risk to this growth line.

### Q2: Is the durable decline competition or demographics — and what is normalised revenue?

This is the most important discrimination in the memo. The report attributes travel −18.3%
and sleep −15.0% entirely to "heightened market competition," while national births fell
−17.0% in the same year. The two explanations carry opposite investment implications:

- **If it is competition:** share can be won back, product and AI upgrades are a real remedy,
  and durables may stabilise in 2026. But it also means the self-described category leader
  lost nearly a fifth of its core revenue to rivals in twelve months.
- **If it is demographics:** share may not have moved at all — the pool simply shrank 17%.
  There is no remedy, durables decline year after year, and 2026 starts from an even lower
  7.92m base.

**These can be separated, and it takes one number: unit volumes and market share by category.**

**Ask:**
- **2025 unit volumes and market share, by category, year on year.** Volume decline ≈ industry
  decline means demographics; volume decline materially worse than industry means share loss.
  **This is the single most valuable set of numbers to extract from management.**
- 2024 was a Dragon year. How much of that base was one-off?
- **Absolute travel and sleep revenue for 1H2026** — the interim results will show it.
- The report says the 2026 user base extends from 0–3 to **ages 3–12 and their families**,
  with smart toothbrushes, hair dryers and smart luggage planned. **None of these carry brand
  equity from nursery products; they compete with Xiaomi and Philips.** Ask management
  directly whether this is category extension or an admission that the 0–3 market can no
  longer carry growth.

### Q3: How much of the ¥65.2m of reported profit is operating?

The annual report quantifies this well enough to compute:

| Item | 2025 | 2024 | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Government grants | **¥27.7m** | ¥15.3m | Non-recurring |
| Interest income | ¥6.5m | ¥3.2m | Non-operating, on IPO proceeds |
| Net gains on FVTPL financial assets | ¥4.5m | ¥2.9m | Non-operating |
| **Other income and net gain, total** | **¥39.3m (+92.9%)** | ¥20.4m | **= 30.9% of pre-tax profit** |
| Listing expenses | ¥25.1m | ¥17.2m | Zero from 2026 |
| Interest on redeemable preferred shares | ¥18.4m | ¥25.4m | Zero from 2026 |
| Equity-settled share-based payments | **¥26.9m (+173.8%)** | ¥9.8m | **Recurring, yet added back in "adjusted"** |
| **Effective tax rate** | **48.8%** | 49.9% | See below |

Three conclusions:

1. **Government grants plus interest income total ¥34.2m against ¥65.2m of reported profit —
   more than half.** Strip out all other income and net gains and pre-tax profit is **¥88.0m**;
   at the 48.8% tax rate, "pure operating" net profit is roughly **¥45m**.
2. **The company's adjusted measure adds back ¥26.9m of share-based payment, which grew
   173.8%.** That is a real cost to shareholders and should not be added back. **The measure
   that survives both objections — excluding genuine one-offs but keeping share-based payment
   — is ¥108.7m.**
3. **The effective tax rate is 48.8%, near half of pre-tax profit.** Even adding listing
   expenses, preferred-share interest and share-based payment back to pre-tax (¥197.7m), the
   implied rate is still **31.4%**, above the 25% statutory rate. **Why?**

**Ask:**
- **Why has the effective rate sat near 50%?** Non-deductible items, unrelieved holding-company
  losses, no High and New Technology Enterprise status? **If the rate normalises to 25% in
  2026 that alone is worth roughly 30% of profit — plausibly the least-discussed source of
  the "+90% first half."**
- **The composition and durability of the ¥27.7m of government grants**, and whether they are
  tied to the new Fenghua plant (if so they recur through 2026–2027, but as a capital subsidy
  rather than operating profit).
- **The absolute figure for other income and net gains in 1H2026.** The profit alert already
  names it as a driver.
- **Net cash from operating activities** — Part 1 of the annual report does not include the
  cash flow statement. **This is the single largest remaining disclosure gap.**

### Q4: AI, robotics, Cyannova — narrative migration as an independent risk

The report restates the corporate mission as "**Empower innovation through AI to create
differences**," and the Chairman's Statement presents "**embodied intelligence and high-end
manufacturing**" as a **business segment** alongside the premium brand, one that "provides
intelligent production equipment, production lines and integrated solutions for industrial
automation and smart manufacturing." **That segment carries no revenue figure anywhere in the
report.** Meanwhile:

- **100 R&D staff and ¥25.4m of R&D spend (1.8% of revenue)**; of 669 total employees, 403
  are sales.
- 2026 plans include smart toothbrushes, smart hair dryers and smart luggage.
- On 21 May 2026 a framework agreement with **Cyannova Capital**, under which **Cyannova
  undertakes to buy no less than US$20m of the shares in the open market within a year.**
- In June 2026 a child-car-seat framework agreement with Caocao Mobility (02643.HK).

A company running ¥25.4m of R&D across 100 engineers has simultaneously announced household
AI hardware, embodied intelligence and industrial automation solutions. **This is not a
capability question but a priority one: if these are real, 2026–2027 opex rises sharply; if
they are not, they should not be in the valuation.**

Cyannova deserves separate treatment: **a listed company signing an agreement whose substance
is that a fund will buy its stock.** It contributes nothing to the income statement; its only
object is the share price.

**Ask:**
- **What revenue did the embodied-intelligence segment generate in 2025?** If zero, ask for it
  to be broken out in the 2026 interim report.
- Whether Cyannova **has actually bought, and how much**; and whether the agreement is a
  notifiable or connected transaction.
- 2026 R&D budget in absolute terms and as a share of revenue; AI product revenue target and
  recognition basis.
- **Signed purchase amounts** under the Caocao agreement, or an explicit statement that there
  are none. Note that ride-hailing car seats are a high-volume, low-price B2B channel —
  **even if real, it may be margin-dilutive revenue.**

### Q5: What has to be true for a HK$3.85bn market cap?

Reverse the valuation. Market cap is about **¥3.50bn**, of which **¥871.9m is net cash**, so
enterprise value is about **¥2.63bn**.

At an **18x** terminal multiple — neutral-to-generous for a Chinese consumer brand whose
growth engine is a lower-margin outsourced FMCG category in a shrinking end market — ¥3.50bn
requires **about ¥194m of reported net profit**:

| Starting point | Amount | Gap to ¥194m |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 HKFRS profit for the year | ¥65.2m | **+198% required** |
| + listing expenses ¥25.1m (zero from 2026) | ¥90.3m | +115% |
| + preferred-share interest ¥18.4m (zero from 2026) | **¥108.7m** | **+79%** |
| + effective tax rate normalising to 25% (on ¥170.8m pre-tax) | **¥128.1m** | **+52%** |

**In other words: even zeroing out listing expenses and preferred-share interest, and even
assuming the effective tax rate falls from 48.8% to 25%, the company still needs roughly 52%
of operating profit growth to justify today's price.** What actually happened in 2025:
EBITDA **−0.8%**, EPS **−5.6%**, gross margin down 0.9pp, selling cost ratio up 0.3pp,
administrative expenses **+36.5%**.

**This differs from my read before the annual report and the correction should be explicit:
on reported numbers, the stock is not cheap.** The "about 18x forward" headline rests on two
things that both need discounting — a numerator that adds back ¥26.9m of recurring
share-based payment, and a denominator from the only broker covering the name. On a genuinely
clean basis the price is **32x 2025 earnings, 16x EV/EBITDA and 3.2x book** for a company
whose EBITDA shrank last year.

**The downside arithmetic is equally clear.** Assume 2026: durables −15% again, care +25%,
feeding +25% → revenue about ¥1.54bn (+6.9%); gross margin 48.6% per the decomposition above;
selling, administrative and R&D ratios held at 2025 levels (42.0% combined); other income flat
at ¥39.3m → pre-tax about ¥141m. At the clean 36.4% tax rate that is **¥90m**; even at 25% it
is **¥106m**. At 12x — what a consumer company with stalled growth deserves — plus ¥871.9m of
net cash, that is **¥1.95–2.14bn ≈ HK$2.1–2.4bn**, or **roughly 39–44% below the current price**.

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — A 12% free float is the root of every price anomaly here.** The IPO sold only
**10,980,900 shares**; public float is about **12.1%**; net proceeds were **HK$702.5m**;
cornerstones took ~1,642,500 shares (~US$15m). Pre-IPO ownership was concentrated: **Wang Wei
held 52.95% through WANGBOYAN**, Shen Ling 6.77%, WEILING (Futu Trust) 5.74%, Tiantu 9.24%,
Gaorong 8.82% + 0.98%, Tembusu (Matrix China) 7.84%, Taikang Life 5.15%.

The price history is a supply history: **listed 23 September 2025, +44% on debut → 5 January
2026 down over 26% intraday and briefly through the HK$71.20 offer price → 13 February added
to the Hang Seng Composite series, 9 March added to Stock Connect → high of the listing in
late February (secondary quotes: HK$138.30 on 23 February, HK$115.50 on 7 March; media report
a ~75% drawdown from a 20 February high, reconcilable only if that high was intraday around
HK$170) → sharp fall in late March → +50% intraday on the results in mid-April, −16.17% the
next day → ~HK$42.56 in July.**

The timing matters: **the standard six-month lock-up expired on 23 March 2026**, and the
company bought back shares on **24, 25, 26 and 31 March** (HK$4.527m/61,600; HK$3.913m/54,800
at HK$69.65–78.60; HK$1.522m/21,200; HK$10,300/200). Before that, on 2 and 25 February,
shareholders deposited shares worth about **HK$415m** and **HK$577m** into Futu Securities
International (HK). **Depositing into a broker is normally a precursor to selling.**

**Also note: the company holds ¥871.9m of net cash (25% of market cap), pays no dividend, has
announced no buyback programme and no material investment plans** — the report states that as
at 31 December 2025 it "did not hold any significant investments and had no specific plans for
significant investments or acquisition of capital assets" (the robotics subsidiary came in
April 2026). **A company with a quarter of its market cap in cash, earning roughly 0.7% on it,
paying nothing out, is itself a capital-allocation question.**

**Ask:** who sold after March and how much (disclosures of interests, CCASS); **the controlling
shareholder's intentions after the twelve-month milestone on 23 September 2026** — one month
away; and the plan for the ¥871.9m. Under HK$10m of buybacks cannot offset an unlock on a ~12%
float; **its signalling value far exceeds its mechanical value.**

**Q7 — The cost base is deteriorating across the board, and "adjusted net profit" hides it.**
In 2025: selling and distribution **+17.0%** (faster than revenue's +15.8%), ratio up from
31.3% to **31.6%**; administrative and other **+36.5%**, ratio up from 7.3% to **8.6%**, with
employee benefit expenses inside it rising from ¥41.3m to **¥63.2m**, which the report
attributes explicitly to "increased costs associated with employee share option scheme." That
is where the +173.8% in share-based payment went. **Revenue +15.8%, gross profit +13.8%,
EBITDA −0.8%** — read together, operating leverage in 2025 was negative. **Ask** for the split
of selling costs between brand, performance and channel rebates; a **CAC time series**; and the
2026 share-based payment budget and vesting schedule.

**Q8 — Distributors +40.5% against key accounts −0.1%: channel inventory risk.** Essentially
all offline growth came from distributors (¥199.0m → ¥279.7m), while key accounts — the large
mother-and-baby retail chains — were **flat**. Distributor revenue is recognised on shipment;
key accounts and end retail reflect real sell-through. **When durables are declining at the
consumer end while shipments to distributors rise 40%, channel inventory is the first thing to
check.** Part 1 does not disclose inventory composition or receivables ageing; the current and
quick ratios imply inventories of about **¥110m** (roughly 55 days of cost of sales), and
**impairment losses on trade receivables rose from ¥24k to ¥112k (+367%), which the report
attributes to a higher receivables balance.** **Ask:** distributor closing inventory and
sell-through; receivables ageing; distributor count and average offtake; return and rebate
policy (the report states revenue is net of returns allowances, rebates and discounts).

**Q9 — Customer and supplier concentration: 30% of a "DTC brand" sits with five counterparties.**
Top-five customers were **30.1%** of 2025 revenue (2024: 32.6%) and the **single largest 13.4%**
(2024: 12.9%); top-five suppliers were **40.0%** of purchases (2024: 43.5%), largest 12.4%. The
report says suppliers "consist primarily of e-commerce platforms, raw material suppliers and
third-party manufacturers" — **counting platforms inside purchases means platform fees sit in
that concentration figure, so the basis needs clarifying.** On manufacturing: the owned Ningbo
facility is about 14,111 sqm with ISO9001 and IATF16949 certification, and the report states the
company can now "manufacture all our core products in-house," switching flexibly between
in-house and outsourced production; **a second Ningbo facility (Fenghua) is slated for
completion in 2026 and operation in the second half.** **Ask:** who the largest customer is
(platform or distributor); the contract manufacturers and quality arrangements for diapers and
wipes (not disclosed in the report; media have previously questioned some manufacturers'
credentials and hygiene licences); and the new plant's capex, capacity, depreciation policy and
categories.

**Q10 — Globalisation: the report contains no overseas revenue figure at all.** The Chairman's
Statement and Business Outlook devote substantial space to the globalisation strategy —
China-centred integrated supply chain and R&D, localisation of channels, products and brand,
centralised management structures in various countries — **but revenue is disaggregated only by
product and by channel, never by geography, and no overseas amount or percentage appears
anywhere in the text.** In accounting terms that normally means the amount is not yet material
enough to segment. **The non-disclosure is the answer.** Ask for 2025 overseas revenue in
absolute terms; certification progress for car seats in the US and EU (FMVSS 213, ECE R129);
and tariff assumptions.

**Q11 — Three governance details.**
(i) **Social insurance and housing provident fund are underpaid.** The report acknowledges
shortfalls caused by high workforce mobility, employee reluctance to bear their own portion, and
administrative barriers for migrant workers, and states that on the basis of consultations and
PRC legal advice the risk of penalties or collection of arrears is "remote" — **so no provision
has been made.** This is an unquantified contingent liability; ask for the exposure.
(ii) **The auditor is KPMG**, the compliance adviser Somerley, and the three independent
non-executive directors (Yan Jianjun, Yu Chun Kau, Chan Wing Ki) are substantive — Yu is a
long-standing HKICPA practitioner, Chan was head of legal and joint company secretary at Xiaomi.
**Board composition is the most credible governance feature here.**
(iii) **The CFO joined in October 2023 and the board secretary in June 2023** — the core finance
and governance team was assembled in the year before the IPO; the 2022–2023 numbers were
produced by a different team.

**Q12 — Sources, and what is still missing.** All 2025 financial data above comes from the
**2025 Annual Report Part 1 (pages 1–40)** and is primary. What remains unavailable sits in the
back half and bears directly on the conclusions: **the consolidated cash flow statement (p.102),
the notes (p.103 onward, covering inventories, receivables, share-based payment, related-party
transactions), directors' and substantial shareholders' interests, share scheme details, and the
use-of-proceeds progress table.** Also still to note: (1) this environment cannot reach HKEXnews,
so **the price and market cap (about HK$42.6 / HK$3.85bn) remain secondary — take a live quote
before trading**; (2) the two February high figures cannot both hold; (3) 2026 618 sales figures
come from trade self-media with no filing to corroborate them; (4) since April 2026 a dense run
of near-identically framed positive articles has appeared across portals, and should be
discounted heavily.

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **1H2026 interim results (due under Main Board rules by 31 August 2026 — days away).** The
   highest-value single event. Five things: **(a) revenue and YoY**, which the alert omitted;
   **(b) revenue by category, above all absolute travel and sleep**; **(c) revenue by channel,
   above all distributors versus self-operated stores**; **(d) the absolute figure and
   composition of other income and net gains**; **(e) operating cash flow.**
2. **The back half of the annual report (Part 2).** Cash flow statement, inventory and
   receivables notes, share-based payment note, use-of-proceeds progress. Together these close
   most of Q3, Q7 and Q8.
3. **23 September 2026: twelve months since listing for the controlling shareholder.** One month
   away. Watch disclosures of interests, block-trade discounts and CCASS.
4. **Whether Cyannova actually buys.** US$20m ≈ HK$156m, close to 4% of market cap; it would
   leave a trace.
5. **Blended gross margin when care passes 50% of revenue.** The decomposition above puts it near
   49.0%; materially below that means diapers have entered a price war.
6. **Depreciation and gross margin once the Fenghua plant starts in 2H2026.**
7. **2026 birth numbers (mid-January 2027).** The denominator of the durable business.
8. **Whether the effective tax rate normalises.** Falling from 48.8% to 25% is worth roughly 30%
   of profit on its own — the item most easily misread as operating improvement in 2026.
9. **Whether a second broker initiates.** Coverage is one house, whose HK$8.4bn target embeds 38x
   2026 adjusted earnings.

## 4. Provisional read

**The business:** the annual report confirms the worse version. The three categories the brand
was built on — strollers, car seats, carriers — fell 9.9%, 20.1% and 31.4%; sleep fell 15.0%;
durables together **−17.4%**, against a national birth decline of **−17.0%**. The category
filling the gap carries, **by the company's own statement, a lower gross margin**. Self-operated
online stores, half of revenue, grew 7.0%; the fastest-growing channel is the one where revenue
is booked on shipment (+40.5%), while the most end-market-like channel was flat. Revenue +15.8%,
EBITDA **−0.8%**, EPS **−5.6%**. This is not a growth company changing lanes. It is **a company
whose core is contracting, holding the top line together with a lower-margin category, while
every expense ratio rises.**

**The stock — and here I have to correct my read from before the filing.** I previously said,
on the basis of "adjusted net profit of ¥135.6m," that the stock was no longer expensive. On
reported numbers that was wrong: **53.7x 2025 HKFRS earnings, 32.2x on a clean basis, 3.2x book,
16.1x EV/EBITDA**, with 25% of market cap in cash that pays no dividend and funds no buyback.
The "about 18x forward" figure depends on a non-HKFRS numerator that adds back ¥26.9m of
recurring share-based payment and on the single covering broker's estimate. **The real price is
32x for a company whose EBITDA shrank last year.**

**What would flip the conclusion:** interim results showing **absolute travel and sleep revenue
stabilising or recovering**, with care still growing above 40% and operating cash flow covering
profit. That would validate "competition" over "demographics," meaning share is being won back,
and 32x for a re-accelerating brand is not absurd. Conversely, continued double-digit durable
decline, distributor shipments still far outpacing key accounts, and other income still a third
of pre-tax profit would set up flat 2027 earnings *and* multiple compression — roughly 40% below
today.

**One judgement that deserves stating separately:** the report presents "embodied intelligence
and high-end manufacturing" as a business segment while attributing no revenue to it; the company
plans household AI hardware, smart toothbrushes, smart luggage and industrial automation
solutions on 100 R&D staff and ¥25.4m of spend; and it has signed an agreement with a fund that
undertakes to buy at least US$20m of its stock. **Taken together these constitute a narrative
risk that should be assessed on its own** — none of them produce revenue, but all of them affect
the price. This is not an allegation of any breach; they are **governance observations, not good
news.**

**Concrete next step: the interim results are days away — do not establish a position before they
land.** That document answers Q1 (margin direction by category), Q2 (absolute durable revenue),
Q3 (earnings quality) and Q8 (distributor sell-through) at once. When it arrives, do one
calculation first: **strip out other income and net gains, put share-based payment back in,
annualise what remains, and compute the multiple on that.** That number is the price you are
actually paying. **And request Part 2 of the annual report** — until the cash flow statement and
the inventory and receivables notes are visible, every profit figure above is an accrual.
