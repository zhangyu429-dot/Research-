# 金海通 (Jinhaitong, 603061.SH) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 12 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Jinhaitong is a **single-product company in the most cyclical corner of semiconductor capital
equipment**, currently experiencing the best demand environment of its listed life, priced as
though that environment is permanent and global. There is no cash-cow segment funding an
option here — there is one business, one product family, and one cycle.

> **The single fact that frames everything:** in **2022**, before it listed, this company earned
> **¥154m**. In **2025**, after a 72% revenue surge and the best year in its history, it earned
> **¥177m** — 15% more, three years later, on 64% more revenue, at a net margin still **11 points
> below** 2022. The bull case is that 2026 permanently breaks that pattern. Consensus agrees, and
> asks you to underwrite **5.2x profit growth in three years**. The bear case is that 2022 was also
> a cycle peak, and this is what the top looks like from the inside.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1. Is the tri-temperature franchise a *moat* or a *window*?

This is the actual bull case and everything else is secondary. The EXCEED-9800 tri-temperature
handler (−55°C to +150°C class testing, for automotive and industrial-grade parts) reached volume
production in 2024; a 32-site parallel-test model launched in 2025, with 64-site in development.
The mix shift is unambiguous and is the reason gross margin went from 47.5% to 52.1%:

| EXCEED-9000 series share | 2024 | H1 2025 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| of **total revenue** | 25.8% | — | **39.0%** |
| of **equipment sales revenue** | — | **51.4%** | — |

*(The 51.4% and 39.0% figures use different denominators — equipment sales vs total revenue — and
are not the contradiction they first appear to be. Confirm both against the primary filings.)*

Tri-temperature handling is the technically hard, high-ASP end where imported equipment still sits.
If Jinhaitong holds it, >50% gross margin is defensible and the multiple is arguable. If this is a
window before 长川科技 (a far larger full-stack platform: testers + handlers + probers + AOI),
台湾鸿劲科技 and Cohu ship equivalent platforms, margin normalises toward the segment's historic
40s and the earnings base is much lower than consensus.

**Sub-questions to answer before underwriting any 2027+ number:**
- **Repeat-order rate on the 9800 from the same customers** — one design win in a shortage is not a
  franchise; a second and third order from the same fab is.
- **Gross margin and ASP by series**, not blended. If 9000-series margin is not disclosed, that
  absence is itself information.
- Does the **64-site model ship on schedule**, and what did 长川 / 鸿劲 announce in the same window?
- Decompose 2025–26 margin gain into **price / mix / scale absorption**. Price and mix in a shortage
  reverse; scale absorption does not.

### Q2. Can the overseas story actually scale — and does the price already assume it has?

Overseas was **¥85.9m in 2025: +125.6%, but still only 12.3%** of main business. Management credits
H1 2026 partly to overseas order growth, and the Malaysia production/operations centre opened in
H1 2025. This is the most important *unproven* line item in the company.

It matters because of arithmetic, not narrative. Consensus 2028 revenue of **¥2.57bn ≈ US$360m** of
handler revenue would place Jinhaitong **at or near global top-3**. The global top five — Cohu
(Xcerra/MCT), 长川科技, Advantest, 鸿劲科技, Techwing — hold roughly **57%** of the market. There is
no path to ¥2.57bn that does not involve **taking volume share inside ASE / Amkor / global IDM
accounts**, in their home regions, against incumbents with decades of installed base and service
networks.

**Ask:**
- **Overseas revenue share in the H1 2026 report.** 12% → 25% would be genuine validation. 12% → 15%
  means the 2028 number is fantasy and the stock is mispriced by a multiple, not a margin.
- **Overseas gross margin vs domestic** — winning offshore on price is a different business.
- Has any **tier-1 non-Chinese OSAT or IDM moved past evaluation into repeat volume**? Name it.
- Malaysia centre **utilisation and headcount** — is it a manufacturing site or a sales/service shell?
- **Export-control, tariff and customer-preference risk** on a China-headquartered equipment vendor
  selling into Taiwan, SE Asia and US-affiliated fabs. This is a live, worsening variable, and it
  cuts against exactly the revenue line the valuation depends on.

### Q3. How much of the "domestic substitution" runway is already spent?

The sources conflict in a way that changes the thesis entirely:

| Claim | Figure |
|---|---|
| Chinese **test equipment overall** localisation | **<30%** (high-end memory/SoC testers **<5%**) |
| Chinese **handlers specifically** | **~60% localised by 2025**; one industry source calls the segment "essentially fully substituted" |

If handlers are already ~60% domestic, then the easy policy-and-shortage tailwind is **behind**
this company, not ahead of it. Remaining domestic growth is a **share war against 长川科技**, not
import displacement — and 长川 is several times larger, sells the tester alongside the handler, and
can bundle. That reframes the investment from "policy-driven substitution runway" to "margin-share
fight plus an unproven export business," which deserves a materially lower multiple.

**Ask:** Jinhaitong's **unit share of Chinese handler tenders** over the last four quarters, split
by whether the displaced vendor was an import or 长川. Those are different wins.

### Q4. Is the reported profit turning into cash?

This is the clearest amber flag in the numbers, and the 洁美-style question of "where did the growth
go" inverts here into "where did the cash go."

- **Accounts receivable +52.4% in FY2025 and +56.8% YoY at Q1 2026** — reportedly ~**328% of profit**.
- **Inventory ¥484m at 31 Mar 2026**, up 17.3% from ¥413m at year-end — roughly **nine months of
  revenue** sitting on the balance sheet.
- **Total asset turnover deteriorating across three annual reports: 0.60 → 0.29 → 0.26.**
- **FY2025 OCF of ¥128m against ¥177m of net profit — 72% conversion**, in the best year the company
  has ever had. Three-year average OCF / current liabilities is only ~15.4%.
- Q1 2026 OCF of ¥102m was genuinely excellent (+1602%) and partially rebuts the above — one quarter.

Building receivables and inventory into a delivery ramp is normal for equipment: you build ahead,
and revenue is often recognised on installation while cash arrives on acceptance. It is *also*
precisely what pulled-forward revenue recognition looks like. The two are indistinguishable from
outside until a full year of cash conversion goes by.

**Ask:** AR **ageing buckets** and provisioning policy; inventory **composition** — specifically the
**发出商品** (goods shipped, pending customer acceptance) line, which is the single most diagnostic
number in the balance sheet; and whether FY2026 OCF converts anywhere near net profit.

### Q5. What has to be true for ¥26.8bn to be cheap?

Reverse the valuation instead of forecasting into it.

At a terminal **25x** — already generous for a single-product, back-end-cyclical equipment maker
with a three-year profit CAGR of ~5% through 2025 — ¥26.8bn requires **~¥1.07bn of net profit**.
That is **6.1x** 2025, and **above** the 2028 consensus of ¥921m. At 30x it requires ~¥893m — which
is the 2028 consensus, almost exactly.

**So the current price is, to a first approximation: the full 2028 sell-side case, delivered on
time, capitalised at 30x, discounted at zero.** There is no margin of safety in it and no allowance
for a cycle in a cyclical business.

A plausible bridge to that number: domestic handler revenue ¥1.4–1.6bn (roughly 2.5x 2025 domestic,
requiring both a sustained capex cycle and share gains against 长川), plus overseas ¥0.9–1.1bn
(**11–13x** the 2025 overseas base), at a ~35% net margin the company has hit exactly once, in 2022,
at a quarter of the scale.

**The question is not whether this is a good company — it plainly is. It is: which of those three
conditions do you believe, and what do you get paid if only two of them happen?** At 115x trailing,
the answer is nothing. Note also that the market has already re-rated hard on this news flow —
three separate abnormal-volatility announcements since 1 July — so the cycle is thoroughly in the
price.

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Customer concentration is going the wrong way.** Top-5 customers: **51.0% (2024) → 65.4%
(H1 2025) → 58.3% (FY2025)**. Concentration *rising* through an upcycle means the growth came from a
handful of accounts, not broad adoption. For a company this size, one large OSAT deferring a line
moves a quarter by 30%. Get the **named top five** and the **single-largest customer share** from the
annual report, and establish whether the biggest is Chinese or overseas.

**Q7 — Where in the cycle is 2026, and what is normalised earnings?** Back-end test equipment is
among the most violent capex lines in semis: order books go from record to empty in two quarters.
This company has now shown you a full cycle — 2022 peak (¥154m), 2023–24 trough (¥85m, ¥79m),
2025–26 boom. If ¥414m is a *peak-cycle* 2026 and mid-cycle is ¥250–300m, the stock is on
**90–110x normalised**. **A view on this stock is mostly a view on whether the AI-driven back-end
build is a multi-year plateau or a 2025–27 pulse** — and note that Jinhaitong's core tri-temp
strength is automotive/industrial, which is a *different* cycle from AI/HPC. Management cites both.
Make them decompose it.

**Q8 — The ¥850m convertible: is capacity actually the constraint?** Plan disclosed 6 May 2026,
approved by shareholders 21 May; proceeds for a semiconductor advanced-equipment intelligent
manufacturing centre (a subsidiary bought 21,000 m² in Shanghai), R&D, and working capital. ¥850m
against roughly ¥1.7bn of book equity is a large raise. **At the current 0.26x total asset turnover,
¥850m of new assets supports only ~¥220m of incremental revenue** unless turns improve sharply.
Ask for **current capacity utilisation** — if it is below ~85%, this is a strategic land-grab, not a
demand-forced expansion — plus the project's stated incremental capacity, IRR and payback from the
feasibility report, and the **conversion price and dilution** at a range of share prices.

**Q9 — Share supply is a live, quantifiable overhang.** **18.17m shares — 30.29% of then-outstanding
capital — came off IPO lock-up on 3 March 2026** (崔学峰 8.51m, 龙波 5.34m, 南通华泓 3.96m, 天津博芯
0.36m). Second-largest holder **宁波旭诺** sold 598,100 shares for ~¥156m (Oct 2025–Jan 2026), then
announced a further sale of up to 1.80m shares (3.00%) for the 30 Apr–29 Jul 2026 window and executed
around 22 July. On **24 July, five block trades cleared at ¥268.70 — an 8.79% discount**. Discounted
blocks from an informed holder into a 115x multiple are not a fundamental argument, but they are
information about what the best-informed sellers think the price is worth.

**Q10 — Governance, alignment and the quality of the "beat."** FY2025 dividend was **¥0.38/share,
¥22.8m total — a 12.9% payout** — alongside a 10-for-4.5 bonus issue that creates no economic value.
Combined with an ¥850m raise, capital allocation is unambiguously growth-first, which is defensible,
but it means shareholders are funding reinvestment at **15.9x book**. Separately: 2024's profit
decline was attributed partly to **share-based payment expense**, and 2025 R&D rose 43.4% partly on
the same line, with an ESOP covering ≤109 people. **Check how much of the 2026 "beat" is
non-cash-comp timing**, and whether the ESOP's performance targets are demanding or trivially met by
a cycle the company does not control. Over 30% of employees are in R&D — genuinely good, and the
main reason to take the technology claim seriously.

**Q11 — Source quality and disclosure gaps.** Much of the bullish 2026 narrative circulating is
retail-facing 财富号 / self-media. Several primary-filing domains (cninfo, SSE, dfcfw) were
unreachable from this environment, so **every figure in this memo is from secondary coverage of the
filings and must be re-verified against the 定期报告 and 投资者关系活动记录表 before it enters a
model.** Two specific items to treat as unverified: (a) the reported FY2025 gross margin appears as
both **52.10%** and **52.21%** depending on source; (b) any global handler TAM figure you encounter —
the market-size claims underlying "top-3 by 2028" are not consistent across the research reports and
none of them should be used without a primary source. Note finally that **the company itself has
publicly warned that its valuation is high relative to the industry**, in three separate
abnormal-volatility filings (1 July, 20 July, 7 August 2026). That is an unusual and creditable
disclosure. Take it at face value.

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **H1 2026 interim report (due late August 2026 — imminent).** The single highest-value event, and
   it answers Q2, Q4 and part of Q1 at once. Look for: **overseas revenue share**; **gross margin
   by product series**; **发出商品 inventory and AR ageing**; H1 operating cash flow against the
   ¥160–190m of profit; top-5 customer concentration.
2. **Contract liabilities (合同负债) trend through Q3 2026** — the cleanest public proxy for backlog.
   Still building = the cycle has legs; drawn down = Q2 was delivery catch-up.
3. **Whether gross margin holds above ~52%** as volume mix broadens. Below 48% means the competitive
   response has arrived.
4. **FY2026 OCF conversion.** Below ~70% of net profit for a second year, with AR still outgrowing
   revenue, changes the earnings-quality question from amber to red.
5. **The 64-site tri-temp platform shipping on schedule**, and what 长川科技 / 鸿劲科技 announce in
   the same window.
6. **Convertible bond terms** — conversion price, timing, and the disclosed capacity utilisation in
   the feasibility documents (settles Q8).
7. **Further insider sales at discounts**, and the remaining unsold portion of 旭诺's plan.
8. **Customer capex guidance** from 长电 / 通富 / 华天 and **Cohu's handler-segment commentary** — the
   external read on whether the back-end order cycle is still accelerating.

## 4. Provisional read

The business is genuinely good and better than it was. The tri-temperature product transition is
real, it is showing up in mix and margin rather than just in slides, the demand environment is
strongly favourable, and over 30% of staff in R&D suggests the technology claim is not marketing.
Three consecutive quarters of triple-digit profit growth is not an accounting artefact.

**The problem is entirely price, cycle position, and concentration.** You are paying ~115x trailing
and ~65x consensus 2026 for a **¥698m-revenue company that earns 88% of its revenue in China, 58%
from five customers, and sells one product family into the most cyclical capex line in
semiconductors** — in a domestic segment that may already be ~60% localised. The valuation embeds a
global top-3 outcome by 2028 that requires the overseas business to grow **11–13x** from a ¥86m base,
against incumbents, into a worsening export-control environment. Meanwhile 30% of the float came
free in March and the best-informed holder is selling at an 8.8% discount.

The honest framing: **a real technology win and a real cyclical up-leg, at a price that requires the
technology win to become a global franchise within three years.** The asymmetry only becomes
attractive if (a) the H1 report shows overseas share materially above 12% *with* margin intact, or
(b) the stock de-rates hard on a cyclical scare while the tri-temp sockets and repeat orders hold.

**Do not size a position before the H1 report.** It is days away, it answers Q2 and Q4 more cheaply
than any amount of further modelling, and at this multiple the downside on a disappointment is a
de-rating, not a miss.
