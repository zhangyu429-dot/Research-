# 登康口腔 (Dengkang Oral Care, 001328.SZ) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 31 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Dengkang is **a capital-light, cash-heavy, state-owned consumer company that pays out four
fifths of its earnings, buying growth in a flat category with a steadily rising advertising
bill.** The core asset is 冷酸灵 (Cold Acid Spirit) — China's leading anti-sensitivity
toothpaste brand, roughly 60% share of that sub-category — plus an offline distribution
network descending from the Chongqing Toothpaste Factory of 1939. There are two growth
narratives: e-commerce scaling (¥588m in 2025, 34.3% of revenue, a 40.7% five-year CAGR),
and "premiumisation" lifting gross margin (42.1% in 2021 → roughly 49% in 2025).
**The central finding of this memo is that those two narratives cancel each other out in
the income statement — and cancel almost exactly.**

> margin has not changed in six years.

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1: Is the gross margin lift profitability, or channel classification?

This sits ahead of growth because it determines whether three years of "premiumisation" had
any economic substance.

The chain of facts: gross margin 42.10% (2021) → 44.1% (2023) → ~47.4% (2024) → ~49.4%
(2025) → 52.59% (Q1 2026). Company and sell-side agree on the cause: **a rising share of
premium products (the 医研 line, priced above ¥30).** If true, this is the most valuable
change a consumer company can produce.

**But net margin over the same period is a flat line.** And each step up in gross margin came
with an equal step up in the selling expense ratio: ~24% (2022) → **28.1% (+4.1pp, 2023)** →
**30.16% (+3.98pp, H1 2024)** → **33.04% (+1.77pp, 9M 2025)**.

**Q3 2025 supplies near-laboratory evidence.** The company said it had "temporarily reduced
Douyin spending":

| Q3 2025 | Level | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 45.9% | **−8.03pp** |
| Selling expense ratio | 25.51% | **−8.00pp** |
| Net margin | 13.13% | **+0.11pp** |

**Dial online spending down once, and gross margin and selling expense collapse together by
eight points while net margin does not move.** Only one explanation survives: **what carries
the high gross margin is the channel, not the product.** E-commerce (interest e-commerce
especially) books revenue at a higher realised price — so gross margin is structurally high —
while traffic fees, slotting and influencer commissions land in selling expense. Offline
distribution books at a lower ex-factory price with the expense carried by the distributor.
**Gross margin is therefore mostly a function of online mix, not premium mix.**

That does not make the growth fake — revenue and profit both genuinely grew. It means:
**do not pay a valuation premium for the gross margin curve, and do not read it as evidence
of pricing power.** The only meaningful metric is net margin, and it has not moved in six
years.

**Ask:**
- **Gross margin by channel** — offline distribution, e-commerce self-operated, e-commerce
  platform — for three periods (2024, 2025, H1 2026). **This one disclosure settles the
  question outright. If it is not broken out, the non-disclosure is itself the answer.**
- Decompose the 2025–H1 2026 gross margin move into **channel mix / product mix / raw
  material prices**. Only the second deserves a multiple.
- **The 医研 line's share of toothpaste revenue and its standalone gross margin**, split
  online versus offline. If 医研 sells almost exclusively online, then "product mix" and
  "channel mix" are the same thing counted twice.
- The split of selling expense between **platform commissions / traffic fees** and **brand
  advertising**. The first is a variable cost of revenue; only the second builds brand
  equity. **That these are disclosed as one line is the biggest analytical blind spot here.**

### Q2: Is e-commerce growth won or bought — what are the unit economics?

E-commerce is the whole of the growth: **¥588m in 2025, +22.3%, 34.3% of revenue**, a
**40.7%** CAGR since 2021; offline is implied at roughly **+4.5%** in 2025. 2024 was
stronger still — **¥480m, +51.99%, 30.81% of revenue.**

The problem is that the growth rate has halved: **+52.0% (2024) → +22.3% (2025).** And the
company itself **cut Douyin spending in Q3 2025**; the price was quarterly revenue growth of
+10.49%, followed by Q4 revenue **declining about 3.8% year on year** (derived: Q4 2025
¥488m against roughly ¥507m in Q4 2024).

**That is a natural experiment that has already run, and its result is unfavourable to the
bull case:** when spending stopped, growth stopped. That is the definition of bought growth.
By the same logic, Q1 2026's reacceleration to **+15.1%** should be presumed to be spending
switched back on until the company demonstrates otherwise.

**Ask:**
- **Net margin, or segment profit, for the e-commerce channel** — not gross margin. This is
  the single most valuable number in the memo and is entirely unavailable externally.
- Decompose e-commerce growth into **customer acquisition cost and repeat rate**; give the
  **first-purchase versus repeat split** for hero products such as 医研7天. A hero product
  that grows on repeat purchase is a completely different asset from one that grows on
  continuous paid traffic.
- After the Q3 2025 pullback, **was H1 2026 Douyin spending restored to H1 2025 intensity**,
  and what GMV did it buy? If "growth restored" and "spending restored" move together, Q2 is
  answered.
- The composition of offline's **+4.5%**: **price increases, outlet count, or sell-through
  per outlet.** Offline is where the profit actually lives, and it is growing more slowly
  than the category.

### Q3: What is the true run rate — Q4's −3.8% or Q1's +15.1%?

The last five quarters of revenue growth read like an ECG:

| Quarter | Revenue (¥100m) | YoY | Net profit (¥100m) | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2025 | 4.30 | — | 0.432 | — |
| Q2 2025 | 4.12 | — | 0.420 | — |
| Q3 2025 | 3.86 | **+10.5%** | 0.507 | +11.4% |
| Q4 2025 | **4.88** (derived) | **~ −3.8%** | 0.451 (derived) | ~ +4.5% |
| Q1 2026 | 4.95 | **+15.1%** | 0.518 | **+20.0%** |

The full-year number (+10.0%) smooths all of this away, and the volatility is the
information. **H1 2025 grew +19.7% and H2 about +2.0% — two halves of one year, eighteen
points apart.**

Three explanations fit, with very different valuation consequences: (a) **channel loading and
destocking**, i.e. noise; (b) **the cost of deliberate expense control**, a reversible
management choice; (c) **genuine deceleration as online traffic economics top out**, which is
structural. The company's own language supports (b) — but (b) and (c) look identical in the
data. **They can only be told apart after spending is restored, and Q1 2026 is exactly such
a quarter.**

**Ask:**
- **Give H1 2026 revenue and the selling expense ratio together.** If revenue is +15% and the
  expense ratio rises another 1–2pp, the answer is (b) at a stated price. If revenue is +15%
  with the ratio flat or falling, the bull case gets its first substantive evidence.
- **Distributor count and channel inventory turns**, three periods. If Q4's decline was
  distributor destocking, it shows up here.
- **The company's own attribution** for the Q4 2025 revenue decline — was it ever answered
  directly in the annual report or on the results call?

### Q4: ¥1.17bn of cash, an 80% payout, and a state parent — for whom is capital being allocated?

This is where Dengkang differs most from the typical A-share consumer company, and where both
bulls and bears tend to misread it.

**The good side first, and it is genuinely good:** receivables of just **¥33.5m** (2.0% of
revenue), inventory ¥237m, 2025 operating cash flow of **¥214m against ¥181m of net profit —
118% conversion**; no interest-bearing debt, gearing ~25%. **Strip out the ~¥1.17bn of cash
and wealth-management assets and the business runs on roughly ¥300m of net operating assets
while producing ¥155m of ex-exceptionals profit — a return on operating capital comfortably
above 50%.** This is a genuinely capital-light, high-return business; its unremarkable 12%
ROE is **entirely an artefact of the cash sitting in the denominator.**

**Now the side that needs pricing:**

1. **The largest single use of the IPO proceeds was always advertising.** Of the ¥660m
   programme, the "omni-channel marketing network upgrade and brand promotion project" was
   **¥370m, about 57%**; smart manufacturing upgrade ¥220m; the oral health research centre
   and the digital platform ¥35m each. Meanwhile the prospectus disclosed capacity
   utilisation of **85.98% / 85.38% / 85.12% / 81.27%** (2019–H1 2022) — **falling every year
   while capacity was being expanded**. **In other words, the rising selling expense ratio in
   Q1 is the IPO plan executing as designed — not a loss of control.** That makes the expense
   more predictable, and it also removes most of the basis for expecting the ratio to fall.
2. **The payout has been about 80% for four straight years** (76.7% / 79.2% / 80.1% / 80.8%);
   the 2025 dividend of ¥8.50 per 10 shares totals ¥146.35m, and cumulative distributions
   since listing are about **¥344m**, more than half the net IPO raise. At 59.83%, roughly
   **¥88m of that dividend flows back to Light Textile Group.** This is a stable, predictable
   cash channel fully aligned with the controlling shareholder — **and it is the single most
   important clue to how this company is run: as a cash-flow asset, not as a growth option.**
3. **Yet the cash is still accumulating.** ¥1.234bn at end-2024, ¥1.166bn at Q3 2025 — flat
   despite an 80% payout, meaning **cash earnings exceed the dividend.** So the question
   becomes: **what is the endgame for that ¥1.17bn?** Three possibilities — keep sitting
   there (dilutive to ROE but safe), raise the payout or buy back stock (best for minorities),
   or **fund an acquisition** (highest risk, and the one most consistent with the stated
   "four tracks" strategy).
4. **The 15th Five-Year Plan points at the third.** At the 23 July 2026 investor day the
   company set out **four tracks — oral care, smart oral care, oral medical, and innovation
   businesses** — with smart oral care to combine "professional care + smart hardware +
   digital services" and **reach a step-change in sales scale within three to five years.**
   Current scale of smart oral care: electric toothbrush revenue of about **¥5m** in H1 2024.

**Ask:** the three-year cash plan **itemised across dividends / buybacks / capex /
acquisitions**; whether any acquisition target is in progress; **the specific revenue number**
behind the smart-oral-care "three-to-five-year step change" — this is the only falsifiable
quantitative commitment management has made, and **with no equity-incentive targets on file
it is the closest public proxy for the internal budget**; and whether a buyback has been
formally discussed — **at 3.1x book, 24.8x earnings, with cash equal to 25% of market cap,
not buying back stock is a choice that requires an explanation.**

### Q5: What has to be true for a ¥4.7bn market cap?

Do not forecast forward — **reverse the valuation.** The conclusion here is counterintuitive:
**this stock is asking for very little.**

**Basis one (including cash):** at a terminal **20x** — neutral for a consumer company with a
10.5% net margin, 4–5% category growth and no debt — ¥4.71bn requires **about ¥235m of net
profit**, which is **1.30x** 2025 and lands somewhere **between the 2026 and 2027 consensus**.

**Basis two (net of cash, the more honest one):** enterprise value is about **¥3.54bn**. At
the same 20x that needs **about ¥177m of ex-exceptionals profit**, against **¥155m** actually
delivered in 2025 — **a cumulative 14% of growth.** At Q1 2026's **+32.1%** ex-exceptionals
run rate, that gap closes in under a year.

**Put differently, the current price embeds something close to "this business will never grow
profits meaningfully again"** — from a company that just posted +27.1% (2024), +14.5% (2025)
and +32.1% (Q1 2026) on that line. This is the mirror image of 洁美科技, where the price
required profit to double.

**So the question to answer is not "can it deliver?" but "why is the market here at all?"**
Three candidates, ordered by testability:

| Explanation | Evidence | Testability |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) The float changed character in April 2026** | On 10 April, **116m shares — 67.19% of the company** — came free, including 103m held by Light Textile Group; restricted stock had been 73.26% with free float of just 26.74%, across only 9,864 registered holders | **High** — one-off, already happened, checkable against the price |
| **(b) The market does not credit bought growth** | Net margin flat for six years; gross margin and selling expense move together | Medium — needs the Q1/Q2 disclosures to confirm |
| **(c) State-ownership and governance discount** | No equity incentive; the chairman's main job is now at the parent; Yufu's intent after two restructurings is unstated | Low — hard to falsify, but persistent |

**(a) deserves the most weight, because it is non-fundamental and already behind us.** Before
the unlock, free-float market value was only about **¥1.3bn** (46.03m shares at roughly ¥28) —
a price levitated by extreme scarcity. Afterwards free float is roughly 94%, and **the
de-rating is largely float normalisation rather than deteriorating fundamentals.** The stock
fell from ¥39.48 at end-2025 to about ¥27.3 (**roughly −31%**) while Q1 ex-exceptionals profit
grew 32%.

**So the real form of question five is: do you think 2026's decline priced float
normalisation (complete, non-repeatable) or growth quality (incomplete, possibly ongoing)?**
The two answers imply completely different actions.

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — The state ownership chain was restructured twice in eighteen months, and the endpoint
is a capital-operations platform.** The sequence is worth reading in full: on **2 January
2025** Jidian Group signed a custody agreement with the Chongqing SASAC, Yufu Holdings and
Light Textile Group; on **26 February 2025** Yufu injected ¥500m into Jidian by private
agreement while the SASAC transferred its 35.42% of Jidian to Yufu without consideration,
leaving Jidian 20% SASAC / 80% Yufu; registration completed **29 April 2025**; and on
**19 May 2025** Yufu and the SASAC transferred their 80% and 20% of Light Textile Group
**to Jidian**. The chain today is **Chongqing SASAC / Yufu Holdings → Jidian Group → Light
Textile Group (100%) → Dengkang (59.83%).** Ultimate control never changed, so the tender
offer requirement was waived, with Southwest Securities providing continuing supervision.

**Why this needs pricing: Yufu Holdings is Chongqing's state capital operations platform, and
asset restructuring is its reason to exist.** A listed company with ¥1.17bn of cash, a ¥4.7bn
market cap and a freshly unlocked 67% float, placed under such a platform, has at least three
possible uses: receive injected assets, act as an acquisition vehicle, or be sold down. **Those
three mean radically different things for minorities, and public information does not
distinguish them. Ask directly, on the results call, about Light Textile Group's medium-term
intentions for its stake and whether Dengkang is designated as a consolidation platform.**

**Q7 — Governance and alignment: the chairman's main job is now at the parent, and there has
been no equity incentive in three-plus years as a listed company.** Deng Rong, born 1967, a
postgraduate in business administration and a certified economist, spent his entire career
inside this factory — **starting on the tube-making floor of the Chongqing Toothpaste
Factory**, then salesman, sales section head, deputy plant manager, general manager in 2007,
chairman and GM in 2012, and Party Secretary and chairman from December 2016. **Since June
2025 he has been Party Secretary, director and general manager of Chongqing Light Textile
Holding (Group) while retaining the Dengkang chairmanship.** The board was re-elected in July
2025 with Deng continuing; CEO Zhao Fengshuo, born 1975, holds an MBA from Chongqing
University and is a senior-grade economist.

Two facts to price: (a) **the listed company chairman's primary role now sits at the
controlling shareholder** — in a company spreading across four tracks while its parent is
mid-restructuring, the top decision-maker's objective function is not identical to a
minority's; and (b) **no equity incentive or ESOP has appeared since the April 2023 listing.**
That is unremarkable for a state-controlled issuer, but it has a concrete analytical
consequence: **the most valuable base rate in the 洁美科技 memo — management's self-set
performance targets scored against actuals — simply does not exist here.** With no targets to
score, there is no yardstick for whether management systematically over-forecasts.
**The quantified 15th Five-Year Plan targets in Q4 are the only available substitute.**

**Q8 — Share supply: 10 April 2026 was the most important day in this stock's history, and it
has passed.** Four things in order: (a) on **6 February 2026**, Chongqing Department Store's
**2.9877m shares (1.74% of the company)** came free, taking restricted stock from 75.00% to
73.26% — the lock-up had been extended from 12 to 18 months from the 6 August 2024 transfer
registration because it is a concert party of the controlling shareholder; (b) on **10 April
2026**, **116m pre-IPO restricted shares, 67.19% of the company**, listed for trading across
three holders, **103m of them Light Textile Group's** — free float went from 26.74% to about
**94%** in one day; (c) there were only **9,864 registered holders** at 31 March 2026; (d)
**no buyback has ever been announced.**

**To be clear: unlocking is not selling.** As a state controlling shareholder, Light Textile
Group would need SASAC approval and pre-disclosure to sell, and its 59.83% is the basis of
control, so large-scale selling is unlikely. **But moving free float from 26.74% to 94%
changes how the stock is priced regardless** — the scarcity premium disappears and the
multiple converges on peers. **This is the simplest and most probably correct explanation for
the roughly 31% decline in 2026, and it is a one-off. Track whether any pre-disclosed
reduction plan appears, and which way the holder count moves.**

**Q9 — Category position: 60% share of a sub-category that is only 10% of the market.** This
is 冷酸灵's sharpest double edge. Share of anti-sensitivity toothpaste is about **60%** (some
sources say above 50%) — an undisputed first — but **anti-sensitivity is only about
10%–10.65% of the Chinese toothpaste market**, against roughly 26% for herbal and 25% for
whitening. Across the whole market, Nielsen 2025 offline data puts adult toothpaste **third**,
brushes **second**, children's brushes **first**. The category is mature: China's toothpaste
market is about **¥35bn** (2024) with production volume growing about **+4.4%**. The main
competitors are Yunnan Baiyao (long-standing offline leader, ~23.9%), Hawley (formerly Darlie,
~20.1%), Crest, Colgate and Saky. **So growth has to come from one of three places: categories
beyond anti-sensitivity, a move up the price ladder, or online share capture — and on all
three, the opponents are larger and better funded.** Ask for revenue and growth outside
anti-sensitivity, and 冷酸灵's share on **non-anti-sensitivity shelves**.

**Q10 — Related-party transactions: small in aggregate, but one sits directly on a profit
line.** On 21 January 2026 the board confirmed 2025 and forecast 2026 ordinary related-party
transactions: sales of goods to related parties, **acceptance of banking-deposit services from
a related party**, and **collection and onward payment of government grants (¥20m forecast for
2026, collected by Jidian / Light Textile Group and passed through)**. Related directors
**Xiang Zhiyong and Li Jiangyi (Jidian) and Deng Rong (Light Textile) recused themselves.**

**The banking-deposit item deserves separate attention.** The company holds about ¥1.17bn of
cash and wealth-management assets, and non-recurring items (¥181m reported versus ¥155m
ex-exceptionals — a gap of about **¥26m, 14% of net profit**) consist largely of investment
income. **If those deposits sit at a related-party bank, the deposit rate is a channel through
which profit can leak into the related group** — structurally the same issue as 洁美科技's
related-party steam contract, on a far smaller scale. Separately, **¥20m of government grants
routed through the parent** equals about **11% of 2025 net profit**, and both its durability
and its timing deserve a question. **Ask for the related-party deposit balance and rate,
benchmarked against comparable market products.**

**Q11 — Earnings quality: about 14% of net profit is not operating.** 2023: ¥141m reported vs
¥106m ex-exceptionals (a 25% gap); 2024: ¥161m vs ¥135m (16%); 2025: ¥181m vs ¥155m (14%).
The gap is mostly **wealth-management income on idle IPO proceeds and own funds, plus
government grants.** At 31 December 2025 the outstanding balance of cash-management products
bought with idle IPO proceeds was **¥108m**, against a ¥300m ceiling authorised at the
21 May 2025 AGM. **The gap is narrowing (25% → 16% → 14%), which is the right direction**, but
it also means **this slice of profit faces a falling-rate headwind, and the ex-exceptionals
line is the one that should be valued.** Basis two in Q5 is built on exactly that line.

**Q12 — Source quality and disclosure gaps.** Essentially every primary disclosure site was
blocked at the network egress layer for this analysis (see the preamble to Sources); all data
is second-hand. The following **could not be obtained and therefore convert directly into
questions**: **the H1 2026 interim report** (statutorily due by 31 August 2026; not found in
search, and the largest recency gap here), **the annual-report figures for full-year 2025
consolidated gross margin and selling expense ratio** (this memo uses derived values, marked
as approximate), **gross margin by channel**, **e-commerce channel profitability**,
**distributor count and top-five customer concentration**, **contract liabilities**, **the
related-party deposit rate**, and **quantified targets for each of the four strategic tracks**.
Two conflicts were reconciled rather than resolved by choice: 2024 e-commerce growth appears as
both **+51.99% (e-commerce, 30.81% of revenue)** and **+42% ("e-commerce and other", 34%)** —
different denominators; and Kaiyuan's estimates are labelled "2025–2027" by some aggregators,
but the report is dated **June 2026** and 2025 actual net profit was already ¥181m, so **this
memo reads them as 2026–2028** — its ¥217m for 2026 (+19.8%) matching Q1 2026's actual +20.0%
corroborates that reading.

## 3. What to watch next — falsifiable checkpoints

1. **The H1 2026 interim report (statutory deadline 31 August 2026 — i.e. now).** The highest
   value single event; it answers Q1, Q2 and Q3 at once. Four numbers, in order of importance:
   **(i) whether gross margin and the selling expense ratio still move in the same direction**
   (gross margin up with the expense ratio down would overturn Q1's conclusion and is the
   strongest evidence the bull case could get); **(ii) whether net margin breaks 11% for the
   first time** in six years; **(iii) e-commerce growth** against a high H1 2025 base; and
   **(iv) ex-exceptionals profit growth** (+32.1% in Q1).
2. **Whether gross margin is broken out by channel or product.** The whole of Q1 reduces to
   whether this disclosure exists. **Non-disclosure is itself information, and means gross
   margin should be read as channel mix rather than premiumisation.**
3. **Q3 2026 (late October) — the control experiment against Q3 2025.** Q3 2025 was the
   deliberate-pullback quarter (gross margin 45.9%, selling expense ratio 25.51%). If Q3 2026
   returns to double-digit revenue growth with the expense ratio back up, growth is
   spend-driven. If the ratio stays low and revenue still grows, Q2's conclusion turns
   favourable.
4. **Whether Q4 2026 revenue returns to growth.** Q4 2025's roughly −3.8% is the only negative
   quarter in recent years, so the base is very low. Failing to grow double digits off it
   would move the explanation in Q3 from (b) to (c).
5. **Post-unlock reduction disclosures and the holder count.** After the 10 April release of
   116m shares, track any Light Textile Group reduction plan, the direction of the registered
   holder count, and block-trade discounts. **This is the most direct test of Q8's claim that
   the decline is already priced.**
6. **Any buyback or dividend increase.** At 3.1x book, with cash at about 25% of market cap and
   the stock down roughly 31% year to date, **management's stance on buybacks is the cheapest
   available read on its capital-allocation priorities.**
7. **Whether the four-track targets are quantified**, particularly the revenue figure behind
   smart oral care's "step change in three to five years." **With no equity-incentive targets
   in existence, this is the only anchor for assessing management's own expectations.**
8. **Action at the Yufu / Jidian level.** Watch for changes in Light Textile Group's stake, any
   restructuring or capital-increase filing involving Dengkang, and actual receipt of the ¥20m
   of government grants.
9. **The direction in which sell-side targets and the price converge.** Targets of ¥44–52
   against roughly ¥27.3 cannot persist; which side moves is itself a vote between
   explanations (a) and (b) in Q5.
10. **Whether the payout ratio holds at 80%.** Four straight years at 76.7% / 79.2% / 80.1% /
    80.8%. If that breaks — especially to retain cash for an acquisition — the capital
    allocation read in Q4 has to be rewritten.

## 4. Provisional read

**On the business**, it is better than its share price suggests and more ordinary than its
narrative claims. 冷酸灵's roughly 60% share of anti-sensitivity toothpaste is real and secure,
and an eighty-year-old offline distribution network is not something a new brand replicates in
three years. The balance sheet is the cleanest analysed in this repository: receivables at 2.0%
of revenue, no interest-bearing debt, 118% cash conversion, and — net of about ¥1.17bn of cash —
roughly ¥300m of net operating assets producing ¥155m of ex-exceptionals profit. Four straight
years at an ~80% payout is a coherent, minority-friendly capital policy. **But the growth is
bought, and the company proved it itself in Q3 2025 with a deliberate pullback: stop the
spending and the growth stops.** The handsome climb in gross margin from 42% to 49% has, after
netting the equally rising selling expense ratio, delivered nothing whatsoever to net margin in
six years.

**On the stock**, the answer is clearer and more interesting. You are buying at roughly
**24.8x trailing, ~21.7x 2026 consensus, 3.1x book, on a 3.11% yield**, a company that just
grew net profit 12.4% and ex-exceptionals profit 14.5%, holding cash equal to **25% of its
market cap**. **Net of that cash, a ¥3.54bn enterprise value at a 20x terminal multiple needs
only 14% more ex-exceptionals profit — under a year at the current run rate.** The gap between
that and the sell-side's ¥44–52 targets (61%–90% above the price) is the real issue in this name.

**And the most probable explanation for the gap is not fundamental: on 10 April 2026, 116m
shares — 67.19% of the company — were released, taking free float from 26.74% to about 94%.**
A stock whose free-float value had been only about ¥1.3bn lost its scarcity premium. The
roughly 31% decline this year lines up closely with that event, while Q1 ex-exceptionals profit
grew 32%. **If that explanation holds, the decline priced a structural change that is complete
and cannot repeat.**

**Governance does not decide the conclusion, but it will keep suppressing the multiple.** The
controlling shareholder holds 59.83% under the Chongqing SASAC; the chairman's principal role
has been at the parent since June 2025; there has been no equity incentive in three-plus years
and therefore no falsifiable performance commitment management has made to itself; and the
parent has just been restructured twice in eighteen months into Yufu Holdings, a capital
operations platform whose medium-term intentions for Dengkang are unstated. **None of this is a
reason to sell, but together they explain why a company with this cash-flow quality does not
command a normal consumer multiple.**

The honest statement: **a real business that has stopped expanding its margin, an exceptionally
clean balance sheet, a float normalisation that has already happened, and a state governance
structure with no incentive constraint.** What would flip the "growth is bought" conclusion is
narrow and specific: **in the H1 or Q3 2026 report, seeing gross margin and the selling expense
ratio move in opposite directions for the first time — gross margin up, selling expense down —
with net margin breaking 11% and holding for two consecutive quarters.** That would rewrite the
story from "buying revenue with IPO-funded advertising" to "premiumisation finally produced
pricing power," and it is worth far more than any hero-product GMV headline.

**The next step is one thing only: find and read the H1 2026 interim report.** Its statutory
deadline is today, and this research could not retrieve it (every primary disclosure host is
blocked in this environment). **Until it is read, every judgement here about the run rate rests
on the single observation of Q1 2026.** When you have it, go straight to two places: **cost of
sales and selling expenses in the consolidated income statement** (compute the direction of both
ratios year on year, which settles Q1), and **the revenue-and-gross-margin-by-channel or
by-product note** (if present, Q2 closes too).
