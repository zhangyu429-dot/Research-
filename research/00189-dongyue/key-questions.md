# 东岳集团 (Dongyue Group, 00189.HK) — The Questions That Decide the Investment

*Research memo · 25 August 2026 · analysis, not investment advice*

---

## 0. What you are actually buying

Dongyue is not a "fluorosilicone new materials company." It is **an administratively created
quasi-rent asset (refrigerant quota) wrapped in three layers of dilution**: a layer of
commodity chemicals (fluoropolymers, dichloromethane/PVC/caustic), a layer of silicones that
is 100% consolidated but only 44.4% owned (Dongyue Silicone, 300821.SZ), and a share
structure that hands roughly a quarter of profit to minorities outside the listco. The money
is made in refrigerants: 1H2025 segment revenue ¥2.29bn, segment profit ¥1.03bn — a **44.9%
segment margin**, one half-year of segment profit exceeding the full-year attributable
profit. Everything else is either a valuation drag on that rent or a claim on part of it.
And the asset with the best story — the hydrogen fuel-cell proton exchange membrane business
(Dongyue Future Hydrogen Energy Materials) — **is largely no longer inside the listco**: the
listco's stake is down to roughly 10%, and the actual controller is chairman Zhang Jianhong
personally.

progress.

> **The single fact that frames everything:** In 2022 Dongyue earned **¥3.856bn** attributable
> — the best year in its history. In 2025 — with refrigerant prices at ten-year highs and the
> quota dividend fully in the numbers — it earned **¥1.642bn, 43% of 2022**, on 28% less
> revenue. The difference is not refrigerants. **2022 was a PVDF year**: lithium-battery
> demand pushed PVDF to a record, and today battery-grade PVDF quotes ¥57,000–65,000/t with
> industry utilisation around 60%. In other words, **this company's last "best year ever" ran
> on a product that is now oversupplied and barely profitable, and that peak lasted exactly
> one year — 2023 attributable profit fell to ¥708m, −81.6%.** Today's bull case is another
> price cycle in a different product. **Judging this stock is, at bottom, judging whether
> quota-era refrigerant profit and 2022's PVDF profit are the same kind of thing.**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1 — Every product price rose. Revenue rose 8%. Where did the volume go, and what price is the company actually realising?

The most diagnostic number, and the one most easily skipped. In 1H2026, on sell-side price
tracking, **R22 +43.8%, R125 +14.6%, R134a +10.3%, R32 +1.6%, PTFE +23.5%, PVDF +19.2%**,
silicone DMC from ¥11,300/t to ¥13,500–14,000/t, and Dongyue Silicone's attributable profit
up roughly 9x. Against that, **group revenue rose 8.0% (¥7.463bn → ¥8.060bn) and attributable
profit 31.8%.**

Two explanations fit, with opposite implications:

1. **Realised prices lag spot badly.** Domestic refrigerant sales are largely annual
   contracts priced at the start of the year; the spot surge (R22 from ¥21,000–22,000/t in
   May to ¥35,000/t by end-July) only shows up in the next contract round. *Bullish — the
   2026 H2 and 2027 repricing lifts profit.*
2. **Volumes are falling.** Quota caps refrigerant output (gen-2 shrinking annually),
   fluoropolymer external sales were already −16.0% in FY2024, and silicones are deliberately
   selling less under coordinated industry cuts (−30% Dec 2025–Feb 2026, −40% Jun–Aug 2026,
   utilisation from ~70% to ~60%). *Bearish — price is paying for lost volume, and when the
   discipline lapses, price and volume fall together.*

A rough back-calculation quantifies it: FY2025 refrigerant segment revenue of ¥4.941bn across
roughly 130kt of quota implies a realised price of about **¥38,000/t**, against R32 spot of
¥63,000–65,000/t, R125 spot of ¥46,000–55,000/t and R22 spot at ¥35,000/t. **The gap between
realisation and spot is too large to ignore.** Whether it is timing (contract lag) or
structure (feedstock-use R22 transferred internally at cost, exports below domestic, a high
share of low-priced contracts) determines directly whether the ¥2.69–2.95bn FY2026 consensus
is achievable.

> **How much does the consensus ask of 2H? Measure it against the company's own half-year
> rhythm.** 1H attributable profit was ¥1.027bn. A ¥2.694bn full year requires **¥1.667bn in
> 2H, +62% half-on-half**; ¥2.953bn requires **¥1.926bn, +88%**.
>
> The intuitive objection is that the air-conditioner build season falls in March–June. **But
> this company's actual rhythm has run the other way for two years:** attributable profit was
> ¥308m in 1H2024 vs ¥503m in 2H2024 (**+63.5% half-on-half**) and ¥779m in 1H2025 vs ¥863m in
> 2H2025 (**+10.8%**); the refrigerant segment ran ¥1.552bn revenue / ¥332m profit in 1H2024
> against ¥1.696bn / ¥474m in 2H2024, and ¥2.292bn / ¥1.030bn in 1H2025 against ¥2.649bn /
> ¥1.258bn in 2H2025 — **revenue and profit both higher in the second half, two years
> running**. The cause is not demand seasonality but prices climbing through the year under
> quota, with annual contracts repricing each round.
>
> So the conclusion needs revising: **+62% (the ¥2.694bn case) has a 2024 precedent and is not
> absurd; +88% (the ¥2.953bn case) has none.** The test is not seasonality but whether R22
> holds the move from ¥21,000–22,000/t in May to ¥35,000/t at end-July through Q3.

**Ask:**
- From the full interim report: **refrigerant segment sales volume (tonnes) and segment
  profit**, item by item against 1H2025.
- The **share of internal-use quota sold on annual contracts and the contract average price**
  for R32/R125/R134a in 2026; when 2027 contract negotiations begin.
- The **internal transfer price of feedstock R22** (for TFE → PTFE) and its share of the R22
  quota — feedstock transferred at cost systematically depresses the refrigerant segment's
  apparent realisation while the profit shows up in fluoropolymers.
- 1H2026 silicone **volumes**, and what the coordinated cuts did to Dongyue Silicone's unit
  costs.

### Q2 (reverse valuation) — What **normal** profit does a ¥25.9bn market cap require? Not growth — durability

Do not forecast into the valuation; invert it. Market cap ~**¥25.9bn** (HK$28.3bn):

| Terminal multiple assumed | Implied required attributable profit | Multiple of FY2025 (¥1.642bn) |
|---|---|---|
| 8x (cyclical chemicals, low end) | ¥3.24bn | **1.97x** |
| 10x (HK mid-cap chemicals centre) | ¥2.59bn | **1.58x** |
| 12x (with a quota-scarcity premium) | ¥2.16bn | **1.32x** |
| 15x (A-share peer multiples converging to HK) | ¥1.73bn | 1.05x |

The conclusion is clean: **at this price the market is not asking for much growth — it is
asking that ¥2.5–3.0bn be normal rather than peak.** On CICC's ¥2.694bn for FY2026 the stock
is at **9.6x**; on ¥3.181bn for FY2027, **8.1x**. Those multiples look cheap, and cheapness is
exactly how the market prices a judgement that this is a cycle peak.

The company's own history is the sharpest counter-evidence: **¥3.856bn in 2022, ¥708m in
2023, −81.6% in one year.** The last time this company earned above ¥2.5bn, it lasted a
single year.

**Ask:**
- Quota scarcity is permanent; **price is not.** Ask management for one number: what is
  refrigerant segment profit if R32 spot falls to ¥40,000/t (about 60% of current)? That is
  the only sensitivity that matters.
- Total capex, committed and planned, for 2026–2027 and where it goes. **If quota caps
  output, capex should trend toward maintenance — if it doesn't, where is the money going?**
- How management explains the 2022→2023 collapse and why this time differs. Make them do the
  attribution, then check the duration of each driver they name.

### Q3 — R22 is the most profitable product today, and its quota goes to roughly zero in 2030

The most under-appreciated structural question here, and the least reversible.

Dongyue has China's largest R22 production quota: **43,000t in 2026, ~27.3% of the gen-2
quota** (national R22 production quota 146,100t, internal-use 78,000t). R22 is also 2026's
sharpest mover: 1H average price **+43.8%** YoY, ¥21,000–22,000/t in May, ¥31,000/t by
end-June, an ex-factory reference of **¥35,000/t** on 30 July.

But R22 is an HCFC under the Montreal Protocol, and China's compliance schedule is: **−67.5%
against baseline by 2025, cut to 2.5% of baseline by 2030** (servicing only), full phase-out
by 2040. Between 2026 and 2030 China must still remove roughly 30% of baseline production.
The 2025 national HCFC production quota already fell to 163,600t (−49,900t) and internal-use
to 86,000t (−42,100t). **The product delivering today's price leverage has to lose more than
90% of its controlled-use volume within four years.**

The offset: **feedstock use is exempt from the phase-out.** Dongyue cracks R22 into TFE and
polymerises it into PTFE. In principle the cut controlled-use quota can be redirected to
feedstock — volume preserved, profit simply migrating from the refrigerant segment to
fluoropolymers. But feedstock R22 is "priced near cost." **Redirect it and the excess profit
disappears.**

**This question just got heavier:** FY2025 refrigerant segment profit of ¥2.288bn was ~89% of
group pre-tax profit. **R22's share of refrigerants is therefore very close to R22's share of
the entire company.**

**Ask:**
- Of Dongyue's **43,000t of 2026 R22 quota, how many tonnes are controlled use (domestic +
  export) and how many are feedstock?**
- **How much of refrigerant segment profit is R22?** (The single most valuable number in this
  memo.)
- What internal assumption does the company hold for the 2027–2030 controlled-quota path?
- Can gen-3 (HFCs) fill the hole? The 2026 HFC production quota is locked at 797,800t and the
  inter-variety adjustment limit has been widened from 10% to 30%. **Is widening the
  adjustment ratio added flexibility, or diluted scarcity per variety?** The two readings
  have opposite implications for R125 and R134a pricing.

### Q4 — Who actually owns this profit? A quarter leaks out, and the company did it to itself

In FY2025 group net profit was ¥2.184bn and attributable profit ¥1.642bn: **minorities took
¥542m, 24.8%** (FY2024 17.9%, 1H2025 26.3%). **As refrigerant and silicone profits rise, that
share keeps widening.**

The cause is the 2023–24 decoupling from Xinhualian Holdings:

- On 23 Oct 2023 Dongyue agreed to repurchase and cancel **521m shares (23.12% of capital)**
  held by Xinhualian for **HK$3.699bn (≈¥3.457bn, HK$7.10/share, a 32.92% premium to the
  HK$5.34 close)**;
- the same day, wholly-owned Dongyue Fluorosilicone agreed to sell Xinhualian, for an
  identical ¥3.457bn, **~23.12% of Shandong Dongyue Polymer (¥1.752bn), ~13.35% of Dongyue
  Silicone (¥1.540bn) and ~2.32% of Dongyue Future Hydrogen (¥165m)**;
- the two were conditional on each other; the equity transfers completed 5 Mar 2024 and the
  521m shares were cancelled on 3 May 2024.

So today: **the listco owns only ~76.9% of Dongyue Polymer, its core fluorochemical operating
entity**, with 23.12% held by a property group in bankruptcy restructuring that is
distributing assets to creditors in lieu of debt; and it owns **44.4% of the silicone
subsidiary while consolidating 100%** — all of the revenue in, 55.6% of the profit out.

The same transaction created a sum-of-the-parts oddity the market has not obviously priced:

- Dongyue Silicone (300821.SZ) has ~1.204bn shares; an implied ~¥20.5/share at 30 June 2026
  (back-solved from 102,065 holder accounts × 11,800 shares × ¥241,700 average account value)
  and a ¥23.23 close on 14 July, up over 160% YTD, put its market cap at **¥24.7–28.0bn**;
- the listco's 44.4% is therefore worth roughly **¥11.0–12.4bn**, i.e. **43%–48% of Dongyue
  Group's own ¥25.9bn market cap**;
- the implied stub — refrigerants + fluoropolymers + chlor-alkali — is about **¥14.2bn**, and
  in FY2025 that stub produced essentially all of the ¥1.642bn attributable profit:
  **~8.7x earnings.**

This is not a valuation conclusion (it deducts neither net debt nor the 23.12% minority in
Dongyue Polymer), but it forces the question: **is the market discounting the refrigerant
rent, or discounting an over-valued A-share silicone stake?**

**Ask:**
- **Dongyue Polymer's standalone accounts** (revenue, net profit, net assets), and the status
  of Xinhualian's 23.12% — transferred to creditors yet, and is there any repurchase or
  pre-emption arrangement?
- Does the listco intend to buy that 23.12% back, and at what price? **This is the only route
  to putting a quarter of the profit back inside the listco, and the most direct value
  creation available.**
- What **cash dividend** does Dongyue Silicone pay up? (Ex-date 21 May 2026, ¥0.02/share; at
  44.4% that is under ¥11m of cash returning to the listco. **Consolidating 100% of the
  profit while receiving single-digit-million cash is a textbook gap between book profit and
  spendable cash.**)
- On the 2023 buyback: paying a **32.92% premium** to retire a bankrupt shareholder's stake
  while handing over core subsidiary equity — did that create or destroy per-share value for
  **continuing** shareholders? Ask management to answer in the segment-valuation terms they
  used at the time.

### Q5 — Is the best story still inside the listco? Hydrogen, PEM membrane and connected transactions

The official narrative is four pillars: fluorine, silicon, membrane, hydrogen. But the
vehicle for "membrane" and "hydrogen" — **Shandong Dongyue Future Hydrogen Energy Materials**
— is largely outside the listco:

- its technology came from inside the group: **patents and equipment were transferred to the
  issuer** from Dongyue Fluorosilicone, Huaxia Shenzhou and Dongyue Polymer;
- ownership has moved repeatedly: Dongyue Fluorosilicone held **31.21%** at one point; on the
  latest IPO tutoring filing the largest holder is **Beijing Xuri at 22.02% with Dongyue Group
  at ~10.03%**;
- **in September 2024 Zhang Jianhong exited the relevant partnerships, with the interests
  passing to Zhang Ke, briefly leaving him under 1%; the latest filing shows Zhang Jianhong as
  the entity's actual controller;**
- valuation reference: the 2.32% sold in 2023 was priced at ¥165m, implying **~¥7.1bn for
  100%** — more than 60% of Dongyue Group's own market cap at the time;
- **it filed for a STAR listing on 30 Sep 2025 seeking ¥2.446bn, was picked for an on-site
  inspection in October 2025, and withdrew on 30 June 2026** with the SSE terminating review.
  This was the third attempt at listing the group's hydrogen assets.
- **There is a continuing connected transaction with the listco:** on 2 Jan 2026 Dongyue
  Fluorosilicone renewed the raw-material supply / product sales framework agreement VI with
  Future Materials through 31 Dec 2026.

None of this is an allegation — incubating and spinning out is ordinary for a group, and some
of the moves happened outside the listco. But for a minority holder of 00189 the structure is
plain: **the listco supplied the original technology and keeps supplying raw materials, while
retaining ~10% of the economics; the rest belongs to entities controlled by the chairman.**

**Ask (request written answers, item by item):**
- The listco's **exact current stake** in Dongyue Future Hydrogen, plus a full ownership
  history: each dilution, its date, price and pricing basis. (The 31.21% and 10.03% figures
  refer to different entities at different dates; a complete table is needed.)
- Under framework agreement VI: the **annual cap, actual amounts transacted, and pricing
  method**, plus the independent non-executive directors' opinion and which directors
  recused.
- The **real reason** for the STAR withdrawal — "adjustment of business development plans" is
  boilerplate. Was there a link to the October 2025 on-site inspection? What is the timetable
  for refiling?
- If Future Materials eventually lists independently, **how do listco shareholders share in
  the value?** Is there any compensation, pre-emption or anti-dilution arrangement?

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — How long can the silicone "anti-involution" hold?** Dongyue Silicone guided 1H2026
attributable profit to ¥424–444m, +904.88%–952.28% (Q1 ¥194m, +427%). The driver is explicit
and exogenous: an industry leaders' meeting on 12 Nov 2025 agreed a 30% industry-wide load
reduction; Hoshine convened an implementation meeting on 18 Nov to formalise output and price
coordination; cuts ran at 30% from Dec 2025 to Feb 2026 and **40% from June to August 2026**,
taking utilisation from ~70% to ~60%; incumbents are barred from adding capacity in 2025–2027;
2026 sees essentially no new domestic capacity and a supply gap widening to ~110kt. DMC went
from ~¥11,300/t in late October 2025 to ¥13,500–14,000/t. **This is a price held up by
industry discipline, not by a cost curve or a patent.** Ask: does the coordination continue
past September? How is compliance verified? How fast does price retrace if one leader
restarts? **Two quality details worth recording:** first, Dongyue Silicone's 1H2026 **profit excluding
non-recurring items, ¥465–485m, is higher than headline attributable profit of ¥424–444m** —
net non-recurring items of about **−¥41m**, meaning **no fire-insurance recovery or similar
one-off is propping up the number**; the underlying result is better than the headline.
Second, Q1 2026 revenue was ¥1.256bn, **up only 4.49%** — **the entire silicone recovery is a
margin event on flat volume, not a demand event.** Together: the profit is real, but it is
wholly dependent on the coordinated cuts. Separately, part of the gross margin gain came from
**lower industrial silicon input costs**, which reverse when silicon rebounds — that must be split out from the price effect.

**Q7 — Customers and downstream concentration.** Domestic refrigerant demand concentrates in
room air-conditioner OEMs (Midea, Gree, Haier) and auto air-conditioning — simultaneously the
contract counterparties and the other side of the policy lobby. The new fluoropolymer story is
PTFE's "AI moment": prices bottomed and rebounded through 2026 to ¥52,000/t on 5 June, +23.81%
YTD, with domestic CR3 ~57% (Dongyue, Haohua Chenguang, Juhua). **What needs checking is how
much of Dongyue's PTFE is genuinely high-frequency-communications or semiconductor grade.**
General-purpose PTFE rising is fluorspar and R22 cost push; the high-end grades are the import
substitution story, and the two differ by an order of magnitude in valuation terms. Request
PTFE volumes by grade and by end industry, plus a three-period top-5 customer concentration
trend.

**Q8 — Cycle position.** Refrigerant spot is at ten-year highs: on 16 June 2026 R32 was
¥63,000–63,500/t and R134a ¥62,000–63,000/t; in April 2026 average unit profit was ¥47,221/t
on R32 and ¥32,611/t on R125. **Unit profit approaching 70% of unit price is an extreme, not a
normal.** Quota will keep supply constrained, but the current spread carries a large restock
and sentiment component — on 10 July 2026 the company issued a positive profit alert and the
shares fell over 7%, which is the market already pricing a peak. The question is not whether
price retraces but **where it stabilises**: if R32 settles at ¥45,000–50,000/t, what is normal
profit?

**Q9 — Balance sheet and cash conversion (the biggest gap).** This research pass **could not
obtain operating cash flow for any year**, nor absolute receivables, inventory or contract
liabilities. That is not acceptable, because this is precisely where you find out whether
quota profit is cash. What is known: FY2025 BVPS ~¥9.25 (attributable equity ~¥16.0bn) and a
gearing ratio around 37%. **Must be filled in:** FY2024–FY2025 operating cash flow against
attributable profit; receivables and inventory growth against revenue growth; capex to
depreciation; net cash or net debt; and how much of Dongyue Silicone's cash (consolidated but
44.4% owned) is actually available to the group. **The gap between consolidated profit and
distributable cash is the most likely place for a problem to hide in this structure.**

**Q10 — Share supply and the register.** After cancelling 521m shares (23.12%) in May 2024,

## 4. Provisional read

**The business.** Refrigerant quota is one of the few genuine rents in Chinese chemicals:
capacity cannot be built, shares are effectively frozen, and Dongyue holds China's largest R22
quota and the #2 R32 position. That moat is real and has years left. **But it has two cracks
the market under-weights.** First, R22 — today's highest-leverage product — must fall to 2.5%
of baseline controlled use by 2030: more than 90% of the volume disappears within four years.
Second, the rent **does not fully belong to 00189's shareholders**: 23.12% of Dongyue Polymer
and 55.6% of Dongyue Silicone sit outside, minorities already took 24.8% of profit in FY2025,
and that structure is one the company created itself in 2023 by paying a 32.9% premium to
retire a shareholder. As for "membrane" and "hydrogen" — the two pillars that would support a
long-duration multiple — the economics have largely left the listco. **It is also far more concentrated than "fluorosilicone platform" suggests: FY2025 refrigerant
segment profit of ¥2.288bn was ~89% of group pre-tax profit, while fluoropolymer segment
profit fell from ¥508m to ¥357m, silicones lost money and chlor-alkali declined. So: the
business is more real than it looks (the rent is genuine), smaller than it looks (the
shareholders' share is discounted twice), and more concentrated than it looks (it stands on
one leg).**

**The stock.** 15.8x trailing, 9.6x FY2026 consensus, 1.6x book, 10.3% ROE — that is not
expensive. The reverse valuation says **the current price only requires normal profit of about
¥2.5–3.0bn, not growth of any kind.** The sum-of-the-parts goes further: strip out the 44.4%
of Dongyue Silicone at market (~¥11.0–12.4bn) and the remaining refrigerant + fluoropolymer +
chlor-alkali business is implied at roughly **8.7x**, while producing essentially all the
profit. **If the quota rent is durable, this price is cheap.** Two things stop it being a
simple value case. **First**, the last time this company earned at this level (¥3.856bn in
2022) it lasted one year and then fell 81.6%; the market's peak-memory has a basis. **Second**,
the shares fell over 7% on the day of the 10 July 2026 profit alert, meaning the marginal
buyer is already pricing a top — and in a Hong Kong line where southbound holds 32.5% and the
local bid is thin, there is no cushion when consensus turns.

**What would flip it.** Against the bull case: the interim report showing refrigerant
**volume** down rather than realisation lagging; the silicone coordination breaking in
September; the 2027 quota draft accelerating the R22 cut. Against the bear case: interim cash
flow validating the profit (OCF/attributable > 1); another large increase in the final
dividend; and an announced buyback of the 23.12% of Dongyue Polymer — **that last one would
remove the main reason for the structural discount in a single move.**

**Concrete next step:** obtain the PDFs of the **FY2025 annual report** and the **1H2026
interim report**, fill in every blank cell in this memo against the Q9 checklist (operating
cash flow, segment volumes, the composition of minority interests above all), and complete the
ownership history tables behind Q4 and Q5. Until those two originals are in hand, this memo is
a question list, not a conclusion.
