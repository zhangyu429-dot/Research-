## 0. What you are actually buying

Dongyue is **a cash machine driven by administrative quota, more than a third of whose output
does not belong to the listco's shareholders.** In 1H2026 group net profit rose 54.6% while
**attributable profit rose only 31.8%** — the difference went to minorities: profit
attributable to non-controlling interests jumped from ¥278m to **¥607m (+118.6%), 37.2% of net
profit** (1H2025: 26.3%). That is not an accident but the structure: the silicone subsidiary
Dongyue Silicone is 100% consolidated on a 44.4% stake, and 23.12% of the core fluorochemical
operating entity Dongyue Polymer sits outside — **and those two are exactly where the growth
came from this half.** Meanwhile the company holds ¥6.065bn of net cash against ¥1.8m of
borrowings, and generated ¥2.088bn of operating cash flow (1.28x net profit). **So this is an
unusual combination: the cash is real, the balance sheet is pristine, the valuation is
undemanding — but the leakage is accelerating, and the assets with the most imagination
attached ("membrane" and "hydrogen") are essentially no longer inside the listco.**

## 1. The five questions that decide the outcome

### Q1 — Minorities are taking 40% of the profit increment. Does this structure get fixed, or keep getting worse?

The numbers are no longer arguable:

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H2025 | **1H2026** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net profit (¥bn) | 0.987 | 2.184 | 1.057 | **1.634** |
| Attributable profit (¥bn) | 0.811 | 1.642 | 0.779 | **1.027** |
| **Minority interests (¥bn)** | 0.177 | 0.542 | 0.278 | **0.607** |
| **Share of net profit** | 17.9% | 24.8% | 26.3% | **37.2%** |

The share has risen monotonically across four observation periods, and **the rate of increase
is accelerating**. The cause is structural and predictable: of this half's ¥549m increase in
segment result, **silicones contributed ¥227m (the listco owns 44.4%) and fluoropolymers
¥179m (23.12% of Dongyue Polymer is outside)** — the two fastest-growing pieces are exactly
the two leakiest. The balance sheet says the same: **non-controlling interests are ¥6.198bn,
28.94% of total equity.**

The company built this structure itself in October 2023: HK$3.699bn (HK$7.10/share, a 32.92%
premium to the then close) to repurchase and cancel Xinhualian's 521m shares (23.12%), paid
for by selling 23.12% of Dongyue Polymer (¥1.752bn), 13.35% of Dongyue Silicone (¥1.540bn) and
2.32% of Dongyue Future Hydrogen (¥165m). **The "23% EPS accretion" case was valid at the
time; with refrigerants and silicones now rising together, the other side of that trade has
surfaced — what the company sold was the part worth most in an upcycle.**

**Ask:**
- **Dongyue Polymer's standalone accounts** (revenue, net profit, net assets), and **who holds
  Xinhualian's 23.12% today** — has the restructuring transferred it to creditors? Is that
  holding dispersed or concentrated?
- Does the listco intend to buy the 23.12% back, **at what price, and is there a right of
  first refusal or tag-along?** On the 2023 price of ¥1.752bn, buying it back at a similar
  multiple would pay back very quickly against this half's annualised fluoropolymer segment
  result — **if the answer is no, the reason has to be articulable.**
- Dongyue Silicone's ex-date of 21 May 2026 paid ¥0.02/share; at 44.4% that returns under
  ¥11m of cash to the listco. **Consolidating 100% of the profit while receiving
  single-digit-million cash** — set out how much of parent-level available cash actually comes
  from the silicone subsidiary and how much is constrained by its minorities.
- Of the ¥6.065bn of net cash, how much sits in Dongyue Silicone (44.4%) and Dongyue Polymer
  (76.88%)? **What is genuinely available to listco shareholders?**

### Q2 (reverse valuation) — After stripping net cash, what **normal** profit does a ¥21.26bn enterprise value require?

Clean up the balance sheet first, then invert. Market cap ¥25.57bn less ~¥4.31bn of
attributable net cash leaves an **enterprise value of ~¥21.26bn**:

| Terminal multiple assumed | Implied required attributable profit | vs FY2025 (¥1.642bn) | vs 1H2026 annualised (¥2.054bn) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8x | ¥2.658bn | 1.62x | 1.29x |
| **10x** | **¥2.126bn** | **1.29x** | **1.04x** |
| 12x | ¥1.772bn | 1.08x | 0.86x |
| 15x | ¥1.418bn | 0.86x | 0.69x |

**This is a looser conclusion than the previous version reached, and net cash is why:** without
stripping cash, 10x required ¥2.59bn; after stripping it, **only ¥2.13bn — roughly holding the
1H2026 run-rate.** In other words, **the current price asks for almost no growth; it asks only
that profit not fall back.**

"Not falling back" is not a mild assumption at this company:
- **FY2022 attributable profit ¥3.856bn; FY2023 ¥708m — down 81.6% in one year.**
- The last cycle's forecast scorecard: for FY2025, two houses forecast ¥2.177bn and ¥1.90bn
  against an actual ¥1.642bn — **33% and 16% too high**; First Shanghai's FY2025 revenue
  forecast of ¥16.09bn came in against ¥14.355bn, **12% too high. Both erred in the same
  direction in the same year.**
- This cycle: CICC ¥2.694bn / ¥3.181bn for FY2026/27 (target HK$16.68); First Shanghai
  ¥2.58bn / ¥2.81bn (target HK$19.5); another house ¥2.953bn / ¥3.023bn. With ¥1.027bn in 1H,
  **¥2.694bn requires ¥1.667bn in 2H, +62% half-on-half.**

On that +62%: the previous version had the seasonality backwards. The correction, with the
right test: **this company's attributable profit has been higher in the second half two years
running** — ¥308m/¥503m in 2024 (**+63.5%**) and ¥779m/¥863m in 2025 (**+10.8%**); the
refrigerant segment likewise. The cause is not demand seasonality but prices climbing through
the year under quota, with contracts repricing each round. **So +62% has a 2024 precedent and
is not absurd; the +88% implied by ¥2.953bn has none.**

**Ask:**
- **The only sensitivity that matters:** what is refrigerant segment result if R32 spot falls
  to ¥40,000/t (about 60% of current) and R125 to ¥35,000/t? That segment ran a **48.51%**
  margin this half and is the company's only high-margin source.
- Capex is **rising**, not trending to maintenance: ¥855m in 1H (+24.1%), **1.27x** the ¥671m
  of depreciation, with management saying newly approved industrialisation and research
  projects carry **"expected new investment of over ¥1bn"**. **In a business whose output is
  capped by quota, where is this money going and at what required return?**
- The effective tax rate fell from 25.70% to **17.43%** (15% high-tech enterprise relief), and
  the period carries an **under-provision for prior years of ¥61.0m** (1H2025: ¥27.9m). **Is
  the rate sustainable, and what are the two consecutive prior-year top-ups?**

### Q3 — Receivables up 56.7% on revenue up 8%. Is the company buying price with credit terms?

The most jarring set of numbers in the announcement — and one partly contradicted by another
set, which is why it has to be asked rather than concluded.

| | 30 Jun 2026 | 31 Dec 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables incl. bills (gross) | **¥3.847bn** | ¥2.455bn | **+56.68%** |
| of which: bills receivable | **¥2.894bn** (75.2%) | ¥1.882bn | +53.78% |
| of which: open receivables | ¥953m | ¥574m | **+66.18%** |
| ageing: within 90 days | ¥1.683bn | ¥1.679bn | **+0.23%** |
| ageing: **91–180 days** | **¥2.148bn** | ¥763m | **+181.40%** |
| Bad debt provision | ¥53m | ¥34m | +56.3% |
| Receivables impairment charged in the period | **¥52m** | (1H2025: ¥12m) | **+4.3x** |
| Inventory | ¥1.449bn | ¥1.348bn | +7.46% |
| Contract liabilities (chemicals) | ¥139m | ¥126m | +10.22% |

**Days sales outstanding on annualised revenue went from ~62 days to ~87 days**; the
within-90-day balance barely moved, and **the entire increase landed in the 91–180 day bucket,
nearly tripling**. Receivables impairment charged rose 4.3x.

**The contrary evidence is just as strong: operating cash flow was ¥2.088bn (+38.7%), 1.28x
net profit and 2.03x attributable profit.** The cash did arrive. The reason is on the payables
side: **bills payable rose from ¥327m to ¥1.078bn (+230%)**, with ¥304m of bank deposits and
¥627m of bills receivable pledged against them. **The company is taking paper and issuing
paper — passing it down the chain.** That is routine in Chinese chemicals, but it means
**cash conversion depends on the bill chain not breaking, and bill tenors are stretching from
90 days to 180.**

Two readings, with opposite implications:
1. **Benign.** Downstream air-conditioner makers settle in bills during a price upcycle and the
   company pays its own suppliers in bills; net working capital is contained, as the cash flow
   shows. *Then this is just a change in settlement convention.*
2. **Malign.** To hold prices that have already risen, the company accepted longer terms; the
   tripling of the 91–180 day bucket and the 4.3x rise in impairment are two faces of the same
   thing. *Then part of today's "high price" was bought with credit.*

**Ask:**
- **Within bills receivable, how much is bank-accepted versus commercial-accepted?** How
  concentrated are the drawers of the commercial paper? (This single split separates the two
  readings — bank acceptances are near-cash, commercial acceptances are customer credit.)
- The 91–180 day bucket went from ¥763m to ¥2.148bn — **which segment and which customers?**
- What specifically drove the ¥52m of receivables impairment?
- Top-5 customer concentration over three periods, and whether settlement terms for
  contracted domestic refrigerant customers changed in 2026.

### Q4 — R22 quota goes to near-zero by 2030. So why add 49,000t of R32 capacity now?

One easily skipped line in the announcement carries a lot of weight: **"the R32 refrigerant
expansion project was also completed and commissioned in the first half, adding 49,000 tonnes
per year of R32 capacity."** A "new energy centre" project was also commissioned, to cut
energy consumption.

In an industry where **output is capped by quota**, that is a paradox: quota does not rise, so
the extra capacity cannot run. Three explanations fit, with very different implications:

1. **Waiting for quota expansion or inter-variety switching.** The 2026 inter-variety
   adjustment limit was widened from 10% to 30%, letting the company convert other quota into
   R32. *Then this is rational, low-risk capacity readiness.*
2. **Reserving volume for exports and feedstock use**, neither of which is bound by the
   domestic-use cut. *Then the export price is what matters.*
3. **Preparing a substitute for R22's exit.** R22 is bound by the Montreal Protocol, with
   **production quota cut to 2.5% of baseline by 2030** and a further ~30% of baseline
   production still to be removed between 2026 and 2030. Dongyue's 2026 R22 quota of 43,000t
   is ~27.3% of the gen-2 total, the largest in China. *Then this is a forced asset
   replacement, not an expansion.*

The announcement itself gives a clue: **refrigerant inter-segment sales fell from ¥1.208bn to
¥994m (−17.71%)**, leaving refrigerant **total revenue (including internal) up only 1.79%**
against external sales up 12.06%. Management states plainly that the group "relies on
internally supplied R22 to produce TFE", with the refrigerant segment supplying **R22 and
R142b** to the polymer segment as feedstock for FEP / FKM / PVDF / VDF. **Internal transfers
shrinking while external sales expand is exactly what R22 moving from feedstock use to
(high-priced) controlled use looks like — and it means PTFE's feedstock supply has to be
re-planned.**

**Ask:**
- Of the 43,000t of 2026 R22 quota, **how many tonnes are controlled use (domestic + export)
  and how many feedstock?**
- **How much of refrigerant segment profit is R22?** (The segment made ¥1.246bn this half,
  63.2% of the group total — R22's share of refrigerants is close to R22's share of the whole
  company.)
- For the new 49,000t of R32 capacity: **planned utilisation for 2H2026 and 2027, and against
  which quota?**
- The internal assumption for the 2027–2030 annual R22 controlled-quota path; will cut
  capacity move to feedstock use or idle? **How much does per-tonne margin fall on the switch?**
  (Feedstock R22 is reported to price "near cost".)

### Q5 — A ¥160m goodwill impairment wrote off half the goodwill, and the unnamed associate — is it the hydrogen business?

Two disclosures in this announcement are extremely brief, and both point outside the listco.

**First, a goodwill impairment of ¥159.5m.** Goodwill fell from ¥303m to ¥143m — **52.7%
written off in one period.** Management's only explanation for administrative expenses rising
49.35% is "mainly due to impairment of certain assets", **without saying which cash-generating
unit this goodwill belonged to or why it was impaired.** It is the largest single
non-operating item in the P&L.

**Second, "share of results of an associate" of ¥8.48m and "interests in an associate" of
¥162m.** The group has exactly **one** associate, and the announcement does not name it. On
outside evidence the most likely candidate is **Shandong Dongyue Future Hydrogen Energy
Materials** — the "membrane" and "hydrogen" of the group's four-pillar narrative, whose
technology assets (patents, equipment) were transferred in from Dongyue Fluorosilicone, Huaxia
Shenzhou and Dongyue Polymer, in which **the listco's stake has fallen to ~10% and the actual
controller is Zhang Jianhong personally**; the 2.32% sold in 2023 was priced at ¥165m,
**implying ~¥7.1bn for 100%**; it filed for a STAR listing on 30 September 2025 seeking
¥2.446bn, was picked for an on-site inspection in October, and **withdrew on 30 June 2026 with
the SSE terminating review** ("adjustment of business development plans"); and on 2 January
2026 Dongyue Fluorosilicone renewed framework agreement VI with it through end-2026.

**If that ¥162m carrying value is all of "membrane plus hydrogen", the market is paying close
to nothing for the story.** It contributed ¥8.48m this half, 0.43% of pre-tax profit.

**Ask:**
- **Which acquisition does the ¥159.5m goodwill impairment relate to?** What were the key
  assumptions in the impairment test (discount rate, growth rate)? What does the remaining
  ¥143m relate to?
- **The associate's name and the stake held — is it Dongyue Future Hydrogen Materials?** What
  makes up the ¥162m carrying value?
- Framework agreement VI: FY2025 actual amounts, the FY2026 annual cap, **the pricing method**
  (market / cost-plus / negotiated), the INEDs' opinion and which directors recused.
- The real reason for the STAR withdrawal and the refiling timetable; **if it lists
  independently, how do 00189's shareholders share in the value?** Any anti-dilution,
  pre-emption or compensation arrangement?

## 2. Second-order questions that still move the answer

**Q6 — Dichloromethane and caustic was the only negative contributor this half, and a big
one.** External sales **−26.82%** and segment result **−72.11%** (¥214m → ¥60m), with segment
margin collapsing from 33.58% to 12.80% — a ¥154m drag on the segment increase on its own. The
announcement offers one sentence: these are "bulk chemical products… prices fell
substantially." **But this is not a rounding-error business:** caustic feeds methane chlorides,
which are required inputs for refrigerants and silicones, and in 1H2025 the segment earned
¥214m at a 33.58% margin. Ask: **was 33.58% an unrepeatable outlier, and what is the normal
margin? Is falling chlor-alkali pricing a help or a hindrance to refrigerant input costs**
(inter-segment sales are charged at prevailing market prices)?

**Q7 — Silicones: a 0.38% → 9.02% margin, built on industry-wide production cuts.** Segment
margin went from 0.38% to 9.02% and segment result up almost 26x. The drivers are exogenous and
reversible: a 12 November 2025 industry leaders' meeting agreed a 30% load reduction; cuts ran
at 30% from December 2025 to February 2026 and **40% from June to August 2026, taking
utilisation from ~70% to ~60%**; incumbents may not add capacity in 2025–2027. DMC went from
¥11,300/t to ¥13,500–14,000/t. Two quality details: Dongyue Silicone's 1H2026 **profit
excluding non-recurring items, ¥465–485m, exceeds headline attributable profit of ¥424–444m**
(net non-recurring items of about −¥41m), so **no one-off is propping up the number**; but Q1
revenue of ¥1.256bn was **up only 4.49%**, making **the whole recovery a margin event on flat
volume**. Note also that **a fire in Dongyue Silicone's phase-3 B bed on 20 July 2025 took out
300kt of monomer capacity (half its total) until units restarted at end-November 2025**; with
DMC's annual average price down ~10.95%, that left FY2025 revenue of ¥3.770bn (−26.8%) and an
attributable loss of ¥19.3m, its first since listing. **The insurance recovery amount and the
period it lands in, post-restart unit costs, and safety remediation capex all need chasing.**

**Q8 — Fluoropolymers were this half's most under-noticed bright spot.** Segment margin rose
from 13.36% to **20.49%** and segment result **+69.21%**, a ¥179m increase second only to
silicones. Management attributes it to "rapid development in downstream industries such as AI,
semiconductors and new energy." External data agrees: PTFE bottomed and rebounded in 2026 to
¥52,000/t on 5 June, +23.81% YTD, with domestic CR3 ~57% (Dongyue / Haohua Chenguang / Juhua).
**But two things must be separated:** general-purpose PTFE rising is fluorspar and R22 cost
push, a **cyclical** phenomenon; high-frequency-communications and semiconductor grades
entering the copper-clad laminate supply chain is the **structural** import-substitution
story. **Request PTFE volumes and margins by grade and by end industry, plus the qualification
stage at named customers.** The company says it is now focused on "AI, semiconductor and new
energy tracks"; R&D spend of ¥425m (+14.98%) at 5.27% of revenue, 645 R&D staff of whom 46.67%
hold doctorates or masters, and 478 patents at period end — the input is real, the output mix
needs verifying.

**Q9 — Cash conversion and the balance sheet (this version largely disproves the earlier
worry).** The previous version flagged missing operating cash flow as the biggest gap; it is
now filled: **1H2026 operating cash flow was ¥2.088bn (1H2025: ¥1.505bn), 1.28x net profit and
2.03x attributable profit.** The balance sheet is unusually clean: **net cash ¥6.065bn, total
borrowings ¥1.8m, finance costs ¥641k, current ratio 2.90** (Dec 2025: 3.27). **That is not
what a cyclical chemical company dressing up a peak looks like.** What remains to be filled in
is **FY2024 and FY2025 full-year operating cash flow and capex** (in the annual report, not
this interim announcement), and how much free cash is left after capex +24% plus the >¥1bn of
newly committed investment.

**Q10 — Share supply: the company's own trustee sold HK$328m into the rally.** Shares in issue
did not change (1,732,711,637), **but the wholly-owned subsidiary Hong Kong Taili
International Trading, as trustee, sold 22,266,000 shares on the exchange for ~HK$328m, an
implied ~HK$14.73**; it sold 16,862,000 in 1H2025. These were held under the employee share
option scheme terminated on 27 September 2024, and **the trust was empty at 30 June 2026**.
Two implications: **first, roughly 39m shares of continuous supply over two half-years came
from inside the company's own structure, largely unnoticed by the market; second, that supply
is now exhausted**, which is a marginal positive from here. Also track southbound (~32.5%), E
Fund (over 7%), and any sale or pledge of Zhang Ke's 14.94%. **Note this also explains the EPS
denominator — weighted average shares of 1,718,480,383 (1H2025: 1,657,350,167) sit below
shares in issue precisely because trust-held shares are excluded.**

## 4. Provisional read

**The business.** With the original document in hand, this business is both better and more
lopsided than the previous version judged. **Better:** a 48.51% refrigerant segment margin,
fluoropolymers from 13.36% to 20.49%, silicones from 0.38% to 9.02% — **three of four segments
improving, a breadth beyond the single-refrigerant story**; ¥2.088bn of operating cash flow at
1.28x net profit; ¥6.065bn of net cash against almost no borrowings. **That is not a balance
sheet that blows up at a cycle peak.** **More lopsided:** 37.2% of profit leaks outside and
the share has risen monotonically across four periods; receivables grew 56.7% against 8%
revenue growth with the entire increase in the 91–180 day bucket; R22 — the largest single
profit source — faces a 2030 schedule that takes its quota to near zero, and the company has
just added 49,000t of R32 capacity **without explaining where the quota comes from**; and
"membrane" and "hydrogen", the assets with the most long-term valuation elasticity, are carried
at ¥162m.

**The stock.** 15.6x FY2025, 12.5x 1H annualised, 1.68x book, ~13.5% annualised ROE — and
**stripping the ~¥4.31bn of attributable net cash leaves an enterprise value of ¥21.26bn,
just 10.4x the 1H run-rate.** The reverse valuation says **10x requires ¥2.13bn of normal
attributable profit, roughly holding the first half's level — the price asks for almost no
growth.** That is genuinely cheap. Three things keep it from being a simple value case:
**first**, the ¥3.856bn peak of 2022 fell to ¥708m a year later, so the market's cycle memory
has a basis; **second**, the leakage is accelerating and management has offered no repair
path; **third**, the shares fell over 7% on the 10 July 2026 profit alert, and in a Hong Kong
line where southbound holds ~32.5% with a thin local bid there is no cushion when consensus
turns — **not least because one of the sellers over the past two half-years was the company's
own trustee, at ~HK$14.73**.

**What would flip it.** Against the bull case: the coordination breaking in September and
silicone margin returning toward zero; the interim report showing bills receivable dominated by
commercial acceptances from concentrated customers; the 2027 quota draft accelerating the R22
cut. Against the bear case: an announced buyback of the 23.12% of Dongyue Polymer; a sharply
higher final dividend with capex converging; and interim-report disclosure of high-end PTFE
grade mix and customer qualifications confirming that "AI / semiconductor" is structural rather
than price-driven. **The first of these — buying back the 23.12% — would remove the main reason
for the structural discount at a stroke, and is the single highest-impact event available to
per-share value.**

**Concrete next step:** wait for the **full interim report due to be dispatched before
end-September 2026** and fill in the four tests in checkpoint 1; in parallel obtain the
**FY2025 annual report** to complete FY2024/FY2025 operating cash flow and capex, and the
ownership history tables for Dongyue Polymer and Dongyue Future Hydrogen. **Of the five
questions, Q1 (minorities) and Q3 (receivables) are now quantifiable and trackable; Q4 (R32
capacity versus R22 quota) and Q5 (goodwill and the associate) depend entirely on whether the
company chooses to answer.**
